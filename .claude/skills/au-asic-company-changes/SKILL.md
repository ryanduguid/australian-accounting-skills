# ASIC company changes and annual review preparation

## Inputs

A current ASIC company extract (officeholders, addresses, share structure and members); the constitution, or confirmation that the replaceable rules govern; the member register and minute book; each proposed change with its event date; signed consents to act, resignation letters and occupier consents; whether each incoming director has a director ID (status only); share subscription, issue or transfer documents and any stamp duty evidence; for a share capital reduction, its proposed terms, the shares and holders affected, any amount to be paid or unpaid liability to be reduced, and the directors' record of what they considered and when; the latest annual statement and review date; the information the directors will use to form their solvency opinion; and who the authorised officeholder is.

## Workflow

1. Build a change register. For each change record the event date, the governing provision of the *Corporations Act 2001*, the ASIC form or portal transaction, the notification period, the lodgement fee and any late fee. Take the form, period and fees from ASIC's pages and INFO 30 at use time, with the check date; state none from memory. The provisions are section 205B of the Corporations Act for appointments, changes in personal details and cessations of directors and secretaries; section 142 for the registered office; section 146 for the principal place of business; section 254X for a share issue; sections 256B and 256C for a share capital reduction and section 254Y for a share cancellation; sections 178A to 178C for a proprietary company's member register and share structure changes; and section 346C for correcting the annual statement extract. ASIC directs most of these through Form 484 (change to company details) in the company officeholder portal, and an officeholder can notify their own resignation on Form 370.

2. Check resolution readiness for any appointment or removal. Identify the governing rules: a provision marked as a replaceable rule can be displaced or modified by the constitution, and does not apply whilst one person is both sole director and sole shareholder, when the special provisions such as sections 198E and 201F of the Corporations Act apply instead (section 135). Record who may act: members by resolution (section 201G of the Corporations Act), the directors, with a proprietary company confirming that appointment by resolution within the period section 201H(2) sets, a sole director and sole shareholder by recording and signing the appointment (section 201F(1)), members removing a director of a proprietary company (section 203C) and a director resigning by written notice to the registered office (section 203A). For each incoming director, record the evidence held against each condition in section 201B of the Corporations Act and any gap, leaving eligibility for the authorised officeholder to decide, that a signed consent was given before appointment and is kept (section 201D) and that a director ID is held (section 1272C, with ASIC's guidance to apply before appointment); record ID status, not the number, unless the law in force requires the company to give it to ASIC. Check the quorum (section 248F of the Corporations Act, a replaceable rule), any circulating resolution (section 248A), a one-director resolution (section 248B) or one-member resolution (section 249B), and that minutes are recorded and signed as section 251A requires.

3. Check the minimum officeholders after every change. A proprietary company must have at least one director who ordinarily resides in Australia (section 201A(1) of the Corporations Act); a resignation does not take effect if no director would remain (section 203AB), and a members' resolution removing the last director is void (section 203CA). ASIC says a sole director cannot resign without a replacement. A proprietary company need not have a secretary, but any secretary it has must meet section 204A(1) of the Corporations Act. Section 203AA of the Corporations Act sets when a resignation takes effect by reference to when ASIC is notified; flag any notice that may be late for the authorised officeholder. Route a departing or incoming director's exposure to ATO director penalties to `au-director-penalty-review`.

4. Prepare the address changes. Record the new registered office, whether the company occupies it and, if not, the occupier's written consent (sections 142 and 143 of the Corporations Act), and the date the change takes effect under section 142(3). Record a change of principal place of business separately (section 146 of the Corporations Act).

5. Prepare share changes. For an issue, confirm the power and approval under the constitution or replaceable rules, the number, class and amounts paid and unpaid, and the Corporations Act section 254X notice; for an issue for non-cash consideration under a contract, record the stamp duty certificate section 254X(2) requires and route the duty question to `au-transfer-duty`. For a transfer, record the signed transfer, the member register entries section 169 of the Corporations Act requires and the proprietary company's notice under sections 178A to 178C, including the top 20 rule in section 178B. Route tax consequences to `au-capital-gains` and loans or payments to shareholders to `div7a-compliance`.

5a. Prepare a share capital reduction. **Human review pending:** an agent read Part 2J.1 and sections 249A to 249H and 254Y of the *Corporations Act 2001* (compilation 149, in force from 19 September 2026) and ASIC's reduction in share capital, share buy-back and cancel company shares pages on 16 November 2026, not a human review. First record what authorises the reduction. A buy-back follows Division 2 of Part 2J.1: prepare its steps in `au-share-buy-back` step 2, which takes them from the table in section 257B(1) of the Corporations Act. A redemption of redeemable preference shares (section 254K of the Corporations Act) and the reductions sections 258A to 258F of the Corporations Act authorise are outside this step; record each as a question for the authorised officeholder. Any other reduction follows Division 1 of Part 2J.1. Record any restriction in the constitution (section 125 of the Corporations Act) and whether the terms vary the rights of a class of shares (sections 246B to 246G of the Corporations Act).

   Classify the reduction from its terms. It is an equal reduction only if it relates only to ordinary shares, applies to each holder of ordinary shares in proportion to the ordinary shares they hold and has the same terms for each of them (section 256B(2) of the Corporations Act), ignoring the differences section 256B(3) lists; otherwise it is a selective reduction. Record the evidence for each condition, and keep the classification `UNVERIFIED` while any is missing.

   Section 256B(1) of the Corporations Act requires the reduction to be fair and reasonable to the company's shareholders as a whole and not to materially prejudice the company's ability to pay its creditors; the second condition does not apply to a cancellation of a share for no consideration, and a cancellation of a partly-paid share is taken to be for consideration (section 256B(1A) of the Corporations Act). These are the directors' judgements. For each, record that the directors considered it, which directors, the date and the information they used, and leave the conclusion to them; do not decide either condition or draft a finding on it. Section 256E of the Corporations Act points to section 588G, under which the directors may have to compensate the company if it is or becomes insolvent when it reduces its share capital.

   Record the approval the classification needs. An equal reduction needs a resolution passed at a general meeting (section 256C(1) of the Corporations Act). A selective reduction needs either a special resolution passed at a general meeting with no votes cast in favour by any person who is to receive consideration as part of the reduction or whose liability for unpaid amounts is to be reduced, or by their associates, or a resolution agreed to at a general meeting by all ordinary shareholders; if it cancels shares, it also needs a special resolution passed at a meeting of the shareholders whose shares are to be cancelled (section 256C(2) of the Corporations Act).

   Record how the resolution will be passed. A company with one member passes it by the member recording and signing it (section 249B of the Corporations Act), and a requirement to lodge a document about the resolution is met by lodging it with the resolution passed (section 249B(2) of the Corporations Act); ASIC's reduction page says such a company needs no notice of meeting, no meeting and no lodgement before the resolution is passed. A proprietary company with more than one member may pass it without a meeting if every member entitled to vote signs (section 249A of the Corporations Act), and lodging the document to be signed meets a requirement to lodge a notice of meeting (section 249A(5) of the Corporations Act). For a meeting, at least 21 days' notice is needed, or any longer period the constitution sets (section 249H(1) of the Corporations Act); a shorter period is open only if members holding the share of the votes that section 249H(2)(b) of the Corporations Act sets agree beforehand, so record each agreement and its date.

   Prepare the notice and lodgement dates. The notice of meeting must include a statement of all information known to the company that is material to the vote (section 256C(4) of the Corporations Act), and before the notice is sent the company must lodge with ASIC the notice and any document about the reduction that accompanies it (section 256C(5) of the Corporations Act). ASIC's reduction page names Form 2560 for this lodgement and says a company other than a public listed company lodges it at least 22 days before the meeting; record both rules and diary the earlier date. Where the members agree to shorter notice, record that ASIC's page does not address it, as a question for the authorised officeholder. For a selective reduction only, the company must lodge a copy of the resolution within 14 days after it is passed and must not make the reduction until 14 days after lodgement (section 256C(3) of the Corporations Act); ASIC names Form 2205.

   After the reduction, diary the notice of any share cancellation, due within 1 month after the shares are cancelled (section 254Y(1) of the Corporations Act), and the member register and share structure notices under sections 178A to 178C of the Corporations Act. ASIC's reduction page says to report the changes through the change of company details transaction in the company officeholder portal within 28 days after reducing the share capital; record each period with its source and diary the earlier. Route the shareholders' capital gains records to `au-capital-gains`. Route the reduction's franking screen to `au-franking-account` step 8, which records the proposal and hands it to a specialist; no skill in this pack decides whether any part of a reduction is a dividend for income tax, so record that as a question for the tax adviser. Do not make the reduction, pay any amount, sign, lodge or decide any condition in this step.

6. Prepare the annual review. Compare the extract of particulars (section 346A of the Corporations Act) with the company's records and list each correction needed under section 346C. Draft the solvency resolution for the directors, who must pass one within the period after the review date that section 347A of the Corporations Act sets unless the section 347A(2) exception applies; a negative resolution, or none, must be notified under section 347B, and paying the review fee is taken to be a solvency representation in the circumstances section 347C describes. Assemble the information the directors ask for; the solvency opinion is theirs alone.

   Record the review date, the extract's issue date and the statement's payment due date separately. A required response to the extract is due within 28 days after its issue date under section 346C(3)(a) of the Corporations Act. A compliant response can also satisfy the notice requirement for that change under subsection (4). Record any late lodgement fee already incurred, which subsection (5) preserves. For each fee, record the transaction, company category, applicable fee period and amount from ASIC's fee schedule. If the page shows placeholders, open the rendered ASIC fee table and match the transaction in INFO 30. If the amount or period cannot be verified, mark the fee `UNVERIFIED` and give the authorised officeholder the source and transaction to check.

7. Draft the minutes and resolutions with proposed dates for the directors or members to review, sign and date. Never sign, lodge, pay a fee or backdate; record any request to date a document before it was signed as an exception for the authorised officeholder. Registration of a new company belongs in `au-business-formation`.

## Site calculator

For a fee on the step 1 register or the step 6 annual review, the site's [ASIC fees calculator](https://duguid.com.au/tools/business-calculators/asic-fees/) (published and last checked 22 November 2026, read 22 November 2026) gives ASIC's 2026-27 amount for a company registration, a company name reservation, an annual review fee or a business name registration or renewal, and the late fee tier for a late review fee payment or a late document from the due date and the day ASIC received the payment or document. It runs in the browser and sends no inputs to a server. Before relying on it, confirm it supports the reporting month in question: it takes due and received dates from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 only, and not a fee due in one financial year and received in the next; for any other date, say so and take the amount from ASIC. It does not work out a due date, move one for a weekend or public holiday, or check public holidays, and it declines to give a tier where a weekend could move the result. It does not cover upfront review fees, deregistration and the review fee exceptions, registered schemes, notified foreign passport funds, small transferring financial institutions, CCIVs, registered Australian bodies, foreign companies, credit licensee late fees, fee waivers, reviews of a late fee or the industry funding levy. Use it as a cross-check of the step 1 register only, never as a fallback when a local tool refuses. Enter only the fee type and dates, never the company's name, ACN or officeholders. Record what it consumed: the page, the date run, the inputs and the result. Keep the page's own labels as its own, beside those inputs. Check every amount against ASIC's invoice or fee table before the reviewer relies on it; this skill never pays a fee.

## Hand-off and checks

A change register with provisions, forms, periods and fees as verified at ASIC with the check date, a resolution-readiness checklist, a share capital reduction record with its classification, the directors' recorded considerations, the approval and the lodgement dates, the minimum officeholder check, draft minutes and resolutions, and an exceptions list. Every change ties to a governing rule and a signed document or carries a flag. Appointments, removals, share decisions, whether a reduction is fair and reasonable or materially prejudices the company's ability to pay its creditors, the solvency opinion, signing, lodgement and fee payment are for the directors, the members or the authorised officeholder.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A sole director and sole shareholder wants to resign and hand over to an incoming director who has no director ID and has not signed a consent. Keep the resignation conditional on a valid appointment, list the missing ID and consent, and draft the record for signing only.
