# Family trust and interposed entity election workpaper

## Inputs

The trust deed and every variation, with the history of trustees, appointors or guardians and, for a corporate trustee, its shareholders and directors; the proposed test individual and the family relationships of each person the trust has benefited or may benefit, as relationships and dates rather than identifiers; every conferral of present entitlement and every distribution of income or capital, in the extended meaning, from the beginning of the earliest year that might be specified until now, with trustee resolutions; each company, partnership and trust in the group, with its share, unit or partnership register and the entities it has paid or may pay; any family trust or interposed entity election already made in the group, from the lodged form and the ATO record; the trust's tax losses and debt deductions with their loss years; franked distributions the trust received and the holding facts for them; any company loss that depends on tracing through the trust; any proposed restructure; and the income year in which the election would be made.

## Workflow

1. **Record why the election is proposed.** The ATO's [family trusts page](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/trusts/family-trusts) (last updated 10 November 2026) gives 5 main reasons a trustee may make a family trust election (FTE): the trust loss provisions, a company loss tracing concession, the holding period rules for franking credits, the trustee beneficiary reporting exclusion and the small business restructure roll-over. For each reason the trustee relies on, record the facts and the workpaper that evidences the need: a loss and the tests it fails without an election from `au-trust-losses`, a company loss from `au-company-tax`, and a roll-over from `au-restructure-rollovers`. For franking credits, record each franked distribution the trust received and why its beneficiaries might not be qualified persons, the case s 207-150(1)(a) of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* addresses. Set against them the cost the same page names: family trust distribution tax (FTDT) on conferrals and distributions outside the family group, for this trust and for every entity that makes an interposed entity election (IEE).

2. **Build the family and family group for each candidate test individual.** Under s 272-80(3) of Schedule 2F to the [*Income Tax Assessment Act 1936*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1936A00027/latest/text) the election specifies one individual, and the ATO page says that individual must be alive between the election commencement time and the making of the election, and stays the specified individual after their death. For each candidate, build the family under s 272-95: the individual; any parent, grandparent, brother or sister of the individual or their spouse; any nephew, niece or child of the individual or their spouse; any lineal descendant of those nephews, nieces and children; and the spouse of anyone in the family. Then add the wider family group under s 272-90: former spouses and former stepchildren under s 272-90(2A), the trust itself, trusts whose FTE names the same individual, entities with an IEE in force, entities in which the individual, family members and those family trusts together hold fixed entitlements to all of the income and capital (s 272-90(5)), the funds and bodies in s 272-90(6) to (8) and, once the individual and every family member have died, their estates (s 272-90(9)). Classify every past and expected recipient as inside, outside or `UNRESOLVED` for each candidate, with its evidence. Aunts, uncles and cousins are not in the family under s 272-95; record each such recipient as outside unless another limb of s 272-90 includes them. The trustee chooses the individual.

3. **Evidence the family control test.** The trust must pass the family control test in s 272-87 at the end of the specified income year (s 272-80(4)). Record which paragraphs of s 272-87(2) the group of the individual and family members satisfies (for example, power to obtain beneficial enjoyment, control of how income or capital is applied, a trustee accustomed to act on its directions, power to remove or appoint the trustee, a more than half stake, or being the only persons who can benefit), with the deed clause or register entry for each, noting that s 272-87(1)(b) lets the group include the family's legal or financial advisers for paragraphs (a) to (e), and s 272-87(1)(c) lets the trustees of family trusts whose elections name the same individual count towards paragraph (f). Status: met, not met or `UNVERIFIED`.

4. **Test each candidate specified income year.** The year may be the year the election is made. An earlier year is allowed by s 272-80(4A) only if, from the beginning of that year until 30 June of the income year before the one in which the election is made, the trust passed the family control test at all times and every conferral of present entitlement or distribution of income or capital was made on or to the individual or members of their family group. Schedule the conferrals year by year against that test and record the earliest year that passes. Under s 272-80(10) the election commences at the beginning of the specified year, or, if the test is not passed at all times in that year, from the earliest time it is passed for the rest of the year. Compare that commencement time with what each reason in step 1 needs, such as the beginning of the loss year for a trust loss.

5. **Screen the FTDT that making the election would create.** Under s 271-15 FTDT applies to conferrals and distributions outside the family group at any time while the election is in force, including a time before it was made. Section 271-75(1)(a) makes FTDT on a conferral made before the election due 21 days after the day the election is made, and s 271-80 adds the general interest charge on tax unpaid 60 days after it was due. The s 272-80(4A) test covers only the period up to 30 June of the year before the election, so list every conferral and distribution from that date to the proposed election date separately. Use the extended meaning of distribution in s 272-60, which includes loans, use of property and debts forgiven, to the extent they exceed any consideration given in return. A conferral on a member of the family group is still outside it where that member receives it as trustee of a trust that is not in the family group (s 271-15(1)(b)(ii)), so record the capacity in which each recipient took it. Record each one outside the group or `UNRESOLVED` as a possible FTDT exposure for specialist review, and read the current rate at use time; do not compute or conclude the liability. Liability falls on the trustee and, for a corporate trustee, jointly and severally on its directors (s 271-15(2)). Route the trust's later yearly screen to `au-trust-distributions` step 4a.

6. **Test each proposed interposed entity election.** An IEE can be made only once the FTE is made (s 272-85(1)). A company or partnership passes the family control test only if the individual, family members and family trusts naming that individual have, directly or indirectly and for their own benefit, fixed entitlements to more than half of its income or capital (s 272-87(3)); a trust uses s 272-87(1). Apply the same end-of-year and earlier-year tests (s 272-85(4) and (4A)) and record the commencement time under s 272-85(6B). An entity already in the family group under s 272-90(5) does not need an IEE; record that instead. Under s 272-85(7) an entity cannot make IEEs for more than one trust unless each FTE names the same individual. For each proposed IEE, list every person the entity has paid or may pay, because ss 271-20 to 271-30 impose FTDT on all of its conferrals and distributions outside the family group, not only amounts that came from the family trust; a shareholder outside the family, for example, is an FTDT exposure on every dividend.

7. **Record the limits on undoing the election.** Under s 272-80(5) an FTE generally cannot be varied or revoked. Record each exception with its conditions and window: one variation to a new individual who was a member of the original individual's family at the election commencement time, where every conferral and distribution while the election was in force, by the trust and by each entity with an interposed entity election in relation to it, went to the new individual or their family group (s 272-80(5A) and (5B)); a variation after a family law order, agreement or award gives the new individual's family control (s 272-80(5C)); revocation of a fixed trust's election where, at the beginning of the specified income year, the individual, family members or family trusts naming the individual held fixed entitlements to all of its income and capital and an outsider later holds a fixed entitlement (s 272-80(6)), made in the return for the year of that later time or, if none is required, in the approved form within 2 months after that year or a later day the Commissioner allows (s 272-80(7)); and revocation under s 272-80(6A), which is not available where, since the start of the specified income year, the trust or another entity has used a tax loss or bad debt deduction, or a beneficiary has had a franked distribution, that depended on the election being in force. Variations under s 272-80(5A) and revocations under s 272-80(6A) are confined by s 272-80(6B)(a) to income years up to the end of the fourth income year after the specified income year. Under s 272-80(8) any other variation or revocation is made in the return for the income year from which it is to take effect, or, if no return is required, in the approved form within 2 months after that year or a later day the Commissioner allows. The ATO page says a lodged return cannot be amended to add a revocation or variation schedule. Record the IEE revocation limits in s 272-85(5A) to (6) the same way, and the ATO page's statement that the death of the specified individual does not revoke an election and does not by itself allow its revocation.

8. **Prepare the form pack without lodging it.** Under s 272-80(2) and s 272-85(2) each election must be in writing and in the approved form. Identify the form the Commissioner has approved for the year: the ATO's [Family trust election, revocation or variation 2026 instructions](https://www.ato.gov.au/forms-and-instructions/family-trust-election-revocation-or-variation-2026-instructions) and [Interposed entity election or revocation 2026 instructions](https://www.ato.gov.au/forms-and-instructions/interposed-entity-election-or-revocation-2026-instructions) (both from 30 May 2026) are the current examples, and a later year has its own form. Prepare a data sheet mapping each item the form asks for to its evidence, keeping TFNs and dates of birth only in the firm's approved environment. Record that s 272-80(11) allows only one FTE for a trust, that the trustee must keep written records of the election (the ATO page says generally until 5 years after the trust is no longer required to lodge returns), and that each later return discloses it. Do not make, sign or lodge an election, variation or revocation, or recommend making one. The trustee, company or partners decide and sign, and a registered tax agent lodges.

## Hand-off and checks

A reasons schedule, a family and family group map for each candidate test individual, a family control test schedule, a specified income year test, an FTDT screen covering the period back to the earliest candidate year and up to the election date, an IEE schedule for each proposed interposed entity, a schedule of variation and revocation limits, and a form data sheet. Every recipient is inside, outside or `UNRESOLVED` with evidence. The trustee, and each interposed entity, decides whether to elect and whom to specify; a registered tax agent advises and lodges.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A discretionary trust wants an election specifying an earlier year to use an old loss, but in that year it paid a cousin of the proposed individual, and a company that would need an interposed entity election has an unrelated minority shareholder. Record that the earlier year fails s 272-80(4A), list the cousin's distribution as outside the family group and the company's dividends to the minority shareholder as an FTDT exposure, and leave the choice to the trustee.
