# Franking account and franked distribution workpapers

## Inputs

Income year and private company status, prior reviewed franking account roll-forward, ATO income tax and activity statement account transactions, general ledger tax, instalment and dividend accounts, distribution statements received, dividend resolutions with payment records and the distribution statements issued, the previous income year's aggregated turnover, assessable income and base rate entity passive income workings, any franking account tax return and Commissioner decision, shareholder loan records and share capital account history with any proposed buy-back, capital return or capital reduction. For a proposed dividend, also the constitution and the terms of issue of each class of shares, a balance sheet and cash flow forecast at or near the proposed date, any draft minute, and each shareholder's loan account with any Division 7A loan agreement.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the company is a franking entity for the period: a company acting as trustee of a trust is not one in that capacity, and consolidated and multiple entry consolidated groups have special rules that need specialist review. Verify the franking period at the ATO franking period page; a private company has one franking period matching its income year, and an approved substituted accounting period changes the dates. Open the reviewed closing balance as the opening balance; an unreviewed prior balance stays `UNVERIFIED`.

2. Rebuild credits and debits from source records, independently of the existing franking ledger, using the tables in sections 205-15 and 205-30 of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* for the period. Post income tax and PAYG instalment credits on the day each payment was made, and only for the part the ATO account allocates to that liability; a partial or reallocated payment credits only what reached the instalment or income tax. Post the franking credit on each franked distribution received where the table's conditions are met, credits for any franking deficit tax liability, debits for the franking credit on each distribution franked on the day it is made and debits for each income tax refund on the day received. Evidence each distribution's date from payment records; do not assume it is the resolution date. Locate the special rule before posting a refund that includes a refundable tax offset or any other table item.

3. Reconcile the rebuilt account to the ATO account transactions and the general ledger. Classify every difference: payment allocated to another liability, credit posted for an amount assessed or accrued but unpaid, refund or offset not debited, distribution debited at declaration, missing received credit or arithmetic. Do not plug a difference into the closing balance; leave each one as a reconciling item.

4. Prepare the corporate tax rate for imputation purposes separately from the rate for tax payable in `au-company-tax`. The ATO says to assume the current year's aggregated turnover, assessable income and base rate entity passive income equal the previous income year's, with a separate rule for an entity that did not exist then. Record those prior-year figures with their sources, verify the current rule and rate at the ATO company tax rate page and work out each distribution's maximum franking credit with the gross-up formula on the ATO allocating franking credits page. Without the prior-year evidence, mark the rate, maximum credits and franking percentages `UNVERIFIED` and leave them blank.

5. Schedule every distribution in the period: date made, amount, frankable or unfrankable (check the ATO list, including share capital account distributions and Division 7A deemed dividends), credit allocated, maximum credit and franking percentage. The ATO allocating franking credits page says a distribution cannot be franked above its maximum, and a stated credit above the maximum counts, and is debited to the franking account, only up to the maximum. Work out each percentage from the credit so limited: once the maximum is verified, a later distribution cannot exceed a benchmark set by a distribution franked to its maximum. The first frankable distribution sets the period's benchmark; compare each later one with it and compute any over-franking tax or under-franking debit with the ATO formula as a conditional consequence. Departure from the benchmark needs the Commissioner's issued permission on file. Compare successive periods' benchmarks with the disclosure rule and verify its variation threshold. For a proposed dividend, show the projected year-end balance and the benchmark constraint; the directors choose whether and how much to frank.

5a. **Prepare a proposed dividend workpaper.** Where the directors are considering a dividend, prepare the evidence they need without choosing the amount, the franking or the date.
   - **Authority and dividend rights.** Record whether the constitution or the replaceable rule in s 254U of the [*Corporations Act 2001*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00818/latest/text) gives the directors the power to determine a dividend and fix its amount, time and method of payment, and, for a proprietary company, the terms on which each class of shares is issued (s 254W(2) of the Corporations Act 2001). List each class with the dividend rights the constitution and the terms of issue give it. Record whether the dividend is determined or declared: under s 254V(1) of the Corporations Act 2001 a determined dividend becomes a debt only when the time fixed for payment arrives and can be revoked before then, whilst under s 254V(2) of the Corporations Act 2001 a dividend declared under a constitution that provides for declaration is a debt when declared.
   - **Section 254T of the Corporations Act 2001 conditions.** Assemble the evidence for each condition in s 254T(1) of the Corporations Act 2001: the company's assets exceed its liabilities immediately before the dividend is declared and the excess is sufficient for the payment (calculated under the accounting standards, s 254T(2) of the Corporations Act 2001); the payment is fair and reasonable to the shareholders as a whole; and it does not materially prejudice the company's ability to pay its creditors. Use a balance sheet at or near that date, a cash flow forecast and the creditor position, and mark each condition met, not met or `UNVERIFIED`. The directors decide whether the conditions are met; refer any sign of insolvency to a specialist before the resolution.
   - **Resolution evidence.** Prepare a schedule of what the minute must record: the date, the amount per share for each class, the time and method of payment (cash, a credit to a shareholder's loan account, shares or other assets), and the franking credit or franking percentage the directors allocate. Section 251A of the Corporations Act 2001 requires the minute to be recorded within 1 month; minutes of a meeting to be signed by the chair of that meeting or the next within a reasonable time (s 251A(2) of the Corporations Act 2001); a resolution without a meeting to be signed by a director within a reasonable time (s 251A(3) of the Corporations Act 2001); and, for a proprietary company with 1 director, the director passes the resolution by recording it and signing the record (s 248B(1) of the Corporations Act 2001), and it is minuted under s 251A(1)(d) of the Corporations Act 2001. Section 202-5 of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* leaves the way a franking credit is allocated to the company, and the ATO's issuing distribution statements page says the statement is the evidence of the allocation. Do not draft the resolution as passed or sign anything.
   - **Franking.** Using steps 4 and 5, show for each proposed distribution the maximum franking credit (s 202-60 of the ITAA 1997), the effect of stating more than that maximum (s 202-65 of the ITAA 1997), the benchmark set by the first frankable distribution in the franking period (s 203-30 of the ITAA 1997) and the over-franking tax or under-franking debit that s 203-50 of the ITAA 1997 would bring if the proposed percentage differs (s 203-25 of the ITAA 1997). Show the projected franking account balance at the payment date and at year end after the debit, and any franking deficit tax that would result.
   - **Distribution statements.** Draft each shareholder's statement for review, with the contents s 202-80(3) of the ITAA 1997 and the ATO's list require, and record the proposed issue date against the s 202-75 of the ITAA 1997 issue time; step 6 checks both. A private company's later issue time is not available where the franking credit would create or increase a franking deficit tax liability (s 202-75(4) of the ITAA 1997). Record each shareholder's withholding position from current ATO guidance read at use time, without computing an amount.
   - **Franking account entries.** Add the projected franking debit to the step 2 roll-forward on the date the distribution is made, not the resolution date, and draft the dividend, payment and loan account journals for review without posting them.
   - **Division 7A.** Where the dividend is to be set off against a loan to a shareholder or associate, record the loan, its agreement and the set-off. A set-off of a dividend payable is not excluded from counting as a repayment by s 109R(2) of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1936* (s 109R(3)(a) of the ITAA 1936). Where the loan has already been treated as a dividend, s 109ZC(2) of the ITAA 1936 treats the amount set off as not a dividend, except for franking purposes, only up to the part of the later dividend that is neither franked nor franked with an exempting credit; any amount set off above that part is still a dividend. The ATO's Division 7A franking implications page says the same (the lesser of the unfranked amount and the amount set off) and that the whole later dividend stays subject to the normal franking rules, including the benchmark. Show the excluded amount and the remaining dividend separately. Route the minimum yearly repayment, the timing of the set-off and any deemed dividend to `div7a-compliance`.

   **Human review pending:** an agent read ss 248B, 251A and 254T to 254W of the *Corporations Act 2001* (compilation 149), Divisions 202 and 203 of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* (compilation 268), ss 109R and 109ZC of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1936* (compilation 193) and the ATO pages cited on 11 November 2026. This is not a human review.

6. Check each distribution statement against the ATO list of required contents and the issue time allowed for a private company, verifying both for the period. The extended private company time does not apply where the allocation would create or increase a franking deficit tax liability.

7. If the rebuilt account is in deficit at year end, schedule the franking deficit tax liability, its credit and the offset under the ATO's step method, testing the offset reduction rule and its private company and Commissioner's discretion exclusions with evidence. Verify from the current franking account tax return instructions whether a return is required (deficit tax, over-franking tax or a benchmark variation disclosure) and its due date.

8. Route Division 7A deemed dividends, set-offs and any Commissioner's discretion to frank to `div7a-compliance`, and keep a Division 7A deemed dividend off the franking schedule unless evidence that s 109RB(6) or 109RC(2) of the [*Income Tax Assessment Act 1936*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1936A00027/latest/text) applies is on file; record that evidence for the reviewer to decide whether either applies. Section 109RB(6) of the ITAA 1936 applies where the Commissioner has decided in writing under s 109RB(2)(b) of the ITAA 1936 that the dividend may be franked. Section 109RC(2) of the ITAA 1936 applies to a dividend taken to be paid because of a family law obligation, and s 109RC(3) of the ITAA 1936 lets that dividend be franked only at the private company's benchmark franking percentage for the franking period in which it is taken to be paid or, if the company has no benchmark franking percentage for that period, at a franking percentage of 100%; s 109RC(4) of the ITAA 1936 treats a recipient who is not a member of the company as one for that purpose. Prepare a share buy-back in `au-share-buy-back`, which sets out the dividend and capital split, market value, franking debits and each seller's position; include the debits it schedules in the rebuilt account and check the buy-back's distribution statements against them, for an authorised person to post. Treat a capital return or capital reduction only as a screen: record the proposal, share capital account history and price against market value evidence, then hand it to a specialist. Route franked distributions received by a trust beneficiary to `au-trust-distributions` and any loss carry back offset to `au-company-tax`.

   **Human review pending:** an agent read ss 109RB and 109RC of the ITAA 1936 in compilation C2026C00454 (compilation No. 193, 1 October 2026) on 11 November 2026. This is not a human review.

An optional local check, [`the-exchequer-tally`](https://github.com/ryanduguid/australian-accounting/tree/main/packages/the-exchequer-tally), can cross-check the imputation rate, maximum credit, benchmark and a franking account roll-forward. Its rate tables cover limited years and its entry types do not cover every table item. Pass role labels and placeholder identifiers, never client names or identifiers. Record its version, inputs and outputs, including every refusal or unknown result, and treat them as a review aid. Always complete the steps as written.

## Hand-off and checks

A rebuilt franking account roll-forward reconciled to the ATO account and ledger, imputation rate evidence, a distribution and benchmark schedule, a distribution statement check and, where relevant, a deficit tax and offset schedule. For a proposed dividend, add the s 254T of the Corporations Act 2001 evidence, the resolution schedule, the draft distribution statements and the projected franking account entries and journals. The rebuilt closing balance must equal opening balance plus credits less debits, with every ledger difference itemised. The directors decide dividends and franking; a registered tax agent decides the tax treatment and any lodgement.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Worked example

Fabricated from scratch for an invented entity, in AUD. It shows the shape of the result; no figure here is a rate, threshold or benchmark.

The reviewed opening balance is 12,000.00. The franking credit on the distribution received and the credit allocated to the dividend paid are taken from the distribution statements, not worked out from a rate.

| Roll-forward | Working | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rebuilt account | 12,000.00 + 3,000.00 + 450.00 - 4,500.00 - 600.00 = 10,350.00 (opening, instalments the ATO account allocates to the instalment liability, credit on a distribution received, debit on the dividend paid, income tax refund) | Rebuilt closing balance, conditional on the maximum-credit check |
| Ledger difference | 11,950.00 - 10,350.00 = 1,600.00 (ledger, rebuilt) | Classified, not plugged |
| Itemised | 1,000.00 + 600.00 = 1,600.00 (an instalment payment the ATO account allocated to another liability, a refund not debited) | Both carried as reconciling items |

The 4,500.00 debit sits on the day the payment records show the dividend was paid, not the resolution date. The maximum franking credit and the benchmark need the imputation rate and its prior-year evidence, so they stay blank and `UNVERIFIED` here. Step 5 debits a stated credit only up to the maximum, so the 4,500.00 debit, the closing balance and the ledger difference stay conditional until the maximum is verified. Had the itemised differences come to less than 1,600.00, the rest would stay an unexplained reconciling item for the reviewer. The directors decide dividends and franking.

## Fabricated acceptance example

The ledger closing balance exceeds the rebuilt balance because one instalment payment was allocated to another liability and a refund was never debited, and a proposed dividend would be franked above the period's benchmark. Itemise both differences and show the benchmark breach as a conditional consequence; do not adjust the ledger or choose the franking percentage.
