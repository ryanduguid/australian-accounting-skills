# Property GST review pack

## Inputs

Contract and variations, property use/history, vendor and purchaser capacity, enterprise and GST registration facts, acquisition history, purchase invoices, margin-scheme or going-concern documents, settlement statement, withholding notices, ledger and relevant BAS workpaper. For farm land, also the farming activity on the land for each year before the supply (sales, livestock or crop records, business schedules and any lease to a farming tenant), when and why any routine farming stopped, the recipient's written statement of intended use, any subdivision plan with the parent title, the relationship between supplier and recipient, and a market valuation where the consideration is below market value.

## Workflow

1. Describe the actual property and transaction before coding it: residential history, development activity, leasing, subdivision and changes of use.

2. Verify the supply classification and entity registration position. Keep eligibility for any exemption, margin scheme or going-concern treatment tied to documented conditions.

3. Build separate schedules for the supplier's GST calculation, settlement withholding and settlement cash. One amount must not substitute for another.

4. Reconcile settlement adjustments and creditable acquisitions to the ledger and BAS workpaper. Check GST Act s 75-20 before allowing a purchase credit: a property supplied under the margin scheme is not a creditable acquisition. Review construction and other costs separately under Division 11. Keep an unsupported margin-scheme classification unresolved. Flag contract defects, mixed supplies and missing agreement evidence before any calculation relies on them.

5. Farm land (Subdivision 38-O of the GST Act). **Human review pending:** an agent read ss 9-5, 38-325, 38-475, 38-480, 48-40, 58-10, 58-95, 75-5, 75-11, 105-5, 135-5 and 135-10 of the GST Act, item 13 of Schedule 1 to the *Tax Laws Amendment (2008 Measures No. 5) Act 2008* on the Federal Register of Legislation, the definition of potential residential land in s 195-1 of the GST Act, ss 14-250, 14-255 and 18-60 in Schedule 1 to the *Taxation Administration Act 1953*, the ATO public ruling *Sale of farmland - Section 38-480 of the GST Act* (Primary Production Industry Partnership issues register item 6.2.1(a), consolidated 17 July 2020), GSTD 2011/2 and ATO ID 2001/291 in the ATO legal database, and the ATO's GST at settlement page, on 11 November 2026 (not a human review). A vendor's being a farmer does not make a sale GST-free. Test each path below in its own condition matrix, one row per condition, each marked evidenced, not evidenced or a question for the reviewer:

   - Farm land supplied for farming (s 38-480 of the GST Act). The supply of a freehold interest in land, a long-term lease of it or a lease of it by an Australian government agency is GST-free if a farming business has been carried on, on the land, for at least the period of 5 years preceding the supply, and the recipient intends that a farming business be carried on, on the land. Record the activity against the kinds of farming business in s 38-475(2) of the GST Act: cultivating or propagating plants, fungi or their products or parts; maintaining animals for the purpose of selling them or their bodily produce, including natural increase; manufacturing dairy produce from raw material the entity produced; or planting or tending trees in a plantation or forest that are intended to be felled. GSTD 2011/2 reads the period as a continuous 5 years immediately before the supply, so collect year-by-year evidence of farming on this land up to settlement, including farming by a tenant or other occupier: ATO ID 2001/291, an edited record of an ATO decision rather than a public ruling, says the use of the land and not its ownership matters, regardless of who conducted the farming business, and that the recipient need only intend that a farming business be carried on, not carry it on itself. If routine farming stopped before the sale, record when, why and what the land was used for afterwards: GSTD 2011/2 says ceasing routine activities in the course of terminating or downsizing the farming business can leave the business carried on, but a change of use before the sale, such as renting the land for a non-farming purpose after the business was wound up, can end it. Where part of the land is used for something else, including private use, record evidence against the indicators in *Sale of farmland* for whether farming is the predominant activity (paragraph 7): the area and value used for farming against the whole, business or hobby, size and scale, profit purpose and prospect, business plan, zoning and any rezoning application, home or business finance, accounting treatment, whether the market value points to farming or another use, how the property was advertised and what a visual appraisal shows, with the period each area was farmed (paragraph 8). *Sale of farmland* treats fixtures such as residential premises, fences, shearing sheds, workers' cottages and dams as part of the land (paragraph 11); who owns an agricultural tenant's fixture depends on State or Territory law (paragraph 12), so leave it as a question for the reviewer. Evidence the recipient's intention in writing, such as a contract clause or declaration. Section 38-480 of the GST Act covers the interest in land only: classify livestock, plant, water entitlements and other things in the same contract separately unless the whole supply is a going concern.
   - Subdivided farm land (s 38-475 of the GST Act). The supply of a freehold interest in, a long-term lease of, or a lease by an Australian government agency of, potential residential land is GST-free if the land is subdivided from land on which a farming business has been carried on for at least 5 years and the supply is to an associate of the supplier without consideration or for less than the GST inclusive market value. Collect the subdivision plan and parent title, the farming history of the parent land, the facts that make the recipient an associate, the consideration and a market valuation, and evidence that the lot is potential residential land (land that it is permissible to use for residential purposes but that does not contain any buildings that are residential premises, s 195-1 of the GST Act).
   - Going concern (s 38-325 of the GST Act). A farm sold with everything necessary for the continued operation of the enterprise can also be a supply of a going concern. That path needs consideration, a recipient that is registered or required to be registered, and a written agreement that the supply is of a going concern (s 38-325(1) of the GST Act), and an arrangement under which the supplier supplies all the things necessary for the continued operation of the enterprise and carries on the enterprise until the day of the supply (s 38-325(2)(a) and (b) of the GST Act); s 38-480 of the GST Act has none of those conditions but covers only the land. Where livestock or plant were sold or routine farming stopped before settlement, record the dates against s 38-325(2)(b) of the GST Act. Where the contract relies on one path, still record the other's evidence, and flag a going concern clause where plant, livestock or other operating assets leave the sale.
   - Recipient's adjustments (ss 135-5 and 135-10 of the GST Act). The recipient of a supply of a going concern, or of a supply that is GST-free under s 38-480 of the GST Act, has an increasing adjustment if it intends some supplies made through the enterprise to be neither taxable nor GST-free, such as residential rent (s 135-5 of the GST Act). After acquisition, s 135-10 of the GST Act applies Division 129 to the intended and actual proportions of such supplies, whether or not a s 135-5 of the GST Act adjustment arose (s 135-10(3) of the GST Act), so a later change of use can cause an adjustment. *Sale of farmland* says no adjustment arises while the land keeps the essential characteristics of farmland, or where the land is used to make taxable or GST-free supplies (paragraphs 13 to 15). Record the recipient's intended uses at acquisition and any later change of use with its date; the reviewer decides whether an adjustment arises.
   - Margin scheme. The margin scheme applies only to a taxable supply (s 75-5(1) of the GST Act), so it has no part in a supply that is GST-free under Subdivision 38-O. For the recipient's later sale, record the supplier's registration status when it supplied the land and how it acquired the land (date, consideration, and whether as a taxable supply on which GST was worked out without the margin scheme): s 75-5(2) and (3)(f) of the GST Act bar the margin scheme on the later supply where the entity that supplied the land GST-free under Subdivision 38-O was registered or required to be registered and had acquired the entire interest through such a taxable supply, and where the scheme is available s 75-11(5) of the GST Act can set the margin from that history. For land acquired from an associate, record the same facts for s 75-5(3)(g) of the GST Act and s 75-11(6) and (7) of the GST Act. Test s 75-11(5) of the GST Act first: subsections (6) and (7) apply only where no earlier subsection does, and (7) also covers an acquisition from an associate for consideration. Section 75-5(3)(e) to (g) of the GST Act and s 75-11(5) and (6) of the GST Act apply only where the entity making the later supply acquired the land through a supply made on or after 9 December 2008, and not under a written agreement entered into, or pursuant to a right or option granted, before that day that specifies in writing the consideration or a way of working it out; otherwise Division 75 as in force before that day continues to apply ([*Tax Laws Amendment (2008 Measures No. 5) Act 2008*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2008A00145/asmade/text), Schedule 1, item 13). Record the date of that acquisition and of any such agreement, right or option. Keep these records with the property file.
   - Settlement withholding (Subdivision 14-E in Schedule 1). The recipient's payment under s 14-250 of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953 arises only on a taxable supply within s 14-250(2) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953: new residential premises, other than premises created through substantial renovations or commercial residential premises, or potential residential land that is included in a property subdivision plan and contains no building in use for a commercial purpose, other than a kind of supply the Commissioner excludes by determination under s 14-250(3) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953. For potential residential land it arises only where the recipient is not registered or does not acquire the land for a creditable purpose. A supply that is GST-free under s 38-475 of the GST Act or s 38-480 of the GST Act is not a taxable supply (s 9-5 of the GST Act), so no payment arises while the GST-free conditions are evidenced. If they are not, keep the withholding position open: on a taxable supply to an associate without consideration or for less than the GST inclusive market value, s 14-250(9) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953 sets the amount by reference to the GST exclusive market value and s 14-250(4)(a)(ii) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953 sets when it is paid where there is no consideration; leave the amount blank for the reviewer. Where an amount was withheld, record the evidence for both outcomes without choosing between them. If the supply was a taxable supply within s 14-250(2) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953, the entity liable for GST on it is entitled to a credit equal to the payment, which arises when that entity's net amount is assessed for its tax period in which the payment was made (s 18-60 of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953). Where the supplier is a member of a GST group, that entity is the representative member (GST Act s 48-40(1)), so record the representative member and its ABN. Where a representative of an incapacitated supplier (s 195-1 of the GST Act: a trustee in bankruptcy, liquidator, receiver, controller or administrator, among others) makes the supply within its responsibility or authority, the representative is liable to that extent instead of the supplier or representative member (s 58-10(1), (5) and (6) of the GST Act), except to the extent the supplier received the consideration for the supply before the representative's appointment (s 58-10(2)(a) of the GST Act); record the representative, its capacity, its appointment date and each amount of consideration with the date it was received. Division 58 does not apply to the extent that s 105-5(1)(a) of the GST Act will apply to the representative's supply of the entity's property (s 58-95 of the GST Act), for example a mortgagee in possession supplying it towards a debt the entity owes it (note to s 58-95 of the GST Act). Under s 105-5(1) of the GST Act, a creditor makes a taxable supply if it supplies the property of the entity to a third entity in or towards satisfaction of a debt the entity owes it (s 105-5(1)(a) of the GST Act) and the supply would have been a taxable supply had the entity made it (s 105-5(1)(b) of the GST Act). The supply is not a taxable supply if the entity has given the creditor a written notice stating, with full reasons, that the supply would not be a taxable supply if the entity made it (s 105-5(3)(a) of the GST Act), or if the creditor cannot obtain such a notice and believes on the basis of reasonable information that it would not be (s 105-5(3)(b) of the GST Act). Record the debt, the creditor, the facts that decide whether the supply would have been taxable had the entity made it, and any notice or the information the creditor relied on. If it was withheld in error, for example on a sale that is GST-free, the ATO's GST at settlement page says the supplier can apply for a refund in writing, lodged at least 14 days before the supplier must pay GST on the supply, with the contract, settlement statement and the form and payment references. Separately, s 14-255 of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953 requires the supplier of residential premises or potential residential land by sale or long-term lease to give the recipient a written notice before the supply stating whether a payment is required, except for commercial residential premises, or potential residential land acquired by a registered recipient for a creditable purpose (s 14-255(2) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953). A lot passed to an associate under s 38-475 of the GST Act is potential residential land, so record whether the notice was given and what it states; a farmhouse sold with farm land raises the same question.

   If any condition is not evidenced, mark the classification `UNVERIFIED` and leave the dependent GST and withholding figures blank for the reviewer. Route land tax and duty on farm land to `au-land-tax` and `au-transfer-duty`, and income tax on the farm business to `au-primary-production`.

6. Margin scheme. Where the contract or the supplier says a taxable supply of real property is under the margin scheme, work it as [Margin scheme calculation](#margin-scheme-calculation) sets out before the GST schedule in step 3 relies on it.

## Margin scheme calculation

**Human review pending:** an agent read ss 9-90, 75-5, 75-10, 75-15, 75-20 and 75-35 of the GST Act in the ATO legal database, the *A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Margin Scheme Valuation Requirements Determination 2020* (MSV 2020/1, F2020L00346, shown in force on the Federal Register of Legislation) and the ATO's margin scheme pages on eligibility (last updated 17 June 2025), the written agreement, methods to calculate the margin, calculating the GST payable and valuations (each last updated 19 August 2021), on 19 November 2026 (not a human review). Open each for the supply's date before relying on it.

1. **Eligibility.** Record the evidence for each condition, and leave the margin blank and `UNVERIFIED` while one is unsupported:
   - the supply is a taxable supply of real property made by selling a freehold interest in land or a stratum unit, or by granting or selling a long-term lease (s 75-5(1) of the GST Act); a supply to an associate counts as a sale whether or not it is for consideration (s 75-5(1B) of the GST Act);
   - the written agreement in item 2;
   - how the supplier acquired the interest. The margin scheme does not apply where the supplier acquired the entire interest, unit or lease through a supply that was ineligible for it (s 75-5(2) of the GST Act): a taxable supply on which GST was worked out without the margin scheme (s 75-5(3)(a) of the GST Act), or a chain through an inheritance, a GST group, a GST joint venture, a GST-free going concern, GST-free farm land or an associate without consideration that ends in such a supply (s 75-5(3)(b) to (g) of the GST Act). Collect the supplier's purchase contract and settlement statement and, for those chains, the previous owner's registration status and acquisition history. Each of paragraphs (b) to (g) bars the scheme only when all of its own conditions are met, such as the previous owner being registered or required to be registered at the time of the acquisition for a going concern, farm land or associate chain (s 75-5(3)(e) to (g) of the GST Act): record each condition against its evidence. Where only part of the interest came through an ineligible supply, the note to s 75-5(2) of the GST Act points to an increasing adjustment: flag it for the reviewer. An ordinary purchase was a taxable supply only if its vendor was registered or required to be registered, among the other conditions (s 9-5 of the GST Act); a vendor's lack of registration alone does not establish that it was not required to be registered. A sale by a creditor in satisfaction of a debt can be a taxable supply through the debtor's status instead (s 105-5 of the GST Act): refer that purchase to the reviewer. The chain rules have start dates: the ATO's eligibility page applies the inheritance, GST group and joint venture rules to sales on or after 17 March 2005, and the going concern, farm land and associate rules only to property purchased or contracted for purchase on or after 9 December 2008. Record each acquisition date in the chain and leave its effect to the reviewer.

   Whether the supply is taxable and the margin scheme available are reviewer decisions.

2. **Written agreement.** The supplier and the recipient must have agreed in writing that the margin scheme applies, on or before the making of the supply or within a further period the Commissioner allows (s 75-5(1A) of the GST Act). The ATO's page on the agreement says, for sales on or after 29 June 2005, that it has no set form, must be signed by both seller and purchaser, must clearly identify the property, and can be in the sales contract; the eligibility page says most contracts carry a tick box and that the agreement must exist before settlement. Record the clause or document, both signatures, the property it identifies and its date against the settlement date. Without that evidence, the margin scheme does not apply: mark the classification `UNVERIFIED`, show GST on the full consideration beside the margin figure, and leave any request for a further period to the reviewer. The ATO says it has no discretion to apply the margin scheme where the parties do not agree that it applies. The same page says no written agreement is needed for a sale made before 29 June 2005, or made later under a contract entered into, or rights or options granted, before that date: where the records show such a contract, right or option, flag it for the reviewer rather than treat the scheme as unavailable.

3. **Margin.** The GST on a taxable supply under the margin scheme is 1/11 of the margin (s 75-10(1) of the GST Act).
   - Consideration method: the margin is the amount by which the consideration for the supply exceeds the consideration for the supplier's acquisition of the interest (s 75-10(2) of the GST Act). The ATO's methods page says the sale price includes any settlement adjustments in the sales contract, and the purchase price excludes development costs, legal fees, options purchased, stamp duty and other purchase expenses. It is available whenever the interest was acquired.
   - Valuation method: only where an item of the table in s 75-10(3) of the GST Act applies, chiefly an interest acquired or held before 1 July 2000, the margin is the amount by which the consideration exceeds an approved valuation as at the day that item names: 1 July 2000 under items 1, 2A and 3; for a supplier that became registered or required to be registered only after 1 July 2000, the date of effect of its registration or, if earlier, the day it applied (item 2); or, for the Commonwealth, a State or a Territory under item 4, the day of the supply, valued as if there were no improvements where s 75-10(3A) of the GST Act applies. An approved valuation is one made under the Commissioner's determination (s 75-35 of the GST Act). MSV 2020/1 applies to valuations for supplies on or after 1 March 2010 and allows the supplier 3 methods: a professional valuer's written valuation of market value at the valuation date, made in a manner not contrary to the professional standards recognised in Australia for real property valuations, with a signed certificate giving the property, the valuation date, the date provided, the value, the approach and calculation, and the valuer's name and qualifications (method 1); the consideration under an arm's length contract for the sale of the interest executed or exchanged before the valuation date (method 2); or the most recent State or Territory valuation for rating or land tax purposes made before that date (method 3). Method 2 is not available in the item 4 case where the land has improvements on the day of the supply. Each must be made by the due date for lodging the activity statement for the tax period the GST is attributable to, or, where the Commissioner allowed a further period for the agreement, by the later of 8 weeks after that period ends and 8 weeks after the decision to allow it; the Commissioner may for good reason allow an additional period, a decision for the reviewer. A valuation the Commissioner obtains under method 4, where the supplier did not produce an approved valuation after notice, is also approved and is not bound by those dates: refer it to the reviewer. The ATO's valuations page says that once the margin is worked from an approved valuation held by that date, the supplier cannot change to another valuation, another method or the consideration method. Record the valuation report, its method, valuation date and date made; which method the supplier uses is its choice, recorded for the reviewer.
   - Particular circumstances: for an interest acquired within a GST group or joint venture, by inheritance, from an associate, as a going concern or as GST-free farm land, s 75-11 of the GST Act can set the margin from the earlier history instead. Three more rules change the default figures: consideration still unpaid at the time of the supply, for the supplier's acquisition or, where it acquired the interest from a fellow member of its GST group, for the last supply into the group, is taken out of it (s 75-12 of the GST Act); a supply to an associate takes the GST inclusive market value as its consideration, whether or not it was for consideration (s 75-13 of the GST Act); and for an interest acquired through 2 or more acquisitions each margin provision applies only to the extent the supply is connected to that acquisition (s 75-16 of the GST Act). Record which subsection or section applies and its facts, and leave the margin to the reviewer; the figures below do not cover them.
   - Subdivided land: where the supply is of part of the land acquired, use the corresponding proportion of the acquisition consideration or valuation (s 75-15 of the GST Act). The ATO's calculating page accepts an area basis, and other reasonable methods; record the basis and the lot's share.

4. **GST and reporting.** Work 1/11 of the margin exactly, then round. The note to s 75-10 of the GST Act says s 9-90 of the GST Act can apply, which rounds a fraction of a cent to the nearest cent, 0.5 cents upwards, for a taxable supply that is the only one on its invoice. Show the GST on the full consideration beside it for the reviewer. The ATO's worked examples report the margin at G1 and the GST at 1A for the period the sale occurred; confirm the labels against the current activity statement instructions. The recipient's acquisition is not a creditable acquisition (s 75-20 of the GST Act), so record no input tax credit for it; the supplier's construction and other costs stay under Division 11 (step 4).

**Worked figures (fabricated).** Amounts are in AUD, kept in a block so no figure reads as a current rate or threshold.

```text
Facts
  A  A GST-registered builder bought vacant land on 3 March 2024 for 480,000.00, paid in full, from
     a vendor neither registered nor required to be registered for GST, paying 26,000.00 stamp duty
     and 1,650.00 legal fees. It sells the completed house and land to an unrelated buyer for
     1,100,000.00 plus a 1,320.00 settlement adjustment in its favour for rates paid in advance.
     The contract's margin scheme clause is signed by both parties and identifies the lot.
  B  An entity bought land in May 1998 for 95,000.00, was neither registered nor required to be
     registered on 1 July 2000, and registered for GST with effect from 1 October 2004, having
     applied on 20 September 2004. It sells the land to an unrelated buyer for 990,000.00 under a
     signed margin scheme agreement, holding a professional valuer's approved valuation of 350,000.00
     as at 20 September 2004 (item 2: the application day, earlier than the date of effect), made
     before its activity statement due date.
  C  Sale B worked from the 1998 purchase price instead.

                                   A (consideration)    B (valuation)    C (consideration)
Consideration for the supply           1,101,320.00        990,000.00          990,000.00
Less acquisition or valuation            480,000.00        350,000.00           95,000.00
Margin                                   621,320.00        640,000.00          895,000.00
GST, 1/11 of the margin, exact           56,483.636...     58,181.818...       81,363.636...
GST, rounded to the cent                  56,483.64         58,181.82           81,363.64
GST on the full consideration            100,120.00         90,000.00           90,000.00
```

In A the consideration includes the rates adjustment, and the stamp duty, legal fees and construction are not deducted. The builder's vendor was neither registered nor required to be registered, so the purchase was not a taxable supply (s 9-5 of the GST Act) and s 75-5(3)(a) of the GST Act does not bar the scheme. Had the builder bought the land from a registered developer in a taxable sale with GST worked out without the margin scheme, s 75-5(2) of the GST Act would bar it, and the GST would be 1/11 of the full consideration. For the land held since 1998, the valuation and consideration methods give different margins; the supplier chooses, and once it relies on the approved valuation held by the due date it cannot switch. In each case the purchaser claims no input tax credit for the land (s 75-20 of the GST Act).

Run it by its full path, the extracted `australian-tax-calculators` or `<tool-dir>/bin/australian-tax-calculators` (`.exe` on Windows), never one found on `PATH`, as other builds also report 1.1.0; check that `--version` prints exactly `australian-tax-calculators 1.1.0`, and pass `--refusal-json` after the command so a refusal (exit 2) prints its code. Use this local executable; a remote calculator is used only where the user has explicitly permitted that call for this question, on fabricated or firm-approved inputs, and never as a fallback when a local tool refuses. Send `{}` to its `worksheet-catalogue` command for each worksheet's scope statement and its required and optional inputs. Before running a command, send its worksheet name, the command's name with underscores, to its `periods` command, such as `{"kind": "gst_margin_scheme"}`, and confirm it supports the reporting month or income year in question rather than merely accepting the year; if it does not, say so and follow the written method. Each command reads one JSON object on stdin, without the `kind` field that only `periods` takes, with amounts as decimal strings and a `scope_confirmed` flag: set it only once each condition in the scope statement the command returns is evidenced, and where one is not, follow the written method for that item. Send only amounts, dates and the established facts the command takes, never a client's name, address, TFN, ABN or other identifier. Record what it consumed: the release, the engine version, the exact input, the output with the scope statement, sources and source-check date it returns, and the run date. Record a refusal and its code as an open item, and do not change the facts to get past it. Keep the calculator's own labels as its own, reported beside the facts it was given, never as the reviewer's decision on a position. A calculator's figure checks the written method, which is always completed: where they differ, record the difference for the reviewer and adopt neither. Without the executable, follow the written method.

`gst-margin-scheme` works out, for one taxable supply of real property in a financial year from 2025-26 to 2027-28 made under a contract entered into on or after 9 December 2008, whether the margin scheme can apply and the GST with and without it. It refuses an acquisition from a fellow GST group member, a GST joint venture operator or an associate, by inheritance, as a GST-free going concern or farm land, or in part through an ineligible supply; a supply to an associate; an approved valuation for an interest acquired from 1 July 2000; a supply that valuation items 2 and 4 both describe; and unpaid consideration above the consideration. Whether the supply is taxable, the agreement's terms, the valuation and its compliance with the Commissioner's requirements, apportionment, the time of supply, GST at settlement withholding and the adjustments stay with the workflow above. Release 1.1.0 takes the valuation date as given for the items that turn on a registration or supply date: check that date against the facts before relying on its result.

A person can compare a result with the site's [GST margin scheme calculator](https://duguid.com.au/tools/business-calculators/gst-margin-scheme/) page, which runs in the browser; it is not a substitute for the executable and is not used when the executable refuses.

## Hand-off and checks

A property fact matrix, classification questions, a condition matrix for each farm land or going concern path relied on, and settlement-to-BAS bridge. A conveyancer or tax reviewer decides legal treatment and conducts settlement or reporting. Route whether a subdivision or development profit is revenue or capital to `au-property-development-income`.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Source and review boundary

Before applying a rule, open the relevant primary authority for the work's period and jurisdiction. Record its title, direct URL, provision or paragraph, effective period, check date and the exact fact used. The adjacent `sources.json` records discovery, not current-law approval. A record in it that carries a `reverify_by` date expires after that date: do not use it even as a cross-check, and mark results that depend on it `UNVERIFIED` until the live source has been read. Search snippets and prior-year instructions do not establish the applicable rule. If authority or evidence is unavailable, mark the affected result `UNVERIFIED`, leave dependent calculations blank and identify what the reviewer needs. Never supply rates, thresholds, labels or deadlines from memory. <!-- rule: evidence-boundary -->
