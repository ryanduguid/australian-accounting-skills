# Property GST review pack

## Inputs

Contract and variations, property use/history, vendor and purchaser capacity, enterprise and GST registration facts, acquisition history, purchase invoices, margin-scheme or going-concern documents, settlement statement, withholding notices, ledger and relevant BAS workpaper. For farm land, also the farming activity on the land for each year before the supply (sales, livestock or crop records, business schedules and any lease to a farming tenant), when and why any routine farming stopped, the recipient's written statement of intended use, any subdivision plan with the parent title, the relationship between supplier and recipient, and a market valuation where the consideration is below market value.

## Workflow

1. Describe the actual property and transaction before coding it: residential history, development activity, leasing, subdivision and changes of use.

2. Verify the supply classification and entity registration position. Keep eligibility for any exemption, margin scheme or going-concern treatment tied to documented conditions.

3. Build separate schedules for the supplier's GST calculation, settlement withholding and settlement cash. One amount must not substitute for another.

4. Reconcile settlement adjustments and creditable acquisitions to the ledger and BAS workpaper. Check GST Act s 75-20 before allowing a purchase credit: a property supplied under the margin scheme is not a creditable acquisition. Review construction and other costs separately under Division 11. Keep an unsupported margin-scheme classification unresolved. Flag contract defects, mixed supplies and missing agreement evidence before any calculation relies on them.

5. Farm land (Subdivision 38-O of the GST Act). **Human review pending:** an agent read ss 9-5, 38-325, 38-475, 38-480, 75-5, 75-11 and 135-5 of the GST Act, the definition of potential residential land in s 195-1, ss 14-250 and 14-255 in Schedule 1 to the *Taxation Administration Act 1953*, the ATO public ruling *Sale of farmland - Section 38-480 of the GST Act* (Primary Production Industry Partnership issues register item 6.2.1(a), consolidated 17 July 2020) and GSTD 2011/2 in the ATO legal database on 11 November 2026 (not a human review). A vendor's being a farmer does not make a sale GST-free. Test each path below in its own condition matrix, one row per condition, each marked evidenced, not evidenced or a question for the reviewer:

   - Farm land supplied for farming (s 38-480). The supply of a freehold interest in land, a long-term lease of it or a lease of it by an Australian government agency is GST-free if a farming business has been carried on, on the land, for at least the period of 5 years preceding the supply, and the recipient intends that a farming business be carried on, on the land. Record the activity against the kinds of farming business in s 38-475(2): cultivating or propagating plants, fungi or their products or parts; maintaining animals for the purpose of selling them or their bodily produce, including natural increase; manufacturing dairy produce from raw material the entity produced; or planting or tending trees in a plantation or forest that are intended to be felled. GSTD 2011/2 reads the period as a continuous 5 years immediately before the supply, so collect year-by-year evidence of farming on this land up to settlement, including farming by a tenant or other occupier, because s 38-480(a) looks at the land rather than at who farmed it. If routine farming stopped before the sale, record when, why and what the land was used for afterwards: GSTD 2011/2 says ceasing routine activities in the course of terminating or downsizing the farming business can leave the business carried on, but a change of use before the sale, such as renting the land for a non-farming purpose after the business was wound up, can end it. Where part of the land is used for something else, including private use, prepare the ruling's indicators of whether farming is the predominant activity (paragraph 7): the area and value used for farming against the whole, business or hobby, size and scale, profit purpose and prospect, business plan, zoning and any rezoning application, home or business finance, accounting treatment, whether the market value points to farming or another use, how the property was advertised and what a visual appraisal shows, with the period each area was farmed (paragraph 8). The ruling treats fixtures such as residential premises, fences, shearing sheds, workers' cottages and dams as part of the land (paragraph 11); who owns an agricultural tenant's fixture depends on State or Territory law (paragraph 12), so leave it as a question for the reviewer. Evidence the recipient's intention in writing, such as a contract clause or declaration. Section 38-480 covers the interest in land only: classify livestock, plant, water entitlements and other things in the same contract separately unless the whole supply is a going concern.
   - Subdivided farm land (s 38-475). The supply of a freehold interest in, a long-term lease of, or a lease by an Australian government agency of, potential residential land is GST-free if the land is subdivided from land on which a farming business has been carried on for at least 5 years and the supply is to an associate of the supplier without consideration or for less than the GST inclusive market value. Collect the subdivision plan and parent title, the farming history of the parent land, the facts that make the recipient an associate, the consideration and a market valuation, and evidence that the lot is potential residential land (land that it is permissible to use for residential purposes but that does not contain any buildings that are residential premises, s 195-1).
   - Going concern (s 38-325). A farm sold with everything necessary for the continued operation of the enterprise can also be a supply of a going concern. That path needs consideration, a recipient that is registered or required to be registered, and a written agreement that the supply is of a going concern; s 38-480 has none of those conditions but covers only the land. Where the contract relies on one path, still record the other's evidence, and flag a going concern clause where plant, livestock or other operating assets leave the sale.
   - Recipient's adjustment (s 135-5). The recipient of a GST-free going concern or a supply that is GST-free under s 38-480 has an increasing adjustment if it intends some supplies made through the enterprise to be neither taxable nor GST-free, such as residential rent. The ruling says no adjustment arises while the land keeps the essential characteristics of farmland, or where the land is used to make taxable or GST-free supplies (paragraphs 13 to 15). Record the recipient's intended uses; the reviewer decides whether an adjustment arises.
   - Margin scheme. The margin scheme applies only to a taxable supply (s 75-5(1)), so it has no part in a supply that is GST-free under Subdivision 38-O. For the recipient's later sale, record the supplier's registration status when it supplied the land and how it acquired the land (date, consideration, and whether as a taxable supply on which GST was worked out without the margin scheme): s 75-5(3)(f) makes the later supply ineligible for the margin scheme where the entity that supplied the land GST-free under Subdivision 38-O was registered or required to be registered and had acquired the entire interest through such a taxable supply, and s 75-11(5) otherwise sets the margin from that history. For land passed to an associate without consideration, record the same facts for s 75-5(3)(g) and s 75-11(6) and (7). Keep these records with the property file.
   - Settlement withholding (Subdivision 14-E in Schedule 1). The recipient's payment under s 14-250 arises only on a taxable supply of new residential premises, or of potential residential land that is included in a property subdivision plan and contains no building in use for a commercial purpose. A supply that is GST-free under s 38-475 or s 38-480 is not a taxable supply (s 9-5), so no payment arises while the GST-free conditions are evidenced. If they are not, keep the withholding position open: on a taxable supply to an associate without consideration or for less than the GST inclusive market value, s 14-250(9) sets the amount by reference to the GST exclusive market value and s 14-250(4)(a)(ii) sets when it is paid where there is no consideration; leave the amount blank for the reviewer. Separately, s 14-255 requires the supplier of residential premises or potential residential land by sale or long-term lease to give the recipient a written notice before the supply stating whether a payment is required, unless the land is potential residential land acquired by a registered recipient for a creditable purpose. A lot passed to an associate under s 38-475 is potential residential land, so record whether the notice was given and what it states; a farmhouse sold with farm land raises the same question.

   If any condition is not evidenced, mark the classification `UNVERIFIED` and leave the dependent GST and withholding figures blank for the reviewer. Route land tax and duty on farm land to `au-land-tax` and `au-transfer-duty`, and income tax on the farm business to `au-primary-production`.

## Hand-off and checks

A property fact matrix, classification questions, a condition matrix for each farm land or going concern path relied on, and settlement-to-BAS bridge. A conveyancer or tax reviewer decides legal treatment and conducts settlement or reporting. Route whether a subdivision or development profit is revenue or capital to `au-property-development-income`.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.
