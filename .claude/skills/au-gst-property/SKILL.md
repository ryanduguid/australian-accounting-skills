# Property GST review pack

## Inputs

Contract and variations, property use/history, vendor and purchaser capacity, enterprise and GST registration facts, acquisition history, purchase invoices, margin-scheme or going-concern documents, settlement statement, withholding notices, ledger and relevant BAS workpaper. For farm land, also the farming activity on the land for each year before the supply (sales, livestock or crop records, business schedules and any lease to a farming tenant), when and why any routine farming stopped, the recipient's written statement of intended use, any subdivision plan with the parent title, the relationship between supplier and recipient, and a market valuation where the consideration is below market value.

## Workflow

1. Describe the actual property and transaction before coding it: residential history, development activity, leasing, subdivision and changes of use.

2. Verify the supply classification and entity registration position. Keep eligibility for any exemption, margin scheme or going-concern treatment tied to documented conditions.

3. Build separate schedules for the supplier's GST calculation, settlement withholding and settlement cash. One amount must not substitute for another.

4. Reconcile settlement adjustments and creditable acquisitions to the ledger and BAS workpaper. Check GST Act s 75-20 before allowing a purchase credit: a property supplied under the margin scheme is not a creditable acquisition. Review construction and other costs separately under Division 11. Keep an unsupported margin-scheme classification unresolved. Flag contract defects, mixed supplies and missing agreement evidence before any calculation relies on them.

5. Farm land (Subdivision 38-O of the GST Act). **Human review pending:** an agent read ss 9-5, 38-325, 38-475, 38-480, 48-40, 58-10, 58-95, 75-5, 75-11, 105-5, 135-5 and 135-10 of the GST Act, item 13 of Schedule 1 to the *Tax Laws Amendment (2008 Measures No. 5) Act 2008* on the Federal Register of Legislation, the definition of potential residential land in s 195-1 of the GST Act, ss 14-250, 14-255 and 18-60 in Schedule 1 to the *Taxation Administration Act 1953*, the ATO public ruling *Sale of farmland - Section 38-480 of the GST Act* (Primary Production Industry Partnership issues register item 6.2.1(a), consolidated 17 July 2020), GSTD 2011/2 and ATO ID 2001/291 in the ATO legal database, and the ATO's GST at settlement page, on 11 November 2026 (not a human review). A vendor's being a farmer does not make a sale GST-free. Test each path below in its own condition matrix, one row per condition, each marked evidenced, not evidenced or a question for the reviewer:

   - Farm land supplied for farming (s 38-480 of the GST Act). The supply of a freehold interest in land, a long-term lease of it or a lease of it by an Australian government agency is GST-free if a farming business has been carried on, on the land, for at least the period of 5 years preceding the supply, and the recipient intends that a farming business be carried on, on the land. Record the activity against the kinds of farming business in s 38-475(2) of the GST Act: cultivating or propagating plants, fungi or their products or parts; maintaining animals for the purpose of selling them or their bodily produce, including natural increase; manufacturing dairy produce from raw material the entity produced; or planting or tending trees in a plantation or forest that are intended to be felled. GSTD 2011/2 reads the period as a continuous 5 years immediately before the supply, so collect year-by-year evidence of farming on this land up to settlement, including farming by a tenant or other occupier: ATO ID 2001/291, an edited record of an ATO decision rather than a public ruling, says the use of the land and not its ownership matters, regardless of who conducted the farming business, and that the recipient need only intend that a farming business be carried on, not carry it on itself. If routine farming stopped before the sale, record when, why and what the land was used for afterwards: GSTD 2011/2 says ceasing routine activities in the course of terminating or downsizing the farming business can leave the business carried on, but a change of use before the sale, such as renting the land for a non-farming purpose after the business was wound up, can end it. Where part of the land is used for something else, including private use, record evidence against the indicators in *Sale of farmland* for whether farming is the predominant activity (paragraph 7): the area and value used for farming against the whole, business or hobby, size and scale, profit purpose and prospect, business plan, zoning and any rezoning application, home or business finance, accounting treatment, whether the market value points to farming or another use, how the property was advertised and what a visual appraisal shows, with the period each area was farmed (paragraph 8). *Sale of farmland* treats fixtures such as residential premises, fences, shearing sheds, workers' cottages and dams as part of the land (paragraph 11); who owns an agricultural tenant's fixture depends on State or Territory law (paragraph 12), so leave it as a question for the reviewer. Evidence the recipient's intention in writing, such as a contract clause or declaration. Section 38-480 of the GST Act covers the interest in land only: classify livestock, plant, water entitlements and other things in the same contract separately unless the whole supply is a going concern.
   - Subdivided farm land (s 38-475 of the GST Act). The supply of a freehold interest in, a long-term lease of, or a lease by an Australian government agency of, potential residential land is GST-free if the land is subdivided from land on which a farming business has been carried on for at least 5 years and the supply is to an associate of the supplier without consideration or for less than the GST inclusive market value. Collect the subdivision plan and parent title, the farming history of the parent land, the facts that make the recipient an associate, the consideration and a market valuation, and evidence that the lot is potential residential land (land that it is permissible to use for residential purposes but that does not contain any buildings that are residential premises, s 195-1 of the GST Act).
   - Going concern (s 38-325 of the GST Act). A farm sold with everything necessary for the continued operation of the enterprise can also be a supply of a going concern. That path needs consideration, a recipient that is registered or required to be registered, and a written agreement that the supply is of a going concern (s 38-325(1) of the GST Act), and an arrangement under which the supplier supplies all the things necessary for the continued operation of the enterprise and carries on the enterprise until the day of the supply (s 38-325(2)(a) and (b) of the GST Act); s 38-480 of the GST Act has none of those conditions but covers only the land. Where livestock or plant were sold or routine farming stopped before settlement, record the dates against s 38-325(2)(b) of the GST Act. Where the contract relies on one path, still record the other's evidence, and flag a going concern clause where plant, livestock or other operating assets leave the sale.
   - Recipient's adjustments (ss 135-5 and 135-10 of the GST Act). The recipient of a supply of a going concern, or of a supply that is GST-free under s 38-480 of the GST Act, has an increasing adjustment if it intends some supplies made through the enterprise to be neither taxable nor GST-free, such as residential rent (s 135-5 of the GST Act). After acquisition, s 135-10 of the GST Act applies Division 129 to the intended and actual proportions of such supplies, whether or not a s 135-5 of the GST Act adjustment arose (s 135-10(3) of the GST Act), so a later change of use can cause an adjustment. *Sale of farmland* says no adjustment arises while the land keeps the essential characteristics of farmland, or where the land is used to make taxable or GST-free supplies (paragraphs 13 to 15). Record the recipient's intended uses at acquisition and any later change of use with its date; the reviewer decides whether an adjustment arises.
   - Margin scheme. The margin scheme applies only to a taxable supply (s 75-5(1) of the GST Act), so it has no part in a supply that is GST-free under Subdivision 38-O. For the recipient's later sale, record the supplier's registration status when it supplied the land and how it acquired the land (date, consideration, and whether as a taxable supply on which GST was worked out without the margin scheme): s 75-5(2) and (3)(f) of the GST Act bar the margin scheme on the later supply where the entity that supplied the land GST-free under Subdivision 38-O was registered or required to be registered and had acquired the entire interest through such a taxable supply, and where the scheme is available s 75-11(5) of the GST Act can set the margin from that history. For land acquired from an associate, record the same facts for s 75-5(3)(g) of the GST Act and s 75-11(6) and (7) of the GST Act. Test s 75-11(5) of the GST Act first: subsections (6) and (7) apply only where no earlier subsection does, and (7) also covers an acquisition from an associate for consideration. Section 75-5(3)(e) to (g) of the GST Act and s 75-11(5) and (6) of the GST Act apply only where the entity making the later supply acquired the land through a supply made on or after 9 December 2008, and not under a written agreement entered into, or pursuant to a right or option granted, before that day that specifies in writing the consideration or a way of working it out; otherwise Division 75 as in force before that day continues to apply ([*Tax Laws Amendment (2008 Measures No. 5) Act 2008*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2008A00145/asmade/text), Schedule 1, item 13). Record the date of that acquisition and of any such agreement, right or option. Keep these records with the property file.
   - Settlement withholding (Subdivision 14-E in Schedule 1). The recipient's payment under s 14-250 of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953 arises only on a taxable supply within s 14-250(2) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953: new residential premises, other than premises created through substantial renovations or commercial residential premises, or potential residential land that is included in a property subdivision plan and contains no building in use for a commercial purpose, other than a kind of supply the Commissioner excludes by determination under s 14-250(3) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953. For potential residential land it arises only where the recipient is not registered or does not acquire the land for a creditable purpose. A supply that is GST-free under s 38-475 of the GST Act or s 38-480 of the GST Act is not a taxable supply (s 9-5 of the GST Act), so no payment arises while the GST-free conditions are evidenced. If they are not, keep the withholding position open: on a taxable supply to an associate without consideration or for less than the GST inclusive market value, s 14-250(9) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953 sets the amount by reference to the GST exclusive market value and s 14-250(4)(a)(ii) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953 sets when it is paid where there is no consideration; leave the amount blank for the reviewer. Where an amount was withheld, record the evidence for both outcomes without choosing between them. If the supply was a taxable supply within s 14-250(2) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953, the entity liable for GST on it is entitled to a credit equal to the payment, which arises when that entity's net amount is assessed for its tax period in which the payment was made (s 18-60 of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953). Where the supplier is a member of a GST group, that entity is the representative member (GST Act s 48-40(1)), so record the representative member and its ABN. Where a representative of an incapacitated supplier (s 195-1 of the GST Act: a trustee in bankruptcy, liquidator, receiver, controller or administrator, among others) makes the supply within its responsibility or authority, the representative is liable to that extent instead of the supplier or representative member (s 58-10(1), (5) and (6) of the GST Act), except to the extent the supplier received the consideration for the supply before the representative's appointment (s 58-10(2)(a) of the GST Act); record the representative, its capacity, its appointment date and each amount of consideration with the date it was received. Division 58 does not apply to the extent that s 105-5(1)(a) of the GST Act will apply to the representative's supply of the entity's property (s 58-95 of the GST Act), for example a mortgagee in possession supplying it towards a debt the entity owes it (note to s 58-95 of the GST Act). Under s 105-5(1) of the GST Act, a creditor makes a taxable supply if it supplies the property of the entity to a third entity in or towards satisfaction of a debt the entity owes it (s 105-5(1)(a) of the GST Act) and the supply would have been a taxable supply had the entity made it (s 105-5(1)(b) of the GST Act). The supply is not a taxable supply if the entity has given the creditor a written notice stating, with full reasons, that the supply would not be a taxable supply if the entity made it (s 105-5(3)(a) of the GST Act), or if the creditor cannot obtain such a notice and believes on the basis of reasonable information that it would not be (s 105-5(3)(b) of the GST Act). Record the debt, the creditor, the facts that decide whether the supply would have been taxable had the entity made it, and any notice or the information the creditor relied on. If it was withheld in error, for example on a sale that is GST-free, the ATO's GST at settlement page says the supplier can apply for a refund in writing, lodged at least 14 days before the supplier must pay GST on the supply, with the contract, settlement statement and the form and payment references. Separately, s 14-255 of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953 requires the supplier of residential premises or potential residential land by sale or long-term lease to give the recipient a written notice before the supply stating whether a payment is required, except for commercial residential premises, or potential residential land acquired by a registered recipient for a creditable purpose (s 14-255(2) of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953). A lot passed to an associate under s 38-475 of the GST Act is potential residential land, so record whether the notice was given and what it states; a farmhouse sold with farm land raises the same question.

   If any condition is not evidenced, mark the classification `UNVERIFIED` and leave the dependent GST and withholding figures blank for the reviewer. Route land tax and duty on farm land to `au-land-tax` and `au-transfer-duty`, and income tax on the farm business to `au-primary-production`.

## Hand-off and checks

A property fact matrix, classification questions, a condition matrix for each farm land or going concern path relied on, and settlement-to-BAS bridge. A conveyancer or tax reviewer decides legal treatment and conducts settlement or reporting. Route whether a subdivision or development profit is revenue or capital to `au-property-development-income`.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.
