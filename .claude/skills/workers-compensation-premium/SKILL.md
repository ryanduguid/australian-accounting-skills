# Workers compensation premium

Prepare the wages each state or territory scheme needs from payroll, check the premium notice against them, and reconcile the estimate to actual wages at the end of the policy period. The output is a wages declaration workpaper for each scheme, a premium notice check and an estimate against actual reconciliation, for a person to review and declare. The insurer's notice is the authority for the industry classification, the rate and every claims-based adjustment: this skill states no rate, chooses no classification and decides no one's worker status.

## Inputs

1. Where each worker works and is based, and the employer's policy or registration in each state and territory, with each policy period and its insurer.
2. Payroll for the policy period by worker and pay item: wages and salary, overtime, each allowance and reimbursement by type, bonuses and commissions, each kind of paid leave, termination payments, superannuation contributions including salary sacrificed amounts, other salary sacrifice and directors' fees, with the payroll summary or STP finalisation totals they tie to.
3. Fringe benefits for each worker with their taxable values, from the FBT workpaper (`fbt-annual-workflow`).
4. For each contractor and labour hire arrangement: the payments, with any GST and the labour component shown separately, the contract terms and the facts the scheme's worker test turns on.
5. For each apprentice and trainee: the training contract or agreement, its start date and any earlier employment with the employer.
6. For each director, partner, trustee or family member who works in the business: the role, the remuneration and whether the insurer has been told of them.
7. Each policy's premium or renewal notice and the prior period's: the industry classification, the rate, the estimated wages, the premium and every levy, discount, incentive, GST and duty line, and any grouping notice.
8. The scheme's wages definition document for the policy period, read at use time (see step 3).

## Workflow

1. **Name the scheme for each worker.** Each state and territory runs its own scheme, and an employer with workers in more than one pays the premium in each ([Safe Work Australia, Premiums](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/book/comparison-workers-compensation-arrangements-australia-and-new-zealand-2025-30th-edition/chapter-8-scheme-administrative-and-funding-arrangements/premiums)). Record each worker's scheme with the evidence of where they usually work and are based; for a worker who works in more than one state or territory, or overseas, record the facts against the scheme's guidance on cross-border workers, such as WorkCover Queensland's [Covering interstate and overseas workers](https://www.workcoverqld.com.au/insurance-and-premium/types-of-insurance/who-your-policy-needs-to-cover/covering-interstate-and-overseas-workers), and leave the conclusion to the reviewer. Comcare employers, which are all experience rated, self-insurers and Seacare employers are outside this skill: record them and stop for those workers. Check at use time whether the employer must hold a policy or register in each scheme for the period: the exemption and registration tests differ, and in Victoria an employer with any apprentice or trainee must register whatever its remuneration ([WorkSafe Victoria, Apprentices and trainees](https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/apprentices-and-trainees)).

2. **Record who counts, as facts for the reviewer.** The definition of worker differs by scheme ([Safe Work Australia Table 4.4](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/book/comparison-workers-compensation-arrangements-australia-and-new-zealand-2025-30th-edition/chapter-4-coverage-and-eligibility-benefits/table-44-coverage-workersemployees-contractors-and-labour-hire-workers)): Queensland and the Northern Territory ask whether the person is an employee for PAYG withholding purposes, the other states and the ACT deem some contractors to be workers under their own tests, and a labour hire worker is generally the labour hire firm's worker. Directors differ too: icare counts payments to working directors as wages, WorkCover Queensland's accident insurance does not cover directors, partners, sole traders or trustees, and in the Northern Territory covering a director is optional and possible only where PAYG is withheld from the director's remuneration and the director's details are disclosed to the insurer. For each contractor, director and family member, record the facts against the scheme's test and the regulator's guide that [Table 4.1](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/book/comparison-workers-compensation-arrangements-australia-and-new-zealand-2025-30th-edition/chapter-4-coverage-and-eligibility-benefits/table-41-guidelines-and-information-determining-coverage-workers) names, with the missing fact where one is missing. Worker status is the reviewer's decision, and a payroll tax or superannuation answer for the same person is not this scheme's answer: route those to `payroll-tax-contractors` and `contractor-super-tpar`.

3. **Map each pay item to the scheme's wages definition for the policy period.** Read the scheme's own document at use time: icare's Wages Definition Manual for the period, from its [Declaring wages](https://www.icare.nsw.gov.au/workers-compensation/employers/manage-your-policy/declaring-wages) page; WorkSafe Victoria's [How remuneration works](https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/remuneration-how-it-affects-your-premium); WorkCover Queensland's Wages definition manual, from its [declaring wages](https://www.workcoverqld.com.au/insurance-and-premium/managing-your-policy/renewing-your-accident-insurance-policy-and-declaring-wages) page; the [WorkCover WA Remuneration Guidelines](https://www.workcover.wa.gov.au/workcover-wa-remuneration-guidelines-2nd-edition-2025/), whose second edition applies from 4 pm on 30 June 2025 under the *Workers Compensation and Injury Management Regulations 2024* (WA); ReturnToWorkSA's Remuneration guide, from its [employer remuneration return](https://www.rtwsa.com/insurance/insurance-with-us/employer-remuneration-return) page; WorkSafe Tasmania's [Guideline on the definition of wages](https://worksafe.tas.gov.au/topics/compensation/workers-compensation/information-for-employers/guideline-on-the-definition-of-wages-a-z); WorkSafe ACT's wages and earnings guide, from its [workers compensation](https://www.worksafe.act.gov.au/workers-compensation) page; and in the Northern Territory the insurer's requirements, with NT WorkSafe's [premiums bulletin](https://worksafe.nt.gov.au/forms-and-resources/bulletins/understanding-workers-compensation-premiums-northern-territory). [Safe Work Australia Table 8.6](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/book/comparison-workers-compensation-arrangements-australia-and-new-zealand-2025-30th-edition/chapter-8-scheme-administrative-and-funding-arrangements/table-86-definition-remuneration-purpose-premium-calculation) compares the definitions and names the provision behind each. Build one table for each scheme: pay item, included or excluded, the passage relied on and the date read. The documents differ on items payroll treats alike. The 2026-27 NSW manual includes employer superannuation contributions and lump sum termination payments for unused leave, and excludes the GST component of payments to workers. Apply the Type 2 gross-up rate to the combined taxable values of Type 1 and Type 2 fringe benefits. For charities, churches, public benevolent institutions and rebatable employers, include the portion below the applicable threshold at actual value and gross up the portion above it. [icare Wages Definition Manual 2026-27, read 21 November 2026](https://www.icare.nsw.gov.au/workers-compensation/employers/manage-your-policy/declaring-wages)

   On the pages read on 19 November 2026, WorkCover Queensland includes all superannuation payments, including salary sacrificed amounts, fringe benefits and individual contractors who count as workers (excluding GST), and leaves out work-related expense reimbursements and lump sum termination payments; WorkSafe Victoria sorts each payment type, including termination payments, contractors, apprentices and trainees and cross-border workers, into included or exempt; and WorkSafe Victoria exempts some apprentice and trainee remuneration on conditions, with a cap for trainees that it sets each year; and the WorkCover WA Remuneration Guidelines include superannuation contributions, including salary sacrificed ones, except those made by force of law. Never carry one scheme's treatment to another, and never use payroll tax wages in place of these: route payroll tax to `au-payroll-tax-states`.

4. **Tie each scheme's wages to payroll.** For each scheme, start from the gross pay of its workers for the policy period, add the included amounts outside gross pay (such as superannuation, fringe benefits and deemed workers' payments), take off the excluded items, and reference each line to payroll, the FBT workpaper or the contractor ledger. Use the scheme's rule for which amounts fall in the period; icare, for example, counts amounts paid between the policy start date and the policy expiry date, so use the period on the notice, not the financial year, unless the scheme's period is the financial year. Then tie back the other way: the schemes' wages, less the amounts added from outside gross pay, plus the excluded items and the gross pay of the workers excluded (each with its reason), equal the gross pay in the payroll summary or STP finalisation total for the same period. Show apprentice wages on their own line where the scheme asks for them, as icare does for its apprentice incentive.

5. **Check the premium notice.** Record each line of the notice: classification, rate, wages, premium and each levy, discount, incentive and charge. Confirm the wages on the notice are the wages the declaration gave. Recompute only what the scheme publishes as arithmetic for this employer, and only where the notice shows the employer is on that method: icare's small employer formula, whose average performance premium for a wages-based classification is wages times the classification's rate, while a per capita classification is priced on its own unit and left to the insurer ([Premium calculation for small employers](https://www.icare.nsw.gov.au/workers-compensation/employers/new-policy/premium-calculation-for-small-employers)); WorkSafe Victoria's standard industry rate for an employer at or under the rateable remuneration threshold it publishes; WorkCover Queensland's industry rate for an employer that started employing workers within the period that page sets, as its [How your premium is calculated](https://www.workcoverqld.com.au/insurance-and-premium/understanding-your-premium/how-your-premium-is-calculated) page sets out; ReturnToWorkSA's BP x (1 - D) + C - A + SUR, with the discount factor for the base premium's band and the claims cost capped at 3 x D x BP ([Your premium explained](https://www.rtwsa.com/insurance/insurance-with-us/premium-calculations/your-premium-explained)); and, for a privately underwritten scheme, the insurer's rate on the notice times the wages, as the notice states it. Where the notice shows an experience-rated, claims-based or large employer premium (such as icare's Loss Prevention and Recovery model, WorkCover Queensland's experience-based rating or WorkSafe Victoria's claims performance), record the notice's figures and the wages and claims inputs it states, and do not recompute the insurer's claims component. Record whether the classification's description matches the business's main activity as a question for the employer and insurer; never choose a classification. Where the employer asks whether the rate on the notice is the published rate, open the scheme's published rate table for the period at use time and record its source and date; do not copy the table into the workpaper.

6. **Reconcile the estimate to actual.** The schemes bill on estimated wages and adjust to actual: icare calculates the premium at renewal on estimated wages and again at adjustment on actual wages; WorkCover Queensland's premium is provisional and adjusted at the next renewal; WorkSafe Victoria asks employers to certify actual remuneration by the date on their notice and to update an estimate that falls short of expected remuneration by the margin its page sets; ReturnToWorkSA lets the employer choose each year between the prior year's actual remuneration, with no later adjustment, and an estimate that is adjusted; and in the Northern Territory the insurer recalculates at expiry. For each policy, set the notice's estimated wages beside the actual wages from step 4. Where the notice's method is wages times a rate, the adjustment is the premium on actual less the premium on the estimate, with GST and levies worked as the notice works them; otherwise the insurer recalculates, and the workpaper records only the wages difference. Take the declaration due date and the consequence of not declaring from the notice or the scheme's page at use time; never supply them from memory.

7. **Carry GST, duty and levies separately.** Take each from the insurer's tax invoice or notice: the GST on the premium, any stamp duty (WorkCover Queensland adds both), and any levy or fee, which may carry no GST (ReturnToWorkSA's work health and safety fee does not). The input tax credit for the GST turns on creditable purpose under s 11-15 of the GST Act; route the BAS treatment to `bas-preparation`.

## Hand-off and checks

For each scheme: the policy, the period and the insurer; the worker list with each person's status as a fact or an open question; the wages mapping table with each passage and date read; the wages tie-out to payroll; the premium notice check; and the estimate against actual reconciliation. Before hand-off, check that each worker appears in exactly one scheme or in the excluded list, that the schemes' wages, less the amounts added from outside gross pay and plus the excluded items and workers, tie to payroll, and that no rate, threshold or due date appears without its source and the date read.

This skill does not decide worker status, a cross-border worker's scheme, an industry classification, grouping, an exemption or the experience-rated part of a premium. Those are for the employer, its insurer or the regulator, on the reviewer's advice. A person declares wages and pays the premium.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A fabricated builder has workers in New South Wales and Queensland and one subcontractor paid by invoice with GST. Its payroll export gives one wages total for both states, superannuation in a separate report and a lump sum paid for unused annual leave to a Queensland worker who left. Split the wages by scheme from each worker's location, add the superannuation to each scheme's wages, leave the lump sum out of the Queensland wages as WorkCover Queensland's page directs rather than following icare's treatment, record the subcontractor's facts against each scheme's worker test as an open question with any wages shown excluding GST, and leave both premiums as the notices state them.
