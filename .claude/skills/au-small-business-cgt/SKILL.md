# Small business CGT concession review

## Inputs

Asset/event details and ownership history, business-use periods, group/affiliate facts, turnover and net-asset schedules, entity and stakeholder interests, valuations, prior concession use and proposed elections/payments. For a company or trust using the 15-year exemption, each payment made from the exempt amount with its date, the recipient's position, any interposed entity it passed through, whether the recipient was a CGT concession stakeholder just before the CGT event and their participation percentage.

## Workflow

1. Start with the underlying CGT event and supported gain. Distinguish depreciating-asset treatment and any other regime before testing concessions. For a CGT event on or after 1 July 2027, or an asset held on that date, establish the gain under the *Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026* first: it replaces the CGT discount with cost base indexation for resident individuals, trusts and partnerships on gains accruing from that date. Verify how the concessions apply to that gain from the Act's provisions rather than the pre-reform order.

2. Build the basic-condition evidence matrix under the applicable law, including group connections, relevant turnover or net-asset pathways and active-asset history.

   **Human review pending:** an agent read [s 152-10 of the ITAA 1997](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/152-10), [s 152-35 of the ITAA 1997](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/152-35) and [s 152-40 of the ITAA 1997](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/152-40) in the ATO legal database on 11 November 2026 (not a human review). The matrix covers the CGT event and gain, and one of the pathways in s 152-10(1)(c) of the ITAA 1997: a CGT small business entity, the maximum net asset value test, a partner's interest in an asset of a partnership that is a CGT small business entity, or the passively held asset conditions through an affiliate, a connected entity or a partnership in s 152-10(1A) and (1B) of the ITAA 1997. Add the active asset test in s 152-35 of the ITAA 1997, and for shares and trust interests the extra conditions in s 152-10(2) of the ITAA 1997. For the active asset test, record the periods each asset was used, or held ready for use, in a business carried on by the owner, an affiliate or a connected entity (s 152-40(1) of the ITAA 1997). An asset whose main use is to derive rent cannot be active unless that main use was only temporary (s 152-40(4)(e) of the ITAA 1997), and s 152-40(4A) of the ITAA 1997 treats use by an affiliate or connected entity as the owner's use when deciding main use.

3. Test additional conditions for each proposed concession separately. Record stakeholder, ownership, retirement, replacement-asset and payment facts where the chosen pathway requires them.

   **Human review pending:** an agent read [s 152-80 of the ITAA 1997](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/152-80), [s 152-105 of the ITAA 1997](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/152-105), [s 152-305 of the ITAA 1997](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/152-305), [s 152-325 of the ITAA 1997](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/152-325) and [s 152-410 of the ITAA 1997](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/152-410) in the ATO legal database on 11 November 2026 (not a human review). For an individual's 15-year exemption (s 152-105 of the ITAA 1997), record continuous ownership for the 15 years ending just before the event and either the individual's age at the event and the event's connection with retirement, or permanent incapacity at the event, plus a significant individual for 15 years for shares or trust interests. For the retirement exemption (s 152-305 of the ITAA 1997), record who makes the choice and, for an individual under 55 just before the choice, the contribution to a complying superannuation fund or RSA and its timing; for a company or trust, the significant individual test and the payments to CGT concession stakeholders that s 152-325 of the ITAA 1997 requires, with their amounts and dates. For the small business rollover (s 152-410 of the ITAA 1997), record the choice and any replacement asset or expenditure, noting the CGT events J2, J5 and J6 it can trigger later. Where an asset passed on death, record whether the deceased would have been entitled to reduce or disregard the gain had a CGT event happened just before death (s 152-80(1)(c) of the ITAA 1997), and whether the CGT event happens within 2 years of the death or a longer period the Commissioner allows (s 152-80(1)(d) and (3) of the ITAA 1997). For the legal personal representative, beneficiary, surviving joint tenant or testamentary trustee or beneficiary (s 152-80(2A) of the ITAA 1997), s 152-80(2) of the ITAA 1997 tests s 152-105(d) of the ITAA 1997 only on the deceased's being 55 or over, or permanently incapacitated, at the time of that notional event, and disapplies the superannuation contribution in s 152-305(1)(b) of the ITAA 1997.

   For the small business reduction in Subdivision 152-C, record the income year of the CGT event. **Human review pending:** an agent read [s 152-205 of the ITAA 1997](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/152-205) and its history note in the ATO legal database on 11 November 2026 (not a human review). Section 152-205(2) of the ITAA 1997, inserted by No 49 of 2026 and applying to assessments for the income year that includes 1 July 2027 and later income years, disregards s 152-10(1AA)(b) of the ITAA 1997 in testing the basic conditions for the reduction, so for those years the CGT small business entity limb with the lower turnover figure does not apply to it. Test the reduction's basic conditions separately from the other concessions' for those years, and with the other concessions for earlier years.

   Where a company or trust disregards a capital gain under the 15-year exemption, record the exempt amount apart from its capital gains. **Human review pending:** an agent read on 4 November 2026 of the [*Income Tax Assessment Act 1997*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A05138/latest/text) (compilation 268, in force from 1 October 2026), not a human review. Section 152-110(1) of the ITAA 1997 disregards the gain in the entity itself, so it is left out in working out the entity's net capital gain (s 102-5(1), step 1, note 2). For a trust, Subdivision 115-C of the ITAA 1997 gives a beneficiary an extra capital gain for each capital gain of the trust estate (s 115-215(3)), worked out from the amount of that gain remaining after steps 1 to 6 of the method statement in s 102-5(1) (s 115-225(1)(a)). A gain disregarded under s 152-110(1) of the ITAA 1997 leaves no such amount, so it does not flow to beneficiaries as a capital gain, unlike a gain reduced under Subdivision 152-C, which s 115-215(3)(b) and (c) gross up for the beneficiary. The Act does not say this in terms; it is a reading of those provisions for the reviewer to confirm. Do not show it on a beneficiary statement as a capital gain or as a discount or indexation component. Income the entity derives from the CGT event is neither assessable income nor exempt income (s 152-110(2) of the ITAA 1997), except income from a balancing adjustment event on a depreciating asset under Division 40 or Division 328 (s 152-110(3)). Record each payment made from the exempt amount on its own line: its date and amount, the recipient's position (not name), any interposed entity it passed through, whether the recipient was a CGT concession stakeholder just before the CGT event and that stakeholder's participation percentage. Section 152-125 of the ITAA 1997 disregards such a payment in working out the taxable income of the company or trust, the recipient and any interposed entity only if it is made to an individual who was a CGT concession stakeholder just before the CGT event (s 152-125(1)(c)) and before the later of 2 years after the event and, for a disposal with a look-through earnout right, 6 months after the latest time a financial benefit could become due under that right (s 152-125(1)(b)), unless the Commissioner extends the time (s 152-125(4)). It disregards the payments only up to the limit in s 152-125(2) of the ITAA 1997, which turns on the stakeholder's participation percentage as that subsection defines it. For a company, a payment within that limit is not a dividend or a frankable distribution (s 152-125(3) of the ITAA 1997). A normal business payment, such as wages, is not a payment relating to the exempt amount (note to s 152-125(1) of the ITAA 1997). List as an exception for the adviser each payment made after that time, made to someone who was not a stakeholder or made above the limit, and do not conclude how it is taxed.

4. Prepare alternative schedules only for supported pathways, respecting verified ordering and election requirements. Keep missing valuations and eligibility facts visible; do not optimise by assuming every concession applies.

## Hand-off and checks

A concession-by-condition matrix and conditional calculation schedules. Where a company or trust uses the 15-year exemption, also hand off the exempt amount and its payment schedule from step 3. The adviser decides elections, eligibility and any restructuring or retirement strategy. Route a proposed restructure rollover to `au-restructure-rollovers`.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.
