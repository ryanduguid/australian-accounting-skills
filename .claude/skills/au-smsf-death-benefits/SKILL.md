# SMSF death benefit workpaper

## Inputs

Evidence of the member's death and its date; the trust deed and every variation, with its death benefit, nomination and pension clauses; every death benefit nomination, binding or non-binding, and any reversionary term in the pension documents, each with its signatures, witnesses, dates and any confirmation, amendment or revocation; the will and the grant of probate or letters of administration, or evidence that none has been sought; the trustee structure before and after the death, with trustee minutes since; the deceased's member account at the date of death and at each later valuation, with its tax free and taxable components, any pension's commencement value and components, and any insurance proceeds with the fund's deduction history for insurance premiums or future liability; for each possible recipient, the relationship and dependency evidence (marriage or de facto evidence, a child's age, financial dependency, the facts of any interdependency relationship, any disability); each income stream recipient's existing transfer balance account, from that person; asset realisation records; and the fund's PAYG withholding registration and payment records.

## Workflow

1. **Fix the event and the fund's position.** Record the date of death and its evidence, and the trustee structure the evidence shows after it. The ATO's [death of an SMSF member page](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/paying-smsf-benefits/death-of-an-smsf-member) (last updated 2 April 2025) says the fund has 6 months to restructure after a member's death and that a change in trustee structure is notified to the ATO within 28 days. The law behind it is s 17A of the *Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993*: under s 17A(3)(a) of the SIS Act the member's legal personal representative may be a trustee, or a director of the corporate trustee, in the member's place from the death until death benefits commence to be payable, and under s 17A(4) of the SIS Act a fund that would otherwise cease to be an SMSF does not cease until the earlier of an RSE licensee's appointment and 6 months after it would have ceased. Record when the fund would have ceased to meet s 17A of the SIS Act, whether the legal personal representative took the deceased's place, and the dates and evidence of each step. Where the member asked for a payment before death and it was paid after, list the factors that page gives for deciding whether it is a member benefit or a death benefit, with the evidence for each; the trustee decides.

2. **Read the governing rules and test each nomination.** The ATO's [SMSFD 2008/3](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=SFD/SMSFD20083/NAT/ATO/00001) says s 59 of the *Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993* and reg 6.17A of the *Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994* do not apply to SMSFs (paragraph 1), so the deed sets the form and effect of a binding nomination. The High Court held the same in [*Hill v Zuda Pty Ltd* [2022] HCA 21](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/sites/default/files/eresources/2022/HCA/21.pdf): reg 6.17A of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 has no application to an SMSF ([4], [12] and [30]), whilst s 55A of the SIS Act still applies to an SMSF through reg 6.17 of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 with regs 6.21 and 6.22 of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 ([33]). A deed may still adopt the requirements of reg 6.17A of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 as its own terms. It also says a nomination is not binding to the extent it names a person who cannot receive a benefit under the operating standards (paragraph 2), and that death benefits are paid under the fund's governing rules, not the will (paragraph 5). For each nomination, record the deed clause it relies on, each requirement that clause sets (form, signing, witnessing, delivery, confirmation and any lapse period), the evidence for each and its status of met, not met or `UNVERIFIED`. Where the deed adopts reg 6.17A of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994, test reg 6.17A(6) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 (signed and dated in the presence of 2 adult witnesses who are not named in it, with their signed declaration) and reg 6.17A(7) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 (lapse 3 years after it was signed, last confirmed or amended, or a shorter period the deed fixes). Record a non-binding nomination and a reversionary term as what the evidence shows, without treating either as binding. Whether a nomination is valid and binds the trustee is a legal question for the trustee's lawyer; do not conclude on it.

3. **Schedule who may be paid, under each law separately.** Reg 6.22(2) of the regulations allows a death benefit to be cashed only in favour of the member's legal personal representative or one or more dependants, with reg 6.22(3) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 allowing another individual only where reasonable enquiries found neither. Section 10(1) of the SIS Act defines legal personal representative and gives an inclusive definition of dependant (spouse, any child and any person with whom the person had an interdependency relationship). Tax uses a different test: s 302-195 of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* makes a death benefits dependant the spouse or former spouse, a child aged under 18, a person in an interdependency relationship under s 302-200 of the ITAA 1997 just before the death, or another person who was a dependant just before the death, with s 302-195(2) of the ITAA 1997 extending it for a member who died in the line of duty. Build one table with a row per possible recipient and separate columns for the relationship evidence, SIS dependant status and death benefits dependant status, each met, not met or `UNVERIFIED`. An adult child can be a SIS dependant without being a death benefits dependant; record the facts that s 302-195(1)(c) or (d) of the ITAA 1997 would turn on, including each element of s 302-200(1) or (2) of the ITAA 1997. The reverse also happens: a former spouse is a death benefits dependant under s 302-195(1)(a) of the ITAA 1997 but is not a SIS dependant as a spouse, and SMSFD 2008/3 (paragraph 31) says a nomination in favour of a former spouse need not be followed because paying them would contravene the operating standards; record any other basis on which they might be a SIS dependant as `UNVERIFIED` for the reviewer. Do not decide who is paid or how much; the trustee decides under the deed.

4. **Record the form each recipient can take.** Under reg 6.21(2) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994, a death benefit is cashed as a lump sum (single, or interim and final), the series of lump sums reg 6.21(2)(aa) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 describes, or retirement phase pensions or annuities. Under reg 6.21(2A) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 an income stream is available only to a recipient who was a dependant at the death and, for a child, was under 18, or was financially dependent on the member and under 25, or has a disability reg 6.21(2C) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 describes; reg 6.21(2B) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 requires a child's pension to be cashed as a lump sum on commutation or expiry (unless it is rolled over to commence a new annuity or pension) or the child turning 25, whichever is earlier, unless that disability applies. For a pension that was paying at the death, record whether the deed or pension terms make it reversionary. TR 2013/5 says an income stream ceases on the member's death unless the governing rules or its own terms automatically transfer it to a dependant beneficiary (paragraph 29), and that the rules must specify both the person and that it is paid as an income stream (paragraph 126); where the trustee has a discretion to choose, the stream ceases (paragraph 127). The ATO's [superannuation death benefits page](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/super/withdrawing-and-using-your-super/superannuation-death-benefits) (last updated 10 July 2026) says a death benefit cannot be retained in accumulation phase, so a commuted amount is paid out as a lump sum. Leave the choice of form to the trustee and recipient.

5. **Map the transfer balance cap consequences.** Under item 2 of the table in s 294-25(1) of the ITAA 1997, a credit arises when a recipient starts a retirement phase income stream, equal to the value of the supporting interest on that day, but for a reversionary beneficiary it arises at the end of the 12 months beginning on the starting day. The ATO's [TBAR page](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-administration-and-reporting/when-to-lodge-a-transfer-balance-account-report-for-smsfs) (last updated 2 April 2025) says the death itself is not reported, and that a reversionary credit is reported with the beneficiary's details, the date of death as the effective date and the value of the income stream on that day. Subdivision 294-E modifies the cap for a child recipient; flag it for the reviewer. Record the value, the credit date, the recipient's existing transfer balance account as that person's own evidence and the reporting date, and route the TBAR timeliness test to `au-smsf-year-end` step 3. Show any amount above the recipient's available cap only as a conditional figure from verified inputs; any commutation is the recipient's decision.

6. **Work out the components and elements, and record the recipient's tax position.** Under s 307-125(3) of the ITAA 1997, a lump sum's tax free and taxable components follow the proportions of the interest just before payment, an income stream's follow the proportions when it commenced, and a lump sum from commuting an income stream, such as a commuted reversionary pension, follows the proportions when that income stream commenced (s 307-125(3)(c) of the ITAA 1997). Under s 307-125(4)(a) of the ITAA 1997, s 307-125.02 of the *Income Tax Assessment (1997 Act) Regulations 2021* replaces that rule where a pension paying to the deceased did not automatically revert on the death, nothing other than investment earnings (which include life insurance proceeds and self-insurance amounts) has been added to its interest since the death, and a lump sum is paid, or a new income stream commenced, only from that interest: the benefit, less any part attributable to life insurance proceeds or self-insurance amounts arising on or after the death, takes the tax free proportion the interest had when the original income stream commenced, and the rest is taxable. Record each of those conditions met, not met or `UNVERIFIED`, and the method that follows. Under s 307-290 of the ITAA 1997, where the fund has claimed or will claim a deduction under s 295-465 of the ITAA 1997 or s 295-470 of the ITAA 1997, part of a death benefit lump sum's taxable component is an element untaxed in the fund; record the deduction history, the date of death, the deceased's last retirement day and the service period start that its formula uses, and leave the element blank until each input is evidenced. Record the provision that governs each recipient's benefit, without computing their tax: s 302-60 of the ITAA 1997 for a lump sum to a death benefits dependant, s 302-145 of the ITAA 1997 and its 2 tax offsets for a lump sum to anyone else, ss 302-65 to 302-90 of the ITAA 1997 for an income stream to a dependant (the ages of the deceased and the recipient decide which), and s 302-10 of the ITAA 1997 for a benefit paid to the trustee of the deceased estate, which turns on whether the estate's beneficiaries were death benefits dependants; route the estate side to `au-deceased-estates`.

7. **Test timing and withholding.** Reg 6.21(1) requires the member's benefits to be cashed as soon as practicable after the death, and reg 6.21(3) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994 accepts a roll-over for immediate cashing instead. Build a timeline from the date of death through each step (deed review, nomination determination, enquiries about dependants, probate, asset sales, trustee decision, payment) with each delay and the reason the evidence gives; the law sets no number of days, so do not conclude the payment was late. The ATO's [PAYG withholding page for SMSFs](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/paying-smsf-benefits/payg-withholding-obligations-when-paying-super-benefits) (last updated 2 April 2025) lists when tax is withheld from a death benefit, including a lump sum to a non-dependant, and says no tax is withheld from a lump sum to a dependant beneficiary. For each payment, record whether withholding applies, the TFN declaration status (not the TFN) and the current withholding schedule the reviewer must use, read at use time. That page gives the payment summary deadlines: within 14 days of a lump sum, and by 14 July after the year for an income stream.

8. **List the records each side needs.** For the trustee: the minute of each decision (recipients, amounts, form, the nominations and deed clauses relied on and the eligibility evidence), the nomination test from step 2, the component and element workings, each TBAR, the PAYG payment summaries and withholding records, the restructure and ATO notification evidence, and the asset sale records. The ATO's page says trustees keep records of all decisions. For each recipient: the payment summary showing the tax free component, the taxed and untaxed elements and any amount withheld, and whether the fund treated them as a death benefits dependant; for an income stream, also its commencement value, components, whether it is reversionary and the transfer balance credit date. For the legal personal representative: the amount, components and elements for the estate's return and the s 302-10 of the ITAA 1997 position. Route the annual accounts, member roll-forwards and audit to `au-smsf-year-end` and contribution questions to `au-super-contribution-caps`.

**Human review pending:** an agent read *Hill v Zuda Pty Ltd* [2022] HCA 21, SMSFD 2008/3, TR 2013/5, the ATO pages cited above, the *Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993* (compilation 131), the *Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Regulations 1994* (compilation 159) the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* (compilation 268) and the *Income Tax Assessment (1997 Act) Regulations 2021* (compilation 16) on 11 November 2026. This is not a human review.

## Hand-off and checks

A nomination test, a recipient table with SIS and tax dependant status in separate columns, a form-of-payment schedule, a transfer balance credit schedule, a component and element working, a payment timeline with withholding status, and a records list for the trustee, each recipient and the legal personal representative. The trustee decides who is paid, how much and in what form; the trustee's lawyer decides whether a nomination binds; the recipient decides any commutation; a registered tax agent decides the tax treatment and any lodgement.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A member dies with a pension the deed makes reversionary to the spouse and an accumulation account whose binding nomination names an adult, financially independent child, witnessed once. Test the nomination against the deed, record the child as a SIS dependant and, in the death benefits dependant column, as not meeting s 302-195(1)(b) or (d) of the ITAA 1997 but `UNVERIFIED` under s 302-195(1)(c) of the ITAA 1997 until the facts of any interdependency relationship are evidenced, date the spouse's transfer balance credit 12 months after the death and leave the payment decision with the trustee.
