# Main residence exemption workpaper

## Inputs

Each owner's name in role form and ownership share; each owner's tax residency history to the date of the CGT event; the acquisition contract with its contract and settlement dates, and the disposal contract or other CGT event with its dates; occupation evidence (move-in date, electoral roll, mail, utility connections, where belongings were kept); every period away from the dwelling and what it was used for (rented, available for rent, short-stay or platform letting, business use, vacant, holiday home); lease, agent and platform statements; floor plans with the area used to produce income; any market valuation at the time of first income use; title plans with the land area and the use of each part; building, repair or renovation contracts and completion evidence; for each spouse and each dependent child under 18, the dwelling that was their home in each period and who owned it; every other dwelling an owner held or treated as a main residence; any marriage or relationship breakdown roll-over, deceased estate, destruction or compulsory acquisition documents; and any choice already made or shown in an earlier return.

## Workflow

1. **Confirm who can use the exemption.** Section 118-110(1) of the [*Income Tax Assessment Act 1997*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A05138/latest/text) disregards the gain or loss only for an individual, and not where the interest passed to them as a beneficiary in, or they acquired it as trustee of, a deceased estate. Route a dwelling from a deceased estate to `au-deceased-estates`, which tests ss 118-195 and 118-200, including the 2-year rule; route a dwelling owned by a company or another trust back to `au-capital-gains`. Record which CGT event happened and check it is one s 118-110(2) lists.

   Record each owner's residency at the time of the CGT event with its evidence, and route a residency question to `au-tax-residency`. Under s 118-110(3) the exemption does not apply to an excluded foreign resident, who has been a foreign resident for a continuous period of more than 6 years (s 118-110(4)), or to a foreign resident who does not satisfy the life events test in s 118-110(5); s 118-185(3) excludes the partial exemption on the same terms. The ATO's [foreign residents page](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/foreign-residents-and-capital-gains-tax/main-residence-exemption-for-foreign-residents) says such an owner also cannot use the home first used to produce income rule, and that, under a contract, the disposal time is when the contract is entered into. For a foreign resident, record the continuous period of foreign residency ending at the CGT event, which must be 6 years or less for the life events test (s 118-110(5)(a)), and the evidence for whichever item of s 118-110(5)(b) is claimed:
   - (i) the owner or their spouse had a terminal medical condition that existed at any time during that period of foreign residency;
   - (ii) the owner's child had a terminal medical condition that existed at any time during that period, and was under 18 at at least one such time;
   - (iii) the owner's spouse, or their child who was under 18 at death, died during that period;
   - (iv) the CGT event happened because of a matter in a paragraph of s 126-5(1) involving the owner and their spouse or former spouse.

   Route withholding to `au-nonresident-cgt`.

2. **Fix the ownership period and the dwelling.** The ownership period starts no earlier than 20 September 1985 (s 118-125). Under s 118-130(2) and (3), an ownership interest under a contract runs from legal ownership, or an earlier contractual right to occupy, until legal ownership ends. The ATO's [rental or business use page](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/property-and-capital-gains-tax/your-main-residence-home/using-your-home-for-rental-or-business) counts days owned from the contract purchase and sale dates. Record both date pairs and both day counts, and leave the basis to the reviewer.

   Describe the dwelling under s 118-115. Under s 118-120, adjacent land is covered to the extent it was used primarily for private or domestic purposes in association with the dwelling, up to 2 hectares less the land under the dwelling, and a garage, storeroom or other structure associated with a flat or home unit is covered on the same use test. The ATO's [page on homes on more than 2 hectares](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/property-and-capital-gains-tax/your-main-residence-home/home-on-more-than-2-hectares) says the owner chooses which 2 hectares, the choice must include the land under the dwelling, land used to produce income is not exempt even within 2 hectares, and its example apportions the gain by a valuation of the house and the chosen land against the whole property. Record each part's area and use, any earlier compulsory acquisition that used part of the limit (s 118-120(4)), and the valuation the apportionment needs as evidence to obtain. Under s 118-165 a CGT event that happens to adjacent land or a structure without the dwelling is not covered, except where s 118-245 applies to a compulsory acquisition or similar arrangement under s 118-250, or the dwelling was accidentally destroyed and the land is sold under s 118-160.

3. **Schedule each period of use.** Build one row per period with no gaps or overlaps across the ownership period: the use, the evidence, and the rule that makes it main residence days, if any. For actual occupation, record the factors the ATO's [eligibility page](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/property-and-capital-gains-tax/your-main-residence-home/eligibility-for-main-residence-exemption) lists: the owner and family live there, belongings are there, mail is delivered there, it is the electoral roll address and services are connected, with the length of stay and intention. Then test each extension as a candidate with its conditions, evidence and status of met, not met or `UNVERIFIED`:
   - **Moving in (s 118-135).** The dwelling counts from acquisition if it became the main residence by the time it was first practicable to move in. The ATO's [moving page](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/property-and-capital-gains-tax/your-main-residence-home/moving-to-a-new-main-residence) treats a delay from illness or another unforeseen cause as practicable once the cause is removed, but not a delay because the property was let to a tenant. Record the cause and dates of any delay.
   - **Changing main residences (s 118-140).** Where the owner acquires a dwelling that is to become the main residence whilst still owning the existing one, both count for the shorter of 6 months ending when the old interest ends and the period from acquiring the new interest to that end, only if the old dwelling was the main residence for a continuous period of at least 3 months in the 12 months before its interest ended and was not used to produce assessable income in any part of those 12 months when it was not the main residence.
   - **Absence (s 118-145).** The owner may choose to keep treating a former main residence as their main residence. While it is used to produce assessable income the maximum is 6 years for each absence, starting again each time it again becomes and ceases to be the main residence; otherwise there is no limit. No other dwelling can be the main residence whilst the choice applies, except under s 118-140. The ATO's [former home page](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/property-and-capital-gains-tax/your-main-residence-home/treating-former-home-as-main-residence) says the choice cannot cover a period before the dwelling first became the main residence, the owner can choose when the period stops, and the choice is made when preparing the return for the year of the CGT event. The choice is not available where the dwelling was the main residence because of s 118-147 and ceased to be so because of s 118-147(3) and (4) (s 118-145(3A)). Record each absence, its income-producing days and the other dwellings it would displace.
   - **Building, repairing or renovating (s 118-150).** For land in which the owner has an interest other than a life interest, the owner may choose to treat the dwelling as the main residence from acquisition, only if it becomes the main residence, other than because of s 118-147, as soon as practicable after the work is finished and stays so for at least 3 months. The choice runs for the shorter of 4 years (or longer if the Commissioner allows) before it became the main residence and the period from acquisition; where an existing dwelling was occupied, it starts when that occupation ended; and no other dwelling can be the main residence during it, except under s 118-140. Record contract, completion and move-in dates and any Commissioner's extension. If the owner died during the work, after it was finished but before moving in was practicable, or within the 3 months, route the s 118-155 choice to `au-deceased-estates`.
   - **Destroyed or compulsorily acquired dwelling (ss 118-147 and 118-160).** Record the event, any insurance or compensation, any substitute dwelling or land with its acquisition date, and the choice each section offers, for the reviewer.
   - **Spouse with a different main residence (s 118-170).** Where a spouse, other than one living permanently separately and apart, had a different main residence in a period, the spouses must choose one dwelling for both or nominate different dwellings. If they nominate different dwellings, a dwelling in which the owner's interest was not more than half of the total interests counts for the whole period; otherwise it counts for half the period. Record each spouse's dwelling, ownership share and any nomination. The ATO's [page on living apart](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/property-and-capital-gains-tax/your-main-residence-home/living-separately-to-your-spouse-or-children) gives the meaning of spouse and says an absence choice that leaves the spouses with different homes also brings this rule in.
   - **Dependent child (s 118-175).** Where the owner's child who is under 18 and dependent on the owner for economic support had a different main residence, one dwelling must be chosen for both. Record the child's age and the evidence of dependence.
   - **Relationship breakdown roll-over (ss 118-178 and 118-180).** Where the interest came from a former partner under a Subdivision 126-A roll-over, record the former partner's acquisition date, use and main residence days, which the owner inherits under s 118-178; where it came from a company or trust, record why s 118-180(2) stops it being the main residence for that period.

   Do not make, vary or recommend any choice or nomination. Show each one available, the periods it would cover and the dwelling it would displace; the owner chooses with their adviser.

4. **Record income use and the first-use rule.** Under s 118-190 the exemption is reduced where the dwelling was used to produce assessable income and interest on money borrowed to acquire it would have been deductible (s 118-190(1)(c)). The ATO's rental or business use page says this interest deductibility test applies even where there is no loan, that someone else's use for which the owner receives no assessable income does not reduce it, and that a business area counts only if it is set aside exclusively for the business and not readily adaptable for private use. Record each income use with its floor area, the shared areas and their split, its start and end dates, and the evidence. Under s 118-190(3), income use during an absence choice is ignored only for a part not used that way just before the dwelling last ceased to be the main residence; record the use immediately before each move-out.

   Test s 118-192 separately. It applies where the owner would get only a partial exemption because of income use, that use first happened after 7.30 pm (ACT legal time) on 20 August 1996, and a CGT event just before that first use would have been fully exempt. The owner is then taken to have acquired the dwelling at that time for its market value. The ATO page lists the cases where the rule does not apply, including income use from acquisition, a full exemption from an absence choice, and a foreign resident at the CGT event. Record the first income use date with its evidence, and the valuation for that date: the ATO says a valuation is needed when the dwelling is first used for rental or business. Apply the market valuation standard in `au-capital-gains` and keep the cost base `UNVERIFIED` until the reviewer accepts a valuation; an agent's appraisal is a screen, not evidence of market value.

5. **Assemble the apportionment inputs.** For a dwelling that was the main residence for part only of the ownership period, s 118-185(2) multiplies the gain or loss by the non-main residence days over the days in the ownership period. Where s 118-192 applies, the ATO's former home example counts the ownership period from the first income use. For income use, s 118-190(2) adds an amount that is reasonable having regard to the extent the interest would have been deductible; the ATO page's method multiplies the gain by the floor area used and, where the use stopped before the sale, by its days over the days from first use to sale. Record every input with its evidence and, where every input is supported, compute each result as preliminary and show the arithmetic. Leave a result blank and mark it `UNVERIFIED` where any input is missing, and present any basis other than the ATO's method as a question for the reviewer. Pass the event row, cost base and the main residence day schedule to `au-capital-gains`, which applies the discount, losses and the residential capital gain rules: Schedule 1, item 6 of the [*Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00049/asmade/text) inserts s 102-6, whose post-July 2027 ownership period for a CGT event on or after 1 July 2027 (s 102-6(2)), and whose pre-July 2027 ownership period for a gain treated as made on a deemed sale on 30 June 2027 (s 102-6(4) and (5)), both leave out days for which the gain can reasonably be expected to be disregarded under Subdivision 118-B.

   **Human review pending:** an agent read Subdivision 118-B in compilation C2026C00471 (compilation No. 268, 1 October 2026) and on the ATO legal database, s 102-6 in the as-made text of C2026A00049, and the ATO pages linked here (last updated 22 June 2026), on 11 November 2026. This is not a human review.

## Hand-off and checks

An eligibility and residency record, the ownership period on both date bases, a dwelling and land schedule, a period-by-period use schedule with each extension rule and available choice, an income use schedule with floor areas, the first income use valuation record and the apportionment inputs with any supported preliminary result. The periods must add up to the ownership period with no gap or overlap; no two dwellings count as an owner's main residence on the same day except under s 118-140 or a s 118-170 nomination; the gain or loss must agree with the `au-capital-gains` event row. The owner makes each choice; the reviewer decides whether, and to what extent, the exemption applies.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

An owner lived in a house, moved interstate and let it for 7 years, then sold it after buying and moving into a new home. Schedule the 2 homes' periods, show the absence choice up to its 6-year limit against the new home it would displace, record the first income use date and the missing valuation for it as `UNVERIFIED`, and leave the choice to the owner and the exemption to the reviewer.
