# Client departure records and clearance

## Inputs

The client's instruction to end the engagement or the new accountant's request, with who made it and when; the engagement terms and any variations, including what they say about ownership of records and cloud accounts; a list of records held, by type and location; for each cloud ledger file, the cloud account owner, the users and their access and who pays the subscription; any communication from the new accountant and the client's permission for disclosure, with its scope; unpaid fees and the work they relate to; open engagements and lodgements in progress; and the practice's ATO client list entry and communication preference for the client.

## Workflow

1. **Record the departure.** Record who asked, when and how, the new accountant named in the request, each engagement that ends and any work in progress or lodgement due that the departure affects. List each piece of work as an open item with any due date the ATO record or the engagement states, or `UNVERIFIED`. Whether to finish, hand over or stop each piece of work is a decision for the authorised practitioner.

2. **Classify each record by owner.** For each type of record held, including source documents, working papers, final accounts, tax returns and schedules, advice letters, ATO correspondence and each cloud ledger file, record where it is held and what the engagement terms say about its ownership. Where the terms are silent, record the engagement type, whether the practice acted as the client's agent or as principal and the purpose the record serves. CA ANZ's [N1 guidance note](https://www.charteredaccountantsanz.com/tools-and-resources/practice-management/risk-management-and-quality-control/books-and-records-ownership-possession-and-disclosure-n1) (updated May 2025) determines ownership by reference to those facts and, where the terms are silent, says records created on the client's instructions belong to the client and records prepared solely for the member's own purposes belong to the member; cite it as member guidance for CA ANZ members, not law. [TPB(GS) 07/2011](https://www.tpb.gov.au/tpb-gs-07-2011-claiming-lien-over-client-property) lists source documents, ATO correspondence and notices of assessment, and returns, financial statements and advice letters prepared for the client once paid for, as likely to belong to the client unless otherwise agreed. Record each record's owner as a proposed classification for review, or `UNVERIFIED` where the facts do not support one.

3. **Cloud ledger files.** For each cloud ledger file, record the cloud account owner (the subscriber, account owner or primary admin), every user with their access and who pays the subscription. N1 says client records held in a cloud system must be returned when the engagement ceases, generally by transferring access to the new accountant on the client's request, with no obligation to prepare reports or do substantive work without charge, and that the existing accountant may export the records it may need before transferring access. Record any export the practice proposes to keep and its date. Do not remove a user, change a subscription or transfer the account here.

4. **Reply to the new accountant.** Record the new accountant's request and the client's permission with its scope. [APES 110](https://apesb.org.au/standards-guidance/apes-110-code-of-ethics/) R320.7 requires the reply to comply with the laws and regulations governing the request and to give any information honestly and unambiguously, and 320.7 A1 says whether the client's affairs may be discussed depends on the nature of the engagement, the client's permission and the legal and ethics requirements; AUST 320.2.1 applies Section 320 where the other accountant is not a Member. For an audit or review of financial statements, R320.8(b) requires telling the new accountant that the client failed or refused permission. Code item 6 in s 30-10(6) of the [*Tax Agent Services Act 2009*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2009A00013/latest/text) bars disclosing client information to a third party without the client's permission unless there is a legal duty, the [TPB](https://www.tpb.gov.au/confidentiality-client-information) says it covers former clients, and APES 110 R114.4 continues confidentiality after the relationship ends. Draft the reply for the practitioner to review, including only what the client has permitted; where no permission is held, the draft discloses no client information. Where disclosure without consent is considered, AUST 114.3 A1.1 advises obtaining legal advice first. Do not send the reply.

5. **Unpaid fees and lien questions.** Where fees are unpaid, record the unpaid work, the records it relates to and any proposal to hold a record until payment, and list each proposal as a lien question with these limits as read. N1 says Australian authorities suggest accountants are not entitled to a general lien and that a particular lien needs every circumstance it lists. N1 also says a lien cannot be exercised over a company's statutory books or over the financial records it must keep under section 286 of the *Corporations Act 2001*, and that members should not deny a client access to a payroll or ledger system it needs for its day to day operation. TPB(GS) 07/2011 says documents given only to assist the practitioner, on which the practitioner expended no labour to make them more valuable, generally cannot be the subject of a lien, and encourages legal advice before exercising one. For a CA ANZ member, paragraph 3.9 of [Regulation CR 3](https://www.charteredaccountantsanz.com/member-services/member-obligations/regulations-and-guidance/australian-regulations) (issued 23 May 2025) requires records belonging to the client to be made available for collection promptly after the change in appointment, subject to any legal right of lien. Flag any request from an administrator, liquidator or bankruptcy trustee for legal advice. Whether a lien applies, and what is released, when and to whom, are decisions for the authorised practitioner, who may seek legal advice.

6. **Records the practice keeps.** Record which records the practice keeps and why. Section 30 of the [Tax Agent Services (Code of Professional Conduct) Determination 2024](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2024L00849/latest/text) requires records of the tax agent services provided to each client, including former clients, to be kept and retained for the period it states ([TPB(GS) 52/2024](https://www.tpb.gov.au/tpb-gs-52-2024-obligation-keep-proper-client-records-tax-agent-services-provided)). **Human review pending:** an agent read s 30 in compilation No. 4 (F2026C00953, compilation date 1 October 2026) on 4 November 2026 and found that s 30(2)(b) requires retention for at least 5 years after the service; this is not a human review. Where a record cannot be returned, for example because the former client cannot be found, N1 advises legal advice before destroying it. Do not delete any record here. Route AML/CTF records to `au-aml-ctf-client-coverage`.

7. **ATO client list and address for service.** Record the client's entry on the practice's ATO client list and its communication preference. The ATO's [client list page](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-and-super-professionals/digital-services/online-services-for-agents/client-list) says you must remove your contact details from the client's record before removing the client, should also remove them from the Australian Business Register, and that lodging a final return does not remove a client. Its [client communication page](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-and-super-professionals/digital-services/online-services-for-agents/client-communication) says a Practice preference makes Online services for agents the client's address for service and that changing a preference requires the client's express written authority. List each step for the authorised practitioner with its source and check date. Do not change ATO or Australian Business Register records here.

Route the new accountant's acceptance work to `au-client-acceptance` and the practice's own Code self-review to `au-tax-agent-obligations`.

## Hand-off and checks

A departure register with each open piece of work, a records list giving each record's location, proposed owner and basis or `UNVERIFIED`, the cloud account owner and users of each ledger file, a draft reply limited to the permitted disclosure, each lien question with the limits read, the records the practice keeps and the ATO client list steps. The authorised practitioner decides ownership, any lien, what is released, the reply and every ATO change.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A company client moves to a new accountant whilst 2 invoices are unpaid and the practice is the cloud account owner of its ledger file. List each record with its proposed owner, record the lien question with the section 286 and ledger access limits, and leave the transfer, any lien and the reply for the practitioner.
