# Trust tax loss workpaper

## Inputs

The trust deed and every variation; the history of trustees, appointors or guardians and, for a corporate trustee, its shareholders and directors; unit or other fixed entitlement registers; each year's tax return, loss schedule and trustee resolutions; distribution records for the income year, the period after it and the earlier years the pattern of distributions test reaches; any family trust election or interposed entity election with its specified individual and specified income year and the ATO's record of it; details of any arrangement under which income or a benefit came from outside the trust; and each tax loss and debt deduction with its loss year and amount.

## Workflow

1. **Separate the losses.** List each tax loss by loss year, amount and the income year in which the trust seeks to deduct it, and each debt deduction for a debt incurred in an earlier year. Keep capital losses out of this workpaper: the ATO's [trust loss guidance](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/trusts/trust-income-losses-and-capital-gains/trust-loss-provisions/about-trust-loss-provisions) says the trust loss provisions don't apply to capital losses.

2. **Classify the trust for each test period.** Under s 267-20(1) of Schedule 2F to the [*Income Tax Assessment Act 1936*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1936A00027/latest/text), the test period runs from the beginning of the loss year until the end of the income year. Record, with its evidence, whether the trust was a non-fixed trust (s 272-70) at any time in the test period and whether it was an excepted trust (s 272-100) at all times in it, as Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936 defines each. A family trust is an excepted trust only whilst its election is in force (s 272-75 of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936). Where an election's start falls after the beginning of the loss year, record that the election does not cover the whole test period and apply step 3 for the reviewer. For a fixed trust, Division 266 applies instead and the tests differ by kind of fixed trust, as the ATO's [table of tests](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/trusts/trust-income-losses-and-capital-gains/trust-loss-provisions/which-tests-to-apply) shows; record the kind and the tests it names for the reviewer.

3. **Evidence the non-fixed trust tests.** Where s 267-20 of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936 applies, the trust cannot deduct the loss unless it meets each condition s 267-20(2) lists. Evidence each test separately and give each a status of met, not met or `UNVERIFIED`.
   - **Pattern of distributions test (s 267-30 of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936 and Subdivision 269-D).** The test applies only if the trust distributed income, or capital, both in the income year or within 2 months after its end and in at least one of the 6 earlier income years. Schedule the test year distributions of income and of capital separately by individual (s 269-65 of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936), with each individual's smallest percentage across the test years (s 269-70). Record whether, within 2 months after the end of the income year, the same individuals received for their own benefit a greater than 50% share of each; the individuals for income and for capital may differ (s 269-60 of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936). Record any incomplete distribution through another entity (s 269-75 of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936), any death or relationship breakdown (s 269-80) and any arrangement to pass the test (s 269-85). Under s 267-35 of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936, record any earlier year in which this condition stopped the deduction.
   - **50% stake test (s 267-40 of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936 and Subdivision 269-C).** The test applies if, at any time in the test period, individuals had more than a 50% stake in the income or capital of the trust. Record that threshold group, the time and whether the same individuals kept more than that stake until the end of the test period. The Commissioner's discretion in s 267-40(3) of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936 is for the reviewer; do not assume it is exercised.
   - **Control test (s 267-45 of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936 and Subdivision 269-E).** For the test period, record each change the ATO's [guidance on applying the tests](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/trusts/trust-income-losses-and-capital-gains/trust-loss-provisions/how-to-apply-the-trust-loss-tests) lists: trustees, appointor or guardian, shareholders or directors of a corporate trustee, new beneficiaries, deed amendments, unit holdings and other unusual changes, each with its date and evidence. Whether a group began to control the trust is a question of fact for the reviewer, against the indicators in s 269-95 of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936.
   - **Current year.** Subdivision 267-C can require the trust to work out its net income and tax loss in a special way, and the ATO's table notes say the pattern of distributions test does not apply for current year loss purposes; flag each for the reviewer.

   Where the trust fails only the 50% stake test or the control test, s 267-50 of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936 may allow the part of the loss attributable to part of the loss year; record the facts for each part and leave the amount for review.

4. **Screen for income injection.** Under Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936, Division 270 applies to any trust, fixed or non-fixed, to which a deduction is allowable for the income year (s 270-10(1)(a)), except an excepted trust under s 272-100(b), (c) or (d) (s 270-10(1)(d)): a complying superannuation fund, complying approved deposit fund or pooled superannuation trust; a deceased estate until the end of the income year in which the 5th anniversary of the death occurs; and a fixed unit trust in which exempt entities have fixed entitlements, directly or indirectly and for their own benefit, to all of its income and capital. It still applies to the other 2 kinds of excepted trust in Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936, a family trust (s 272-100(a)) and a designated infrastructure project entity (s 272-100(f)). Record any arrangement under which the trust derived assessable income, an outsider to the trust directly or indirectly provided a benefit to the trustee, a beneficiary or an associate of either, and the trustee, a beneficiary or an associate directly or indirectly provided a benefit to the outsider or to an associate of the outsider, other than an associate covered by s 270-25(1)(a) to (f) of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936 (s 270-10(1)(b)). For a family trust, s 270-25(1) of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936 narrows who is an outsider: the trustee, a person with a fixed entitlement to a share of the trust's income or capital, the individual specified in the family trust election, a member of that individual's family, a trust with the same specified individual, an entity whose interposed entity election was in force when the scheme commenced, and a fixed trust, company or partnership whose income and capital those people or such family trusts were fixedly entitled to throughout the scheme are not outsiders. For any other trust, s 270-25(2) of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936 excludes only the trustee and a person with a fixed entitlement. Under Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936, for each party and each associate who received a benefit, record the paragraph of s 270-25 that excludes it, with its evidence, or that none does; a place in the family group alone does not settle it. Whether the arrangement happened wholly or partly, but not merely incidentally, because the deduction would be allowable is for the reviewer.

5. **Record the family trust election evidence.** Where a family trust election is claimed, record from the lodged election and the ATO's record the specified individual, the specified income year, the family control test evidence at the end of that year (s 272-80(4) of Schedule 2F to the ITAA 1936), for an earlier specified year the conditions in s 272-80(4A), any variation or revocation and each interposed entity election. The ATO's [family trusts page](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/trusts/family-trusts) says only one individual can be specified and that an election is generally in force from the beginning of the specified income year, or from a later time if the family control test is not passed for the whole year. Record where the trustee keeps its written records of each election. Do not make, vary or revoke an election, or recommend doing so; the trustee decides with their adviser. Route the family trust distribution tax screen to `au-trust-distributions` and deed questions to `au-trust-deed-review`.

   **Human review pending:** an agent read Schedule 2F in compilation C2026C00454 (compilation No. 193, start date 1 October 2026), the ATO's trust loss pages (last updated 25 February 2025) and its family trusts page (last updated 20 October 2026) on 4 November 2026. This is not a human review.

6. **Hand the position to the reviewer.** For each loss, present the test period, the trust's classification, each applicable test with its evidence and status, the income injection screen and any election evidence. Leave the deduction amount blank. Whether the trust can deduct each loss, in full or in part, is the reviewer's decision.

## Hand-off and checks

A loss register by loss year, a trust classification for each test period, a separate evidence schedule and status for each applicable test, an income injection screen and a family trust election record. The reviewer decides whether each loss can be deducted; the trustee decides any election.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A discretionary trust with a loss from an earlier year changed its appointor and later made a family trust election whose specified year begins after the loss year. Record that the election does not cover the whole test period, evidence each test separately and leave the deduction for the reviewer.
