# Super contribution cap workpapers

## Inputs

Income year, each fund's contribution report by type (employer, salary sacrifice, personal, spouse and any the fund reports as excluded), evidence of any personal contribution to be claimed as a deduction, total super balance at 30 June of the previous year, unused concessional cap history from ATO online services, date of birth, prior bring-forward triggers and amounts, and any excess contributions determination. For an FHSS release, the ATO's FHSS determination, or each eligible voluntary contribution's fund, date, amount and type with the date a determination was requested, and the release request.

## Workflow

1. Read the concessional and non-concessional caps for the year from the ATO pages. Do not carry a prior year's cap forward.

2. Classify every contribution by type from the fund reports. Treat a personal contribution as concessional only where evidence shows it will be claimed as a deduction and the ATO's conditions for that deduction are met; otherwise mark its type `UNVERIFIED`. Keep any contribution the ATO excludes from the caps out of both tests and show it separately.

   For a proposed personal deduction, retain the fund reference, contribution date and amount, proposed deduction amount, notice-of-intent evidence and fund acknowledgement as separate fields. Reconcile the proposed deduction to the relevant contributions and statement. A notice sent without acknowledgement, conflicting amounts or a missing date remains an evidence question; keep explicit zero and unknown amounts distinct and retain the separate Division 293 review where relevant.

3. Concessional cap: compare concessional contributions with the general cap. If they exceed it, test carry-forward: total super balance below the ATO limit at 30 June of the previous year, unused amounts from up to 5 previous years starting no earlier than 2018-19, applied oldest first. Unused amounts expire after 5 years. Tie the unused amounts to the ATO online services record.

   For 2026-27, list separately each employer contribution for the quarter ending 30 June 2026 that a fund received on or after 1 July 2026, with its receipt date and whether it is super guarantee, required by an industrial instrument or salary sacrificed. Under current law it counts towards the 2026-27 concessional cap, because a contribution counts in the year the fund receives it. Schedule 6 of the *Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 3) Bill 2026* proposes transitional relief that would disregard some of those made in July 2026, but not salary sacrificed contributions, to the extent they would take the individual over the 2026-27 basic concessional contributions cap. As at 4 November 2026 the Bill had passed the House of Representatives but not the Senate, and was not law. Do not apply the relief until it is enacted: check the Bill's stage on its Parliament of Australia page at use time, and show any result that relies on it as a labelled scenario beside the current-law cap test.

4. Excess concessional contributions: compute the excess and note that it is included in assessable income with a tax offset under s 291-15 of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997*, that part of it can be released by a request under s 131-5 of Schedule 1 to the *Taxation Administration Act 1953* within the time that section allows and up to the limit in s 131-10 of Schedule 1 to the TAA 1953 (read the percentage and period there), and that any unreleased excess counts as non-concessional. Prepare the facts for the election; do not lodge it.

5. Non-concessional cap: check the total super balance at 30 June of the previous year against the general transfer balance cap (nil cap at or above it), then the bring-forward table for the year and the age condition. Track any open bring-forward period from earlier years before assuming a fresh cap.

6. Prepare a cap schedule by year showing contributions, cap, carry-forward or bring-forward used, and headroom remaining.

7. **Proposed salary sacrifice.** Where the question is how much more salary could be sacrificed this year, project concessional contributions by the date each fund receives them, because the ATO's employer guidance counts a contribution in the year the fund receives it. In 2026-27 this includes any June 2026 quarter contributions from step 3. Include the employer's super guarantee on the base the period's law requires: the ATO's employer guidance says sacrificed amounts stay in the ordinary time earnings base and do not count towards super guarantee; check the base that applies to paydays from 1 July 2026. Add any carry-forward from step 3 and show the remaining headroom with each assumption. Route the Division 293 effect to `au-division-293`. Record the evidence the ATO lists for an effective arrangement: a signed document covering future earnings only, not salary, wages, entitlements or leave already earned or accrued, and a complying fund. Do not recommend an amount; the ATO says it does not advise on entering a salary sacrifice arrangement, and the decision rests with the client and their financial adviser.

8. **First home super saver release.** Under the FHSS scheme an individual can have eligible voluntary contributions released from super towards a first home: the non-concessional ones and a share of the concessional ones counted, within a limit for each financial year and one in all, plus associated earnings at the shortfall interest charge rate (*Taxation Administration Act 1953* Sch 1 ss 138-25, 138-30, 138-35 and 138-40; the ATO's [About FHSS release amounts](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/super/withdrawing-and-using-your-super/early-access-to-super/first-home-super-saver-scheme/about-fhss-release-amounts), published 8 July 2026 and read 22 November 2026; read the share and limits there for the determination's period). The determination's concessional contributions and associated earnings, less any shortfall of the amounts released below the maximum release amount and not below nil, are assessable income for the income year that corresponds to the financial year of the release request, with a non-refundable tax offset at the rate s 313-25 sets (*Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* ss 313-20 and 313-25, read 22 November 2026). Unless the individual enters a contract to buy or build residential premises in Australia or recontributes the required amount, and notifies the ATO, within the time limits, FHSS tax on the assessable amount applies at the rate the *First Home Super Saver Tax Act 2017* sets (ITAA 1997 s 313-60; the ATO's [FHSS tax assessment](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/super/withdrawing-and-using-your-super/early-access-to-super/first-home-super-saver-scheme/fhss-tax-assessment) page, published 8 July 2026 and read 22 November 2026). Those contract, recontribution and FHSS tax rules (Subdivisions 313-C to 313-E) do not apply in circumstances relating to, or resulting from, directly or indirectly, a transitional action, and only where every condition of the transitional rule holds: a determination was made from 1 July 2018 to 14 September 2024; after it, the individual began holding an interest in real property or land of a kind TAA 1953 Sch 1 s 138-10(2)(a) mentions; from 15 September 2024 to 14 September 2027 the individual applied to amend that determination, amended the valid release request or applied to vary a release authority for it; and when that action was taken the Commissioner had not begun treating any FHSS credit (item 30(1) and (4) of Schedule 4 to the [*Treasury Laws Amendment (2023 Measures No. 3) Act 2023*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2023A00075/asmade/text), read 22 November 2026). A later release unrelated to such an action keeps those rules.

   Work out the release with the `fhss` command (Tax calculators below), from the ATO's determination where there is one. The Commissioner's determination governs: a determination worked out from contributions is an estimate, and its associated earnings are labelled `estimate` because the ATO's published Jill example does not reproduce on the method the command applies. Read the time limits for the contract, notice and recontribution from the ATO's pages for the release date; the command does not apply them. The command takes the amount requested, the maximum release amount when none is given, as the amount released: where the funds released a different gross amount, work the assessable amount from what they released under ITAA 1997 s 313-20(2) and record the difference. Its tax withheld is an estimate, labelled by its `withholding_basis`, and its amount paid comes before any debt with the ATO or another Commonwealth agency that the ATO offsets against the release (the ATO's [Step 4: receiving your FHSS amount](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/super/withdrawing-and-using-your-super/early-access-to-super/first-home-super-saver-scheme/when-you-want-to-release-your-fhss-amount/step-4-receiving-your-fhss-amount) page, published 8 July 2026 and read 22 November 2026), which the command does not take; reconcile both to the ATO's FHSS payment summary and records. Where steps 3 to 5 find excess contributions in a year the scheme counts, keep that year's FHSS figures `UNVERIFIED`, as the command's scope excludes excess contributions. Route the assessable FHSS released amount and the offset to `au-individual-return`. Eligibility, the release request, the purchase or recontribution and the notices are the client's; prepare the facts and do not request, release or notify anything.

gh attestation verify <archive> --repo ryanduguid/australian-accounting --source-ref refs/tags/australian-tax-calculators/v1.1.0 --source-digest c4a716a25f065ccc957c5db83dec711251cb4121 --signer-workflow ryanduguid/release-policy/.github/workflows/release-binary.yml

Stop if any of the 4 fails. On another platform, build the tagged commit from its lock file with Rust 1.99 or later, into a tool directory of its own; `cargo install --list --root <tool-dir>` must show `australian-tax-calculators v1.1.0` with that commit in its source:

```text

```

Run it by its full path, the extracted `australian-tax-calculators` or `<tool-dir>/bin/australian-tax-calculators` (`.exe` on Windows), never one found on `PATH`, as other builds also report 1.1.0; check that `--version` prints exactly `australian-tax-calculators 1.1.0`, and pass `--refusal-json` after the command so a refusal (exit 2) prints its code. Use this local executable; a remote calculator is used only where the user has explicitly permitted that call for this question, on fabricated or firm-approved inputs, and never as a fallback when a local tool refuses. Send `{}` to its `worksheet-catalogue` command for each worksheet's scope statement and its required and optional inputs. Before running a command, send its worksheet name, the command's name with underscores, to its `periods` command, such as `{"kind": "fhss"}`, and confirm it supports the reporting month or income year in question rather than merely accepting the year; if it does not, say so and follow the written method. Each command reads one JSON object on stdin, without the `kind` field that only `periods` takes, with amounts as decimal strings and a `scope_confirmed` flag: set it only once each condition in the scope statement the command returns is evidenced, and where one is not, follow the written method for that item. Send only amounts, dates and the established facts the command takes, never a client's name, address, TFN, ABN or other identifier. Record what it consumed: the release, the engine version, the exact input, the output with the scope statement, sources and source-check date it returns, and the run date. Record a refusal and its code as an open item, and do not change the facts to get past it. Keep the calculator's own labels as its own, reported beside the facts it was given, never as the reviewer's decision on a position. A calculator's figure checks the written method, which is always completed: where they differ, record the difference for the reviewer and adopt neither. Without the executable, follow the written method.

`fhss` works out a first home super saver determination's components and what its release pays and costs, for a determination made from 1 July 2018 to 31 December 2026 in the financial year given as `year`. Give it either `determination`, the 3 components the ATO's determination states, which it takes as given, or `contributions`, each with its date, amount and kind (`employer_voluntary` or `personal`), with `determination_requested` and any `personal_deductions`, from which it estimates the determination; either the Commissioner's `estimated_marginal_rate` or `commissioner_cannot_estimate` true; and, optionally, `amount_requested`, which is the maximum release amount when left out. It returns the maximum release amount, the assessable FHSS released amount, the tax withheld, the amount paid, the offset, the FHSS tax if neither a contract nor a recontribution is notified in time and the recontribution that avoids it. Its scope statement lists the eligibility and contribution conditions to evidence before setting `scope_confirmed`. It refuses an unconfirmed scope (`scope.not_confirmed`); a `year` outside 2018-19 to 2026-27 (`period.unsupported`); a `determination_date` outside `year` (`fhss.year_mismatch`) or after 31 December 2026, the last quarter whose shortfall interest charge rate the ATO had published when it was built (`fhss.rate_unpublished`); both or neither of `contributions` and `determination` (`fhss.basis_invalid`); any contribution kind but those 2, so super guarantee and other mandated or required contributions stay out (`fhss.contribution_kind_invalid`); a contribution before 1 July 2017 or after the determination day (`fhss.contribution_out_of_period`); `contributions` without `determination_requested` (`input.missing_field`); a request date before 1 July 2018 or after the determination day (`fhss.determination_requested_invalid`); a deduction above the year's personal contributions (`fhss.deductions_invalid`); an `amount_requested` above the maximum release amount (`fhss.request_exceeds_maximum`); and neither or both withholding inputs (`input.missing_field`, `input.field_unknown`). Its scope excludes the contract, notice and recontribution time limits, KiwiSaver and other foreign fund transfers, excess contributions, amended determinations, foreign residents, a Medicare levy exemption or reduction in the withholding estimate, and the income tax payable on the assessable amount. For a determination made before 15 September 2024 it adds `transitional_note`, which flags the transitional rule in step 8 for every such determination without testing its conditions: where step 8 finds the circumstances relate to or result from a transitional action, do not rely on its `fhss_tax_if_not_used` or `recontribution_required`. Each excluded case stays with the written method in step 8.

A person can compare a result with the site's [first home super saver calculator](https://duguid.com.au/tools/individual-tax/fhss/) page, which runs in the browser; it is not a substitute for the executable and is not used when the executable refuses.

For step 3's carry-forward test, the site's [concessional catch-up calculator](https://duguid.com.au/tools/individual-tax/concessional-catch-up/) (published and last checked 19 November 2026, read 23 November 2026) works out each earlier year's unused concessional cap from 2018-19 and what is left of the previous 5 years' unused cap for 2024-25, 2025-26 or 2026-27. It runs in the browser and sends no inputs to a server. Before relying on it, confirm it supports the reporting month or income year in question; use it as a cross-check of the cap schedule only, never as a fallback when a local tool refuses. It works the cap only: it does not decide what counts as a concessional contribution, the year a contribution counts in or whether a deduction notice is valid, and it does not work out tax on excess contributions, Division 293 tax, a defined benefit interest or non-concessional contributions. Compare its carry-forward amounts with the ATO's online services record. Enter only amounts and balances, never the person's name, TFN or member number. Record what it consumed: the page, the date run, the inputs and the result. Keep the page's own labels as its own, beside those inputs. Where the page and the cap schedule differ, record the difference for the reviewer and adopt neither.

## Hand-off and checks

A contribution schedule by fund and type tied to fund reports, a concessional cap test with carry-forward workings, a non-concessional cap test with the bring-forward position, and an excess and release summary; for an FHSS release, the determination or contribution schedule with the command's output or the written method's figures. Contribution totals agree to the fund reports. Elections, withdrawals and further contributions are decisions for the client and reviewer.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A client made concessional contributions above the general cap and wants to use carry-forward, but the total super balance at 30 June of the previous year is not evidenced and there is no evidence that one personal contribution will be claimed as a deduction. Do not apply carry-forward or treat the personal contribution as concessional.
