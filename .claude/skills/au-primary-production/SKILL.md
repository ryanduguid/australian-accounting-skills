# Primary production workpapers

## Inputs

Income year, the entity and every owner's interest (individual, partner, trust beneficiary or company), a description of each activity with business evidence (plans, scale, sales records, registrations and history), primary production and non-primary production income and deductions for each owner, prior years' basic taxable income and averaging history including any withdrawal election, farm management deposit statements from each provider (deposits, repayments, transfers, interest and loan offset arrangements), evidence for any early-repayment exception, a livestock reconciliation by class (opening, purchases, natural increase, sales, deaths, rations and closing), the valuation basis used for each class last year, forced disposal or death circumstances and documents, capital expenditure records for water facilities, fencing, fodder storage, horticultural plants and landcare works, and fuel records. For a farm succession, also the proposed transfer steps and any draft documents, a register of the assets and entities involved (land by title, water entitlements, plant, livestock, farm management deposits, shares, units and partnership interests, with each owner and the capacity in which it is held, before and after each step), acquisition dates and cost base evidence, market valuations (valuer, basis and date), turnover and net asset workings for each entity and its affiliates and connected entities, each transferor's age and any retirement or permanent incapacity evidence, how each asset has been used and by whom, any lease of farm land between family entities, and any will, partnership agreement or trust deed that governs the assets.

## Workflow

1. Establish whether a business of primary production is carried on. Record the activity against the primary production categories in the ATO's information for primary producers and prepare a TR 97/11 indicator matrix (significant commercial purpose, more than an intention to engage in business, purpose and prospect of profit, repetition and regularity, similarity to the ordinary trade, businesslike organisation, size, scale and permanency, and whether the activity is better described as a hobby or recreation). No single indicator decides it; the reviewer does. Route any individual's loss to `au-non-commercial-losses`, carrying the other-income facts its primary production exception needs, and the business schedule to `au-sole-trader`, `au-partnership-tax` or `au-trust-distributions`.

2. Split income and deductions into primary production and non-primary production for each owner, and reconcile the split to the business schedule. The farm management deposit test, the averaging component and the loss exception each use their own income definition; build each from its source rather than reusing one total.

3. Farm management deposits (Division 393). Keep a register for each deposit: owner, provider, date, amount, repayments, transfers, interest and offset arrangements. Confirm the owner was an eligible individual carrying on a primary production business in Australia when each deposit was made, then verify the year's non-primary production income test, holding limit, minimum amounts and deduction limit at the source. Test each repayment's assessability under s 393-10, separating the transfers and merged deposits the ATO treats differently. Under s 393-40, an amount repaid within the early period is treated as never having been a farm management deposit unless an exception applies; the ATO's exceptions (natural disaster assistance, severe drought measured from rainfall records, bankruptcy, ceasing the business, death) each have their own evidence. Keep a claimed exception `UNVERIFIED` until its specific evidence is supplied; the ATO describes the drought exception by reference to rainfall records, so check whether a supplied document meets that test rather than assuming a drought declaration does. If a prior-year deduction falls away, route the correction to `au-return-amendment`. Never instruct a provider.

4. Income averaging (Division 392). Record eligibility under s 392-10 (an individual carrying on a primary production business in Australia, the consecutive-year condition and the basic taxable income comparison) and any withdrawal election and its period. Assemble basic taxable income and the averaging component for the year and prior years, with the non-primary production income inclusion bands verified at the source. The ATO calculates the averaging result on assessment; any estimate is a labelled verify item, not the return figure.

5. Livestock and other trading stock. Reconcile head counts by class to the movement records, then values: opening values must equal last year's closing values, and the closing basis (cost, market selling value or replacement value) is chosen and recorded for each class or animal. Check whether a small business entity has chosen the simplified trading stock rules for the year. For natural increase, record the cost election under s 70-55 (actual cost or the cost the regulations prescribe for the class, verified for the year). Account for livestock slaughtered for rations or taken for private use as the ATO's information for primary producers requires. For forced disposal or death of livestock, record the qualifying circumstance and its evidence against s 385-100 and the alternative election in s 385-110 (Subdivision 385-E); the taxpayer decides whether to elect, with the reviewer.

6. Primary production depreciating assets. Classify each item against the meanings in s 40-520 (water facility, horticultural plant, fodder storage asset, fencing asset) for Subdivision 40-F, or as a landcare operation or other Subdivision 40-G item, recording the expenditure date (the rules differ by date), use for primary production or a taxable purpose, and whether the entity is the owner or a lessee. A small business entity has the choice s 328-175(3) describes between those rules and simplified depreciation for these assets. Build the schedule through `au-depreciation-schedule`.

7. Route fuel tax credits to `fuel-tax-credits` and any capital gain on farm land or water entitlements to `au-capital-gains`.

8. Farm succession. Where a farming business is to pass between generations, by sale, gift, a change of partners, a restructure or on death, prepare the evidence a registered tax agent or adviser needs. Do not choose the structure, the timing or who receives what. **Human review pending:** an agent read ss 40-295, 40-340, 70-90, 70-100, 70-105, 102-25, 104-10, 104-60, 104-185, 104-190, 116-30, 128-15, 152-10, 152-40, 152-105, 392-10 and 393-35 of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* in the ATO legal database, and the ATO's farm management deposit and income averaging pages, on 11 November 2026 (not a human review).

   - Assets and entities. Build one register row per asset with its owner and capacity before and after each proposed step, acquisition date (including pre-CGT status), cost base evidence and market value with the valuer, basis and date. Map every individual, partnership, trust and company involved and each interest held. A missing cost or value is unknown, not zero, and leaves every dependent test open.
   - CGT events. For each transfer record the CGT event and the capital proceeds. A change of ownership from one entity to another, including by operation of law, is CGT event A1 (s 104-10), but where more than one event can happen, s 102-25(1) uses the one most specific to the situation, subject to the exceptions in s 102-25(2) to (2C), under which CGT event J2, K5 or K12 applies in addition to the other event: for a transfer to an existing trust, record CGT event E2 (s 104-60(1)) as a candidate for the reviewer. Where the owner chose a small business roll-over under Subdivision 152-E, record the roll-over, its replacement asset period (which starts one year before the last CGT event for the roll-over, s 104-190(1A)), the gain disregarded and each replacement asset: an asset acquired in that period, including before the rolled-over event, or an asset on which fourth element expenditure was incurred in that period (s 104-185(1)(a) and (9)). For each, record whether it was an active asset at the end of the period and, for a share or trust interest, the facts s 104-185(1)(c) requires. CGT event J2 (s 104-185) can happen alongside the transfer (s 102-25(2A)) where, after that period, a change in s 104-185(2) happens, such as the asset stopping being an active asset, or, for a share or trust interest, a change in s 104-185(3); for a share or trust interest that stopped being an active asset only because of changes in the market values of assets the company or trust owned when the interest was acquired or the expenditure incurred, s 104-185(8) says J2 does not happen because of paragraph (2)(a). The reviewer decides whether J2 happens. Record the time of each event under the provision for the event used: for A1, when the contract for the disposal is entered into or, with no contract, when ownership changes (s 104-10(3)); for E2, when the asset is transferred (s 104-60(2)). In a staged succession that decides the income year. Where there are no capital proceeds, or the parties did not deal at arm's length and the proceeds differ from market value, s 116-30 substitutes market value, so a gift or a below-value sale to a family member needs a valuation at the time of the event. For assets that pass on death, record the date of death and each asset passing to the legal personal representative or a beneficiary; s 128-15 treats them as acquired on that day with the cost base its table sets, including market value at death for assets the deceased acquired before 20 September 1985. Route the estate to `au-deceased-estates` and the gain and any CGT reform rules for the period to `au-capital-gains`.
   - Small business CGT concessions. Route the concession evidence to `au-small-business-cgt`, which holds the basic conditions and each concession's extra conditions, and carry the farm facts it needs. For land an owner holds outside the partnership that farms it, the pathway to test is s 152-10(1)(c)(iv) through the passively held partnership asset conditions in s 152-10(1B), not s 152-10(1)(c)(iii), which covers only an interest in an asset of the partnership. For a farming trust, s 152-40(2) disregards s 392-20(1), so a beneficiary is not treated as carrying on the trust's business for the active asset test. For farm land leased to a family farming entity, record the lessee, its relationship to the owner and how it used the land (s 152-40(4A) treats an affiliate's or connected entity's use as the owner's), and each lease's term and purpose, because a main use of deriving rent excludes the land unless that main use was only temporary (s 152-40(4)(e)). For an owner who keeps a partnership interest after the transfer, record the facts the adviser needs to weigh whether the event happens in connection with retirement (s 152-105(d)). Record the income year of each event and, where the owner has died, the date of death. Verify every threshold, limit and age test at the source for the year.
   - Rollovers. Route a transfer of the business or its assets into a company or between entities to `au-restructure-rollovers`, which covers the small business restructure roll-over in Subdivision 328-G and Subdivisions 122-A and 122-B.
   - Depreciating assets. A transfer of plant is a balancing adjustment event (s 40-295(1)); so is a change in interests in a partnership asset where an entity with an interest before still has one after (s 40-295(2)). For that change, s 40-340(3) allows rollover relief if the transferor and transferee jointly choose it in writing, with the content and timing s 40-340(4) requires; record whether a choice exists and who signed it. Record whether any plant is in a small business pool, because s 40-340(3) note 2 points to Subdivision 328-D for the relief in that case. Build the schedule through `au-depreciation-schedule`.
   - Livestock and other trading stock. Reconcile head counts by class at the transfer date. A disposal outside the ordinary course of business brings the market value into assessable income (s 70-90). Where stock becomes owned by a new partnership or other group of owners that includes the original owner, s 70-100 treats it as disposed of at market value unless the owners before and after make the s 70-100(4) election to use its trading stock value; record whether each condition in s 70-100(6) to (10) is met, not met or unevidenced, including the total value of the interests the owners before keep in the stock against s 70-100(6)(b), and whether the election is in writing and signed by each owner before and after. On death, s 70-105 includes the market value of the business's trading stock in the deceased's assessable income unless the legal personal representative elects its trading stock value, which needs the business to continue after the death with the stock still held in it (s 70-105(3) and (5)); the election must be made on or before the day the legal personal representative lodges the deceased's return for the period up to death, unless the Commissioner allows later (s 70-105(7)), so record that lodgement date; route the election to `au-deceased-estates`.
   - Farm management deposits. A depositor's rights cannot be transferred to another entity (item 6 of the table in s 393-35), and the deposit must be repaid if the owner dies or becomes bankrupt, or ceases to carry on a primary production business in Australia and does not start carrying on such a business again within 120 days (item 11). For each outgoing owner, record each deposit, the date the owner stops farming, any primary production business the owner continues or starts, and the date of death if any; test any repayment under step 3. The successor cannot take over a deposit; test any deposit the successor makes under step 3 on the successor's own eligibility.
   - Income averaging. Averaging applies to an individual who has carried on a primary production business for 2 or more income years in a row and meets the basic taxable income condition in s 392-10(1). After an individual stops carrying on the business, s 392-10(2) continues averaging only while, in each later year, the individual has assessable income derived from that business or carries on a primary production business. For each outgoing individual, record the last year of carrying on the business and later income from it; for each incoming individual, record the first year of carrying on a primary production business (directly, as a partner or as a trust beneficiary) and the basic taxable income history step 4 uses. The ATO's income averaging page says calculations do not start until the first year in which basic taxable income is greater than or equal to the year before; the ATO calculates the result on assessment.
   - State taxes and GST. Route transfer duty, including any family farm or primary production concession the jurisdiction's revenue office provides, to `au-transfer-duty`, and land tax, including any primary production land exemption, to `au-land-tax`. Carry the land's use, the business evidence from step 1, the parties' relationship and the transfer dates. Route the GST treatment of the land and business, including farm land and going concern treatment, to `au-gst-property`.
   - Decisions outside this skill. Whether, when and how to transfer, who receives which assets, equalisation between family members, wills, powers of attorney, family agreements, changes to a partnership agreement or trust deed, finance and guarantees, social security effects and the choice between pathways are legal, financial and family decisions for the family with their lawyer and registered advisers. List each as an open decision with its owner; do not recommend one. Route trust deed questions to `au-trust-deed-review`.

## Hand-off and checks

A business indicator matrix, an income split for each owner, a farm management deposit register and exception evidence list, averaging inputs, a livestock reconciliation in numbers and values, the forced disposal election evidence and the classification of primary production assets. For a farm succession, also the asset and entity register, a CGT event schedule, a concession and rollover evidence matrix, the effect on each individual's deposits and averaging, the routing list and the open decisions with their owners. Head counts agree to movement records and values to the trading stock schedule; deposits and repayments agree to provider statements.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A partner in a farming partnership also earns off-farm wages and plans a farm management deposit before year end. Record the partner's taxable non-primary production income evidence and keep the deduction conditional until the year's income test and the partner's share of partnership income are verified.
