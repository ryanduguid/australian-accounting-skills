# Fixed asset register and tax depreciation schedule

## Inputs

Income year, entity type and aggregated turnover evidence, small business entity status and every simplified depreciation choice or opt-out by year, the prior-year closing fixed asset register and tax depreciation schedule (adjustable values, pool balances and effective life choices), additions with invoices, contract or acquisition dates, cost elements and GST treatment, the date each asset was first used or installed ready for use, taxable-use evidence (logbooks, usage records), disposals, losses and transfers with dates and proceeds, building or structural improvement expenditure with construction dates and use, business capital expenditure that is not part of an asset's cost (each outlay's amount, date, invoice, the business it relates to and its purpose), the accounting depreciation schedule and trial balance, the entity's documented accounting policy for property, plant and equipment (useful life or rate, method and residual value by asset class, and any part depreciated separately), and any deferred tax workpaper.

## Workflow

1. Roll the register forward. Agree the opening register to the prior-year closing tax schedule and to the accounting register, one row per asset with a stable reference. Tie additions to invoices and the ledger, and disposals to sale or scrapping documents. Flag duplicates, any asset still listed after a disposal and any asset both pooled and depreciated individually. Each asset must sit in one regime only: individual Division 40 decline in value, a low-value pool, the general small business pool, Division 43 capital works or an immediate deduction. Route per-machine plant costing, plant effective life tables and finance structure to `plant-and-equipment-costing`, rental property assets to `au-rental-property`, primary production asset classification to `au-primary-production` and an employee's car to `au-car-expenses`.

2. Establish cost and start time. Confirm the entity holds the asset and record the first and second elements of cost under Subdivision 40-C, reduced by any input tax credit entitlement as the Guide to depreciating assets explains. The start time is when the asset is first used or installed ready for use for any purpose (s 40-60 of the ITAA 1997); an invoice or payment date is not a substitute. A missing start time leaves that asset's decline in value blank and `UNVERIFIED`.

3. Record effective life and method. Under s 40-95 of the ITAA 1997 the entity chooses either the Commissioner's determination (limited to the one in force at the time s 40-95(2) specifies) or a self-assessed life under s 40-105, for the year the start time occurs; record the choice, its source and any capped life as a verify item. Record the diminishing value or prime cost method chosen for each asset and check any change against the method choice rules. Apportion each year's decline in value for non-taxable use from the evidence; an estimated percentage stays `UNVERIFIED`.

4. Apply the pools. For Subdivision 40-E, verify the low-cost and low-value asset definitions in s 40-425 of the ITAA 1997 for the year, check whether an earlier pool choice binds later low-cost assets, and roll the pool forward (opening balance, additions at their taxable-use portion, reductions on disposal, decline in value, closing balance). For a small business entity, confirm status and the Subdivision 328-D choice for the year (s 328-175 of the ITAA 1997 applies it to all depreciating assets the entity holds, subject to its exceptions). The instant asset write-off limit, its period and the lock-out rules depend on the law for the actual income year: read the ATO pages below and the enabling Act, record the Act, its assent status and the check date, and state no threshold from memory. Roll the general small business pool forward the same way. Where the released calculators in [Tax calculators](#tax-calculators) are installed, cross-check a low-value pool's decline in value and closing balance with `depreciation-low-value-pool`, and a general small business pool's deduction and closing balance with `depreciation-small-business-pool`, giving each the taxable use or taxable purpose amounts this step establishes. Both cover the income years 2020-21 to 2026-27. Neither decides which assets may or must go into a pool, small business entity status, the simplified depreciation choice, each taxable use percentage or which assets the instant asset write-off or temporary full expensing took instead: those stay with this step. The low-value pool command leaves out whether temporary full expensing applied to an asset in 2020-21 to 2022-23, the sampling rule, the capital gain or loss on a disposal's non-taxable part and primary production apportionment; the small business pool command leaves out roll-over relief, assets excluded from simplified depreciation and the car limit, and refuses the one 2020-21 case where backing business investment could change the result. Work those from the sources alone.

5. Apply caps. Reduce a passenger car's first element of cost to the car limit under s 40-230 of the ITAA 1997 for the financial year the entity started to hold it, verifying the limit at the source. Record any other limit or cap as a verify item with its provision.

6. Process each balancing adjustment event (sale, scrapping, loss, destruction or ceasing to hold) under Subdivision 40-D: compare termination value with adjustable value just before the event (s 40-285 of the ITAA 1997), reduce for any non-taxable use, and record the assessable or deductible amount and its evidence. Check rollover relief and any CGT consequence for non-taxable use with `au-capital-gains`. For a pooled asset, the taxable-use portion of its termination value reduces the closing pool balance instead; verify the excess rule if that portion exceeds the balance. Remove the asset from the closing register only after the event is recorded. Where the released calculators in [Tax calculators](#tax-calculators) are installed, cross-check an individually depreciated asset's balancing adjustment, its reduction for non-taxable use and any CGT event K7 amount with `balancing-adjustment`, giving it each year's decline and deduction from this schedule, and a trade-in at arm's length for a replacement first held and used in the same income year with `trade-in`, which also works the GST on both sides and the replacement's cost. They cover events in 2024-25 to 2026-27 (`trade-in` only 2025-26 and 2026-27) and leave out pooled and written-off assets, roll-over relief and involuntary disposals: work those from the sources alone.

6a. **Business capital expenditure.** Schedule each capital outlay that is not part of an asset's cost and that the records show relates to a business: its amount, the income year incurred, the business it relates to (one carried on, one that used to be carried on, one proposed to be carried on, or the wind-up of an entity that carried one on, s 40-880(2)(a) to (d) of the ITAA 1997), the extent the business is carried on for a taxable purpose (s 40-880(3) and (4)), each exclusion in s 40-880(5), (8) and (9) tested against its evidence and, for a business proposed to be carried on, the circumstances bearing on whether, having regard to any relevant circumstances, it is reasonable to conclude that it is proposed to be carried on within a reasonable time (s 40-880(7)). Read [s 40-880 of the ITAA 1997](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/40-880) and [TR 2011/6](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=TXR/TR20116/NAT/ATO/00001) for the period. The eligible amount is deducted in equal proportions over 5 income years starting in the year it is incurred (s 40-880(2) of the ITAA 1997), or in full in that year for a start-up expense that meets s 40-880(2A); roll each item's remaining proportions forward from the prior-year schedule. Whether an outlay is capital, business related and not excluded, the taxable purpose extent, the reasonable time test in s 40-880(7) of the ITAA 1997 and the entity tests in s 40-880(2A) and (2B) of the ITAA 1997 are reviewer decisions: record the evidence for each, not a conclusion. Where the released calculators in [Tax calculators](#tax-calculators) are installed, cross-check the year's deduction and each item's schedule with `business-capital-expenditure`. It covers items incurred from 1 July 2020 for the income years 2020-21 to 2027-28, for an income year from 1 July to 30 June, and refuses an outlay that forms part of a depreciating asset's cost (s 40-880(5)(a) of the ITAA 1997) or could be taken into account in working out a capital gain or loss (s 40-880(5)(f)) unless s 40-880(6) applies. Non-arm's length amounts, non-commercial losses, personal services income, an entity that ceases to exist within the 5 years and website and software costs are outside its scope; work those from the sources alone.

7. Keep capital works separate. Under Division 43 (s 43-10 of the ITAA 1997), schedule each construction expenditure area with its construction expenditure, construction start date, current-year use and the rate the law sets for that date, verified at the source. Check the construction expenditure definition before including any item, and never carry an item in both schedules.

8. Reconcile tax to accounting. By asset class, reconcile tax written-down values to accounting carrying amounts and explain each difference (write-offs, pools, different lives or methods, the car limit, Division 43 against building depreciation, impairment and revaluation). Where the accounting register or trial balance is missing, still lay out the reconciliation with the accounting values blank and list the tax-side corrections as potential differences to explain. Once the accounting values are supplied, give temporary-difference inputs to the deferred tax workpaper and tie accounting depreciation to the trial balance. Route the ledger close to `month-end-close` or `year-end-workpapers` and the tax reconciliation to `au-company-tax` or `au-sole-trader`.

8a. **Prepare the accounting depreciation schedule.** Work only from the entity's documented accounting policy, and record the policy document, its date and who approved it. **Human review pending:** an agent read on 12 November 2026, not a human review. [AASB 116 Property, Plant and Equipment](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-116-dec-2022) (compilation 6, for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2023) defines the depreciable amount as cost, or another amount substituted for cost, less residual value (AASB 116 paragraph 6), and requires it to be allocated on a systematic basis over the asset's useful life (AASB 116 paragraph 50) by a method that reflects the expected pattern of consumption (AASB 116 paragraph 60). AASB 116 also requires each part whose cost is significant in relation to the item's total cost to be depreciated separately (AASB 116 paragraph 43), starts depreciation when the asset is available for use (AASB 116 paragraph 55), and requires residual value, useful life and method to be reviewed at least at each financial year-end (AASB 116 paragraphs 51 and 61). Open the compilation that applies to the reporting period. Where the policy gives no useful life or rate, method or residual value for a class, leave that class's depreciation and carrying amounts blank and `UNVERIFIED`, and list the missing policy item for the reviewer. Never adopt a tax effective life, pool, write-off or tax method as an accounting one unless the recorded policy adopts it. For each asset, roll forward from the opening accounting register: cost, accumulated depreciation, additions, disposals, depreciation for the period and closing carrying amount, starting depreciation from the date the evidence shows the asset was available for use; a missing date leaves that asset's depreciation blank and `UNVERIFIED`. By class, tie closing cost, closing accumulated depreciation and the period's depreciation expense to the trial balance. Where a class does not tie, show the difference as its own line, do not adjust the schedule to force the tie, and carry it as an open item with evidence needed, owner and next action. Carry the closing carrying amounts into step 8; for a class that does not tie, carry the schedule's carrying amount, the trial balance's and the difference, adopt neither, and keep that class's step 8 results conditional until the open item is resolved. Whether the policy suits the assets and any change in an estimate are reviewer decisions; propose journals, never post them.

9. If the `aus-accounting` MCP tools are available in this session, they can cross-check the first-year decline in value of an ordinary tangible Division 40 asset with an established effective life and taxable use, using the `depreciation` worksheet of `calculate_tax_worksheet`; it does not check any other step. From `aus-accounting-mcp` 0.12.0 that worksheet runs the released calculators executable, so it refuses until the executable in [Tax calculators](#tax-calculators) is on `PATH` or named by its absolute path in `AUS_TAX_CALCULATORS_BIN`, and it runs one only if its SHA-256 is the released executable's or, for one built from source, the value in `AUS_TAX_CALCULATORS_SHA256`. Prefer the variable, naming the executable verified as that section describes. An earlier server, such as 0.10.0 or 0.11.0, accepts only an earlier release and refuses this one; the development plugin starts 0.12.0. Record a refusal as an open item. Record what it consumed and returned, including its version and every `UNKNOWN` result. Always complete the step as written.

## Tax calculators

The `business-capital-expenditure`, `balancing-adjustment`, `trade-in`, `depreciation-low-value-pool` and `depreciation-small-business-pool` commands come from the released [`australian-tax-calculators` 1.2.0](https://github.com/ryanduguid/australian-accounting/releases/tag/australian-tax-calculators/v1.2.0) ([source at its tagged commit](https://github.com/ryanduguid/australian-accounting/tree/31ecb4022aa0127ae9f672798d1d9fbfe6388e85/packages/australian-tax-calculators)), the executable `aus-accounting-mcp` 0.12.0 also runs for its tax worksheets. It is installed separately, and `pip` does not install it. When installation is approved, download the release's x86_64 Linux or Windows archive with its `SHA256SUMS`, and verify both before extracting anything:

```text
gh release download australian-tax-calculators/v1.2.0 --repo ryanduguid/australian-accounting --pattern SHA256SUMS --pattern <archive>
gh attestation verify SHA256SUMS --repo ryanduguid/australian-accounting --source-ref refs/tags/australian-tax-calculators/v1.2.0 --source-digest 31ecb4022aa0127ae9f672798d1d9fbfe6388e85 --signer-workflow ryanduguid/release-policy/.github/workflows/release-binary.yml
sha256sum --check --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS
gh attestation verify <archive> --repo ryanduguid/australian-accounting --source-ref refs/tags/australian-tax-calculators/v1.2.0 --source-digest 31ecb4022aa0127ae9f672798d1d9fbfe6388e85 --signer-workflow ryanduguid/release-policy/.github/workflows/release-binary.yml
```

Stop if any of the 4 fails. On another platform, build the tagged commit from its lock file with Rust 1.99 or later, into a tool directory of its own; `cargo install --list --root <tool-dir>` must show `australian-tax-calculators v1.2.0` with that commit in its source:

```text
cargo install --locked --root <tool-dir> --git https://github.com/ryanduguid/australian-accounting --rev 31ecb4022aa0127ae9f672798d1d9fbfe6388e85 australian-tax-calculators
```

Run it by its full path, the extracted `australian-tax-calculators` or `<tool-dir>/bin/australian-tax-calculators` (`.exe` on Windows), never one found on `PATH`, as other builds also report 1.2.0; check that `--version` prints exactly `australian-tax-calculators 1.2.0`, and pass `--refusal-json` after the command so a refusal (exit 2) prints its code. Use this local executable; a remote calculator is used only where the user has explicitly permitted that call for this question, on fabricated or firm-approved inputs, and never as a fallback when a local tool refuses. Send `{}` to its `worksheet-catalogue` command for each worksheet's scope statement and its required and optional inputs. Send the command's name in underscores to its `periods` command first, such as `{"kind": "business_capital_expenditure"}`, and confirm it supports the reporting month or income year in question rather than merely accepting the year; if it does not, say so and follow the written method. Each command reads one JSON object on stdin, without the `kind` field that only `periods` takes, with amounts as decimal strings and a `scope_confirmed` flag: set it only once each condition in the scope statement the command returns is evidenced, and where one is not, follow the written method for that item. Send only amounts, income years and the established facts each command takes, never client names, asset serial numbers, registrations or other identifiers. Record what it consumed: the release, the engine version, the exact input, the output with the scope statement, sources and source-check date it returns, and the run date. Record a refusal and its code as an open item, and do not change the facts to get past it. Keep the calculator's own labels as its own, reported beside the facts it was given, never as the reviewer's decision on a position. A calculator's figure checks the written method, which is always completed: where they differ, record the difference for the reviewer and adopt neither. Without the executable, follow the written method.

For step 4's low-value or general small business pool, or step 7's capital works deduction, the site's [depreciation pools and capital works calculator](https://duguid.com.au/tools/business-calculators/depreciation-pools/) (published and last checked 22 November 2026, read 22 November 2026) works one pool or capital works area for an income year from 2020-21 to 2026-27. It runs in the browser and sends no inputs to a server. Before relying on it, confirm it supports the reporting month or income year in question; use it as a cross-check of the schedule only, never as a fallback when a local tool refuses. You enter each amount after the taxable use percentage: it does not decide which assets may go into a pool, small business entity status or the simplified depreciation choice, or each asset's taxable use. It does not work out temporary full expensing, the instant asset write-off or backing business investment, and refuses the one 2020-21 small business pool case where that could apply. It leaves out the change of business use adjustment, roll-over relief, the car limit and balancing adjustments outside a pool. For capital works it refuses works begun before 16 September 1987 and the higher rate for works begun before 27 February 1992, and it does not decide whether the works qualify, work out the undeducted construction expenditure or split it amongst co-owners. Enter only amounts, years, dates and percentages, never the client's name, an asset serial number or a property address. Record what it consumed: the page, the date run, the inputs and the result. Keep the page's own labels as its own, beside those inputs. Where the page and the schedule differ, record the difference for the reviewer and adopt neither.

For an asset's effective life, the site's [depreciation rates page](https://duguid.com.au/tools/depreciation-rates/) (read 23 November 2026) lists every effective life in the Commissioner's current effective life determination with both rates and a year's deduction worked out. Before relying on it, confirm it supports the reporting month or income year in question; use it as a cross-check of the schedule's effective lives and rates only, never as a fallback when a local tool refuses. It does not decide whether something is a depreciating asset, who holds it, what it cost or its taxable use, and it does not cover an asset first held before 10 May 2006. Check each life against the registered determination before relying on it. Enter only costs, dates and percentages, never an asset serial number or the client's name. Record what it consumed: the page, the date run, the inputs and the result. Keep the page's own labels as its own, beside those inputs. Where the page and the schedule differ, record the difference for the reviewer and adopt neither.

## Hand-off and checks

A rolled-forward register, an individual asset schedule, pool roll-forwards, a balancing adjustment schedule, a separate capital works schedule, an accounting depreciation schedule for each class the documented policy covers and a tax-to-accounting reconciliation. Each asset appears in one regime; closing tax values agree to the schedules and accounting values to the trial balance. Accounting lives, methods and residual values come from the recorded policy, never from the tax schedule. Effective life choices, regime choices and disputed taxable-use percentages are reviewer decisions.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A small business entity opted out of simplified depreciation in an earlier year that the file does not identify. Keep the instant asset write-off and pool treatment conditional until the opt-out year and the lock-out rules for the actual income year are verified; do not assume re-entry.
