# BAS preparation

Prepare a BAS workpaper from ledger exports and tie every label back to the general ledger. The output is a review-ready workpaper, not a lodgement. Lodgement belongs to the registered agent.

For a transaction GST audit or coding review, read [the transaction review procedure](references/transaction-gst-review.md) before classifying rows. Account for every supplied detail occurrence and assess supply treatment, buyer credit entitlement and attribution separately. A report or control-account tie-out alone cannot establish correct treatment. Missing evidence remains unresolved; it does not mean zero GST. Transaction-only work does not require unrelated payroll or prior-period reports and does not establish readiness of the complete BAS.

The inputs, workflow and handover checklist below cover full BAS preparation. Apply withholding, instalment and prior-period checks only when those activities are in the agreed scope. For a transaction-only engagement, use the reference's workflow and handover contract, including required population and GST reconciliation checks. Hand over the clearly labelled transaction register with unresolved items and coverage limits; it does not establish full-BAS readiness.

For full-BAS preparation, if the folder uses the filenames [Workpaper Review Gate](https://github.com/ryanduguid/accounting-review-pipeline/tree/main/packages/review-ready-gate) expects and the operator has already installed the `review-ready` tool, run `review-ready gate --profile bas` before handing over the full pack. A `NOT_READY` or `BLOCKED` full-BAS pack goes back to the preparer. Retain any existing gate status unchanged alongside a transaction-only register and explain that it concerns full-BAS readiness. Do not treat `READY` as lodgement approval.

## Inputs needed

Ask for these if not provided (period-locked where possible; see `xero-exports`, or `myob-exports` for MYOB, for pulling and validating them):
1. Activity Statement report for the BAS period, plus the GST Reconciliation report for the GST control tie-out
2. Trial balance as at period end
3. GL detail for GST control accounts (GST collected / GST paid, or single GST account)
4. Prior period BAS figures (for variance comparison)
5. The entity's GST registration basis (cash or accruals) and lodgement cycle (monthly/quarterly)
6. Payroll activity summary for the BAS period, per the payroll system (gross, pre-tax salary sacrifice, PAYG withheld) and the PAYG instalment rate or amount if the entity pays instalments
7. The figures actually lodged for each prior period in scope, and the ATO statement for the entity's account, where the drift and post-lodgement checks are in scope. An absent input means the check is `not run`, not clean
8. Where the activity statement shows ATO PAYG withholding pre-fill, the pre-filled W1 and W2 amounts and the date they were viewed
9. Where payroll is in scope, taxable wages by state or territory and total Australian wages (group wages where the entity is grouped), each for the month and the year to date, total Australian wages for each week of the month where wages are paid in Queensland or another jurisdiction whose registration page sets a weekly amount, and the payroll tax registrations held, including evidence of wages paid and wages the employer is liable to pay, for the payroll tax screen

A connector read can supply the trial balance, but only through the read-only connector route in `xero-exports` (for MYOB, in `myob-exports`), which stops unless the connection is confirmed read-only, records the organisation, period, basis and capture time and refuses every write. The Activity Statement's transaction tabs and the GST Reconciliation report still come from exports, so request them; never tie a label to a figure assembled from connector records.

**Human review pending:** an agent read on 6 November 2026, not a human review. Before any connector read, a registered tax or BAS agent needs a record of this client's permission: [TPB(GS) 55/2026](https://www.tpb.gov.au/tpbgs-552026-use-artificial-intelligence-and-code-professional-conduct) says that entering client information into an AI tool can be a disclosure to a third party, depending on how the tool is configured and used (paragraph 23). If no record is supplied, stop, call nothing on that connection and use exports. A file export is not an exemption: where client information in it reaches an external AI provider, assess that disclosure first or use the firm's approved local process.

## Workflow

Before mapping any label, record the period, labels actually present on the
entity's statement, STP status, withholding category and applicable reporting
method. Record the primary ATO URL, check date and verified treatment for each
label used. The dated examples below are prompts for verification, not defaults.
If the statement or primary-source verification is unavailable, stop label
mapping and request confirmation and supporting evidence. A user-supplied
answer remains unverified and flagged for the authorised reviewer. Search snippets
and page titles do not establish the rule. Keep source-dependent labels and
treatment pending while continuing arithmetic supported by the supplied facts.
Use only supplied or verified rates and measurement bases in that arithmetic;
leave a gross-up blank when either is missing. For a fabricated exercise, list
missing evidence as requirements for the reviewer and retain the fictional
scope, without requesting real client records.

1. **Confirm the basis.** The report basis (cash versus accruals) must match the entity's ATO registration basis before relying on its BAS-period totals. Flag a mismatch and stop reliance on affected totals, mapping, reconciliation and completeness claims. Continue source-row accounting and supply/entitlement assessments supported independently of the discrepancy. Obtain a correctly based export or sufficient verified reconciliation and invoice/payment-event evidence before supporting affected period amounts. Keep unknown attribution unresolved and retain the basis discrepancy; do not conclude that the complete BAS is ready.
2. **Map ledger figures to the labels actually present.** Inspect the entity's activity statement and current ATO guidance before building the workpaper; do not assume that every entity must report every label. G1 total sales (confirm GST-inclusive versus exclusive convention), 1A GST on sales, 1B GST on purchases, and G10/G11 capital versus non-capital acquisitions apply according to the entity's reporting method. Simpler BAS entities report just G1, 1A and 1B in the GST section (the entity must meet the ATO's simpler BAS turnover test; read the current threshold at ato.gov.au rather than stating it from memory). For PAYG withholding, first establish whether the employer reports through Single Touch Payroll (STP), whether W1 appears on this statement, and whether a special reporting rule such as the large-withholder rule applies. Current ATO guidance says a large withholder reporting through STP no longer needs to report an amount at W1 on its activity statements; that relief is a large-withholder rule, not a rule for every STP reporter, so small and medium withholders still complete W1 where the statement carries it (source: ATO, 'Pay as you go (PAYG) withholding', activity statement labels page at ato.gov.au, checked 19 August 2026; re-verify at use time). Never create or require W1 merely because payroll data exists. Where W1 is present and required, map total payments subject to withholding (payroll gross less pre-tax salary sacrifice, plus payments under a voluntary agreement). Map W2 and any other PAYG labels the statement requires. Take the underlying payroll amounts from the payroll activity summary, never from GL wages expense; the accrual in the expense account is not the amount paid in the period. Check whether the payroll report already carries voluntary-agreement payments (STP reporting can include them). Where it does not, take the gross payments from the payment register or the underlying payment records, never from the PAYG withholding payable account: that account holds only the amount withheld, which is the W2 component, so using it at W1 understates gross payments by the amount paid to the payee. Reconcile the withholding separately through the liability account's period transactions and roll-forward. Non-payroll withholding does not all belong at W1/W2: withholding because no ABN was quoted sits at its own label (W4 at time of writing), other amounts withheld sit at W3, and of the non-payroll amounts only voluntary-agreement payments and, for a labour-hire firm, payments to its workers under labour hire arrangements, together with their withholding, belong in the W1/W2 reconciliation when those labels apply. The withheld amounts for all 3 sit in the PAYG withholding payable account, so split that balance rather than treating it as one label's worth. Confirm the current label for each at ato.gov.au before lodging. If ato.gov.au is unreachable from this session, stop and ask the user which label applies, record it as 'per [name], [date], unverified', and flag it on the workpaper. Labels and instalment arrangements change, so verify the current label set the same way.

   Where wine equalisation tax (WET) or luxury car tax (LCT) is in scope, verify the entity's reporting method and period against the [ATO's activity statement guidance](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/business-activity-statements-bas). Take WET labels 1C and 1D and LCT labels 1E and 1F from supported workpapers for the same reporting period. Keep payable and refundable amounts separately traceable to the sales, import, credit or adjustment records and reconcile each amount to its ledger control account. Keep these amounts separate from the GST tie-out. If the reporting period, label, classification or calculation is unresolved, mark the affected amount `UNVERIFIED` and carry it to the reviewer.

   Then compare any ATO PAYG withholding pre-fill. Where the activity statement shows pre-filled W1 and W2 amounts, compare each with the payroll-derived amount above and list every difference with the pay events and payments behind it. The payroll records stay the source for W1 and W2: never replace a payroll-derived amount with the pre-filled one, and never treat the pre-fill as clearing a difference. **Human review pending:** an agent read on 4 November 2026, not a human review. The ATO's [Correcting information reported through STP](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/hiring-and-paying-your-workers/single-touch-payroll/correcting-information-reported-through-stp) and [STP reporting issues and errors](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/hiring-and-paying-your-workers/single-touch-payroll/stp-reporting-issues-and-errors) pages (both last updated 23 October 2026) say pre-fill is available for small and medium withholders, that an employer-level correction reported through STP is included in it, and that a correction reported through STP must still be included in the activity statement. Classify each difference by its cause. The causes these current pages support are: a payment that is voluntary to report through STP, such as a payment under a voluntary agreement (Table 2 of [Payments you must report through STP](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/hiring-and-paying-your-workers/single-touch-payroll/payments-you-must-report-through-stp)), that was not reported through STP; an employer-level correction made in payroll or the ledger but not reported through STP; and closely held payees reported quarterly by a reasonable estimate, for whom the ATO's [Concessional reporting](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/hiring-and-paying-your-workers/single-touch-payroll/concessional-reporting) page says PAYG withholding is reported and paid on the activity statement on that estimate. The ATO's dedicated pre-fill page, which listed the causes of mismatch, could not be read on 4 November 2026: its former address redirects to the STP landing page, and the ATO sitemap lists no replacement. Until a person verifies a current list of causes, leave any other difference unclassified and carry it to the reviewer. Pre-fill that agrees with payroll does not show that W1 or W2 is right.

   Then, where payroll is in scope, screen payroll tax. Payroll tax is a state and territory tax with no BAS label; this screen only reuses payroll data already in scope. Run the registration test in step 3 of `au-payroll-tax-states` on input 9: for each state or territory where wages are paid, compare total Australian wages (group wages where the entity is grouped), not that jurisdiction's own wages, with that jurisdiction's registration thresholds, the month's total against its monthly threshold and the year-to-date total against its annual threshold, apportioned as its revenue office page directs. Where a jurisdiction's registration page also sets a weekly amount, compare each week's total with it too: Queensland's page requires registration within 7 days of the first month in which Australian wages exceed its weekly amount, even where wages for the year are expected to stay below its annual threshold, and Tasmania's and South Australia's pages also state weekly figures; read how each applies. Read each threshold from the revenue office page that skill names. For each jurisdiction record its own wages, the total compared, each threshold with its source and check date, and each difference; list each jurisdiction where wages are paid and the total exceeds a threshold with no registration recorded. Separately, where the entity is a group member and pays or is liable to pay Queensland wages with no Queensland registration recorded, list it whatever the thresholds show, because that skill's step 3 records Queensland's group member registration rule; the rule turns on taxable wages, which that step settles and this screen does not. A hit is a lead for that skill and the reviewer, not a liability or a registration decision, and the payroll activity summary's gross is not taxable wages. Report the screen as `not run` where wages by jurisdiction, total Australian or group wages, the weekly totals a weekly amount needs or the registration list are not supplied, or where the evidence cannot establish whether Queensland wages are already payable, or where `au-payroll-tax-states` is not installed.
3. **Map the PAYG instalment.** Where the entity pays PAYG income tax instalments, the statement carries an instalment section alongside GST and withholding. Establish which option the entity uses: the ATO-advised instalment amount, or the instalment rate applied to the period's instalment income. Map the advised amount, or the rate and the instalment income it applies to, to the instalment labels actually present on the statement; verify the current instalment label set at ato.gov.au rather than assuming label numbers. For the rate option, take instalment income from the ledger for the period and document the calculation; a varied rate or amount must be supported by the variation record and its reason. If ato.gov.au is unreachable from this session, stop and ask the user which label applies, record it as 'per [name], [date], unverified', and flag it on the workpaper. If the entity does not pay instalments, record that and move on; never invent an instalment section the statement does not carry.
4. **Tie out the GST control account.** Net GST per BAS (1A − 1B) must equal the movement in the GST control account(s) for the period, adjusted for payments/refunds of prior BAS. Reconcile to the cent; document any rounding. Retain the original amount, verified rounding rule, formula and resulting difference. Recompute the formula, including whether it drops cents or rounds to nearest. For cash-basis GST registrations with accrual ledgers, the control account movement will not equal 1A − 1B directly. Reconcile via Xero's GST Reconciliation report or adjust for the GST component of opening and closing AR/AP.
5. **Review coding exceptions.** Use the linked transaction procedure for a GST coding audit. Review recorded GST and zero-GST groups in both directions: missing GST and unsupported GST claims. For bank and payment-service charges, establish the actual service and verify its treatment; the account or supplier name cannot distinguish a financial supply from a separately supplied service. Review stamp duty, wages, entertainment/FBT, private use and capital/non-capital mapping against the relevant facts and authority, rather than automatically reversing credits. G10/G11 checks apply where the reporting method requires those labels. Classify by the nature of the purchase first; there is no generic capital threshold that overrides that classification. If a capital-purchase concession is proposed, verify its current amount limit, turnover condition and record-keeping condition in the primary ATO text before applying it. If that text is unavailable, leave the concession and affected classification unverified for review.

   Then test import GST on broker and courier invoices. List each customs broker, freight forwarder or courier invoice amount described as GST on imported goods (often inside 'duties and taxes') that is coded to a cost account with no GST credit. Each is a possible missed credit, not a finding. As read on 4 November 2026, the ATO's [GST and imported goods](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/rules-for-specific-transactions/international-transactions/gst-and-imported-goods) page allows a credit where the entity makes a taxable importation, is registered for GST and imports the goods for a creditable purpose, and only with the finalised import declaration (N10, or N30 for goods cleared out of a warehouse) and the matching Home Affairs receipt, kept by the entity or by its broker for it. Under [GSTR 2003/15](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=GST/GSTR200315/NAT/ATO/00001) the entity imports the goods only if it causes them to be brought to Australia for its own purposes and completes the customs formalities, itself or through a broker or agent (paragraphs 49 and 54); a broker, courier or freight forwarder that moves or clears the goods does not import them (paragraph 56). A tax invoice is not the evidence, and where the declaration names another entity as owner, the entity also needs evidence that it paid the GST and that the named owner acted as its agent (paragraph 292). The credit belongs to the tax period in which the GST on the importation was paid, even where the broker paid it and the entity reimbursed it later (paragraphs 289 and 290). Exclude each of the following and record why:
   - an entity approved for the [deferred GST scheme](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/rules-for-specific-transactions/international-transactions/deferred-gst), whose deferred GST the ATO pre-fills at label 7A of the monthly BAS. Reconcile that amount to the import declarations, and propose at 1B for the same period only the credit the evidence establishes (paragraph 291). The ATO's deferred GST page says to claim the full deferred amount where the entity is entitled to a full credit; an importation not wholly for a creditable purpose, such as goods applied partly privately or in making input taxed supplies, carries a smaller credit (paragraph 18), and a portion the evidence does not establish stays unresolved. Take no second credit from a broker invoice;
   - goods another entity imported on its own account, such as a supplier that brought them to Australia as its own stock, entered them in its own name and sold them to this entity after importation (paragraphs 49, 72 and 73): this entity makes no creditable importation, and any credit depends on that supplier's taxable supply and tax invoice. Where the named owner may have acted as this entity's agent, do not exclude the item: leave it unresolved until the agency and payment evidence in paragraph 292 is supplied or ruled out;
   - a supplier's or broker's recharge that is not import GST, such as customs duty, freight or the broker's own fees; assess each such amount on its own facts.

   Propose no reclassification until the evidence above is supplied; carry each item to the reviewer with what is missing.
6. **Variance check.** Compare each label to the same period prior year (same quarter for quarterly lodgers, same month for monthly) and the immediately prior period. Flag movements beyond the agreed threshold, measured on the whole movement even where part of it is explained, with a one-line explanation each. The threshold is the firm's or engagement's call, so ask for it rather than inventing one. Turn each flagged movement, or the part of one, with no supported explanation into one plain-words question on a draft client question list: the area in words (such as sales, purchases or wages, not a label number), the period, the movement and the record that would explain it. The list asks for facts and records only, states no GST or other tax treatment, position, conclusion or advice, and no rate, threshold or due date, and suggests no reason the records do not show. Mark it drafted and not sent: an authorised human reviews and sends it, and this skill never sends it.
7. **Lodged-figure drift.** Re-run the prior periods in scope on the same basis and compare each label with the figure actually lodged. List each difference with the transactions behind it, including back-dated entries and edits made after lodgement. Mark each for an authorised human to decide: correct on a current BAS, revise the earlier period or treat as an adjustment. Read the ATO's [mistakes and adjustments guidance](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/business-activity-statements-bas/fixing-bas-mistakes-or-making-adjustments) for the current limits and conditions at use time, including its separate route for PAYG withholding corrections reported through STP; do not state them from memory. A matching re-run does not show the lodged figures were right.
8. **After lodgement.** Once an authorised human has lodged, inspect what has already been posted to the ATO control account for the period, propose a closing journal only for the lodged GST, withholding and instalment amounts not yet moved there, and check that account against the ATO statement. Show unexplained differences as exceptions. A human posts the journal. If no lodgement is evidenced, leave this step pending.
9. **Assemble the workpaper.** Summary page (labels, amounts, tie-out proof), exceptions list with resolutions, preparer/date, space for reviewer sign-off. Use the firm-approved secure client-data location. If none is configured, ask before creating a path beside a checkout. Confirm the selected path is outside every version-control checkout, not merely ignored by one; do not change `.gitignore`, output locations or repository configuration without explicit approval.

Run it by its full path, the extracted `australian-tax-calculators` or `<tool-dir>/bin/australian-tax-calculators` (`.exe` on Windows), never one found on `PATH`, as other builds also report 1.2.0; check that `--version` prints exactly `australian-tax-calculators 1.2.0`, and pass `--refusal-json` after the command so a refusal (exit 2) prints its code. Use this local executable; a remote calculator is used only where the user has explicitly permitted that call for this question, on fabricated or firm-approved inputs, and never as a fallback when a local tool refuses. Send `{}` to its `worksheet-catalogue` command for each worksheet's scope statement and its required and optional inputs. Before running a command, send its worksheet name, the command's name with underscores, to its `periods` command, such as `{"kind": "wine_equalisation_tax"}`, and confirm it supports the reporting month or income year in question rather than merely accepting the year; if it does not, say so and follow the written method. Each command reads one JSON object on stdin, without the `kind` field that only `periods` takes, with amounts as decimal strings and a `scope_confirmed` flag: set it only once each condition in the scope statement the command returns is evidenced, and where one is not, follow the written method for that item. Send only amounts, dates and the established facts the command takes, never a client's name, address, TFN, ABN or other identifier. Record what it consumed: the release, the engine version, the exact input, the output with the scope statement, sources and source-check date it returns, and the run date. Record a refusal and its code as an open item, and do not change the facts to get past it. Keep the calculator's own labels as its own, reported beside the facts it was given, never as the reviewer's decision on a position. A calculator's figure checks the written method, which is always completed: where they differ, record the difference for the reviewer and adopt neither. Without the executable, follow the written method.

`gst` works out the GST on one ordinary taxable supply the operator has already classified, from an amount and whether it includes GST. Mixed supplies, exemptions, margin schemes, adjustments, tax invoice rounding across line items, input tax credit entitlement and BAS preparation are outside it.

`wine-equalisation-tax` works out the wine tax on one tax period's taxable dealings in wine in a financial year from 2023-24 to 2027-28, by an entity registered or required to be registered for GST, and the producer rebate on them, for the WET labels. It refuses a year outside that range, the average wholesale price method for wine that is not grape wine or that lacks what the method needs, and a rebate on an application to own use of wine purchased under quote. Deciding what a dealing is, whether the wine is assessable wine or grape wine, the price, quoting entitlements, the rebate conditions and associated producers are outside it, as are importations and other customs dealings.

`luxury-car-tax` works out the luxury car tax on one tax period's taxable supplies and taxable importations of luxury cars in a financial year from 2019-20 to 2026-27, by an entity registered or required to be registered for GST, with its price and change of use adjustments and the primary producer and tourism operator refunds. It gives labels 1E and 1F for supplies and adjustments, and shows tax on importations apart, as it is paid to the Department of Home Affairs; a refund is claimed on the approved form, not the BAS. It refuses a year outside that range, a supply year outside its table, a refund for a car supplied before 1 July 2019 and a second primary producer refund. Deciding whether a vehicle is a car or a luxury car, quoting, the value of a car supplied with other goods or of a demonstrator, refund eligibility, bad debts and attribution of a line to a tax period stay with the step above.

`gst-creditable-purpose-change`, `gst-annual-apportionment` and `gst-financial-acquisitions-threshold` work an adjustment for a change in creditable purpose with its adjustment periods, annual apportionment eligibility with the credit and its later adjustment, and the financial acquisitions threshold tests, each for 2020-21 to 2026-27. Amongst the cases their refusal codes name, they refuse a car or a reduced credit acquisition under annual apportionment, an election status not settled, an acquisition that is not partly creditable, a change in purpose without its basis, an unsettled last adjustment period, an invalid period end, an unsupported tax period and conflicting facts.

`gst-going-concern`, `gst-second-hand-goods` and `gst-bad-debt` test whether a supply of a going concern is GST-free, work the credit for second-hand goods and work a bad debt adjustment, each for 2020-21 to 2026-27. They refuse farm land and a part of the enterprise outside the arrangement for a going concern; goods divided for resupply, goods leased before sale and valuable metal for second-hand goods; and, for a bad debt, a changed accounting basis, annual apportionment, a reduced credit acquisition, a special rule and invalid or conflicting facts. For each of these 6 commands, whether a supply or adjustment arises, its classification and the tax period it belongs to stay with the workflow above, and the BAS labels come from the supported workpapers, never from the command.

`no-abn-withholding` works out the amount to withhold from a payment made from 1 July 2024 to 30 June 2027 for a supply made in carrying on an enterprise in Australia where the supplier quoted no ABN, and nil where the payments for the supply do not exceed the threshold, excluding GST; the operator establishes that the rule applies and that none of its exceptions does. `voluntary-agreement-withholding` works out the withholding from a payment in the same period under a voluntary agreement, and refuses an agreement not in force, a payee whose ABN is not in force or not quoted in the agreement, and an agreement applying the payee's instalment rate with no rate given. Both leave out a Commissioner's variation and 2021-22 to 2023-24. Whether a payment is subject to either, and the label it belongs to, stay with the payroll and payables records, never with the command.

A person can compare a result with the site's [GST calculator](https://duguid.com.au/tools/business-calculators/gst/) page, which runs in the browser; it is not a substitute for the executable and is not used when the executable refuses.

For the WET and LCT labels, the site's [wine equalisation tax calculator](https://duguid.com.au/tools/business-calculators/wine-equalisation-tax/) and [luxury car tax calculator](https://duguid.com.au/tools/business-calculators/luxury-car-tax/) (each published and last checked 22 November 2026, read 22 November 2026) cross-check the commands above. Both run in the browser and send no inputs to a server. The wine page works a tax period's dealings in a financial year from 2023-24 to 2027-28 and the producer rebate within the year's cap, for labels 1C and 1D; it refuses earlier years, the average wholesale price method for wine that is not grape wine or without the required share of wholesale sales, and a rebate on own use of wine bought under quote, and it flags rebates claimed above the cap without working out the excess. It does not cover importations, wine tax credits other than the producer rebate, whether producers are associated or an arm's length price. The luxury car page works a tax period in a financial year from 2019-20 to 2026-27, with price and change of use adjustments, for labels 1E and 1F, and shows tax on importations and primary producer and tourism operator refunds apart; it refuses a tax period outside that range, a supply year outside 2012-13 to the tax period's and a second primary producer refund in the period, and cannot see a primary producer refund in an earlier period of the same year. It does not decide whether a vehicle is a car or a luxury car, quoting or refund eligibility, and does not work bad debt adjustments. Neither page decides which tax period a dealing or line belongs to. Before relying on either, confirm it supports the reporting month in question; use it as a cross-check only, never as a fallback when a local tool refuses. Enter only amounts, years and the established facts each page asks for, never the client's name or ABN. Record what it consumed: the page, the date run, the inputs and the result. Keep the page's own labels as its own, beside those inputs. Take labels 1C, 1D, 1E and 1F from the supported workpapers, never from the page.

For a 2026-27 lodgment date, the site's [BAS due dates page](https://duguid.com.au/tools/bas-due-dates/) (read 23 November 2026) lists the monthly and quarterly due dates with the online and registered agent concessions, each checked at the ATO on 15 November 2026. Before relying on it, confirm it supports the reporting month in question; use it as a cross-check of the due date only, never as a fallback when a local tool refuses. The ATO does not say which state's public holidays apply, the page does not decide whether a client qualifies for a concession, and the date on the activity statement governs. Record what it consumed: the page, the date read and the date taken. Keep the page's own labels as its own.

## Checks before handing over

- 1A − 1B ties to GST account movement (documented), either directly or via the cash-basis bridge per step 4
- The entity's STP status, withholding category and actual statement labels are recorded; W1 is not invented or required where current ATO guidance says the employer need not report it (at time of writing that relief applies to large withholders reporting through STP, not to every STP reporter; verify at ato.gov.au)
- Where W1 is present and required, it agrees to payroll gross less pre-tax salary sacrifice for the period, plus any voluntary-agreement payments
- Where W2 is required, it agrees to payroll reports for the period, plus withholding on those voluntary-agreement payments
- Where the statement shows ATO PAYG withholding pre-fill, each difference between the pre-filled and payroll-derived W1 and W2 is listed with its cause or as unclassified, and no payroll-derived amount was replaced by the pre-fill
- Where W3 or W4 is required, it agrees to the applicable share of the PAYG withholding payable account's non-payroll withholding, excluding any voluntary-agreement withholding already counted in W2
- Where W2, W3, W4 and W5 are carried on the form, W2 + W3 + W4 ties to total withholding for the period and to W5; record any special reporting rule that changes that label set
- Where payroll is in scope, the payroll tax screen compares total Australian wages (group wages where grouped) with each paying jurisdiction's monthly and annual thresholds and any weekly amount its registration page sets, records each threshold's source and difference, and lists any jurisdiction where the total exceeds a threshold with no registration recorded, and separately any Queensland group member paying or liable to pay Queensland wages with no registration recorded, or is reported as `not run` with what is missing
- Where the statement carries an instalment section, the reported figure agrees to the ATO-advised amount or to the documented rate-times-instalment-income calculation per step 3, with the current label set verified and any variation supported
- Each flagged movement, or the part of one, with no supported explanation appears once as a question on the step 6 draft client question list, drafted and not sent, with no GST treatment stated
- Basis confirmed and stated on the workpaper
- Every exception either resolved or explicitly carried to the reviewer
- Each broker or courier import GST amount coded to cost is listed with its import declaration and importer evidence, its tax period and any exclusion applied (deferred GST, another importer or a recharge that is not import GST); none is reclassified without that evidence
- Where the entity defers GST on imports, label 7A reconciles to the import declarations and 1B carries only the credit the evidence establishes, with any unestablished portion carried to the reviewer
- Where in scope, prior-period drift is listed against the lodged figures with a decision pending for each difference, and the proposed closing journal agrees to what was lodged, with the ATO control account checked against the ATO statement
- For transaction GST reviews, source-row counts, detail totals and cross-view relationships are accounted for; unparsed rows and population gaps prevent a complete-review claim
- Each lead screen is reported as run, with its hit count, or as `not run` with the missing field; no hit is reported as an error without its review-unit assessment
- Every reviewed detail occurrence has separate treatment, entitlement and attribution findings, with evidence, applicable authority and an action for each unresolved dependency

## Worked example

Fabricated from scratch for an invented entity, in AUD. It shows the shape of the result; no figure here is a rate, threshold or benchmark.

The entity's registration and the Activity Statement report are both on the accruals basis. The GST amounts are taken from that report and the ledger, not worked out from a rate.

| Tie-out | Working | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net GST per BAS | 9,400.00 - 3,150.00 = 6,250.00 (1A, 1B) | Proposed net GST |
| GST control account | 3,200.00 + 9,400.00 - 3,150.00 - 3,200.00 = 6,250.00 (opening, GST on sales, GST on purchases, prior BAS paid) | Agrees to net GST to the cent |
