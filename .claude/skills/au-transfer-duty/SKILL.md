# State transfer duty review pack

## Inputs

Jurisdiction, transaction and agreement dates, contracts and any associated agreements, asset and ownership details (each asset's class and location, with the consideration or value allocated to it), consideration and valuations, beneficial ownership changes, trust/company structure, purchaser status and concession evidence.

## Workflow

1. Identify the relevant jurisdiction and transaction category before looking up duty. Flag indirect acquisitions, trusts and restructures for specialist classification.

   List every asset class the transaction and any associated agreement transfer, with each asset's location and the consideration or value allocated to it: land, fixtures, leases and other interests in land, goods (plant and equipment, furniture, stock-in-trade, livestock, registered vehicles) and other business assets (goodwill, intellectual property, business licences, debts). For each class, record whether the revenue office of the jurisdiction where the asset is located treats it as dutiable in this transaction, with that office's page and the date it was read. A class the office's guidance does not settle is `UNVERIFIED`, not non-dutiable. Goods that are not dutiable on their own can be dutiable when transferred with land or other dutiable property, so test the classes together, including separate agreements the office may aggregate as one arrangement.

   **Human review pending:** an agent read the revenue office pages listed under Primary-source starting point, and the Stamp Duty Act 1978 (NT), on 4 November 2026 (not a human review). Their rules for goods and other business assets differ:

   | Jurisdiction | Goods with land or other dutiable property | Goods excluded | Other business assets |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | New South Wales | Dutiable when sold or transferred under an agreement or arrangement that includes other dutiable property, such as land or a lease. Fixtures are an interest in land and always dutiable (Revenue NSW, last updated 4 November 2026) | Stock-in-trade, materials held for use in manufacture, goods under manufacture, goods held or used in connection with primary production land, livestock, registered vehicles and ships or vessels | Goodwill, intellectual property, statutory licences and debtor receivables are not dutiable |
   | Victoria | Goods in Victoria are dutiable where they are the subject of an arrangement that includes a dutiable transaction over an estate or interest in land, including goods used in connection with a business carried on or in connection with the land (s 10(1) Duties Act 2000; SRO Victoria evidentiary manual, updated 24 August 2026). Revenue Ruling DA-054 (issued 1 March 2011) says so too, but it is under review and predates the fixtures provisions of 19 June 2019. Fixtures are always dutiable property | Stock-in-trade, materials held for use in manufacture, goods under manufacture, goods held or used in connection with primary production, and livestock (s 10(1)(d)) | Revenue Ruling DA-029 says the Duties Act 2000 does not charge duty separately on the sale of a business, though the business may affect the land's value. DA-054 says intangible items such as licences and unbundled water entitlements are not dutiable as goods |
   | Queensland | A chattel in Queensland is dutiable only if the transaction includes another type of dutiable property or is aggregated with a transaction for one (Queensland Revenue Office definition, issued 8 January 2025) | None listed on the pages read. Personal property in Queensland, such as trading stock or plant and equipment, is itself a business asset | Business assets are dutiable property: goodwill, a statutory business licence or a right to use one, a business name, a right under a franchise arrangement, a debt where the debtor lives in Queensland, a supply right, intellectual property and personal property in Queensland. An agreement solely for a debt, a supply right or intellectual or personal property of a business, with no other property, may not be dutiable (s 37 Duties Act 2001; page last updated 27 September 2024) |
   | Western Australia | Chattels such as plant and equipment are dutiable when transferred with other dutiable property, including business assets (RevenueWA fact sheet, last updated 2 June 2026) | Stock-in-trade, chattels held for use in or under manufacture, chattels held or used in a primary production business, livestock, vehicles within vehicle licence duty, and ships or vessels. A thing fixed to land is land, with listed exceptions | WA business assets are dutiable: goodwill, a restraint of trade arrangement, a business identity, a business licence, a right under an uncompleted supply contract, intellectual property, and rent rolls and client lists, but not a trade debt. Intellectual property alone is not dutiable unless aggregated with another WA business asset or chattel |
   | South Australia | Not dutiable. Only a conveyance of land is dutiable, and land includes interests in land such as a lease, fixtures and items fixed to land (RevenueSA Information Circular 99, issued 10 May 2017, status current). Duty on goods with a significant connection to transferred land applied only to transactions from 18 June 2015 to 30 June 2016 | Not applicable | Plant and equipment that is not fixed, goodwill, intellectual property, receivables, and statutory leases and licences are not dutiable |
   | Tasmania | Not dutiable by themselves, but dutiable when transferred in an arrangement that includes other dutiable property, such as a freehold interest in land, a mineral tenement or a lease of the business premises, including a new lease the sale is conditional on (SRO Tasmania; no update date shown) | Stock in trade, materials held for use in manufacture, goods under manufacture, livestock and registered motor vehicles (SRO Tasmania business assets guideline) | Business goodwill (but not site goodwill) and intellectual property are not dutiable; the guideline lists them with the excluded goods as the most common non-dutiable assets |
   | Australian Capital Territory | Dutiable only if the subject of an arrangement that includes a dutiable transaction over other dutiable property, other than intellectual property, such as land, a Crown lease or a commercial lease with a premium. Fixtures are not goods and are dutiable as land (ACT Revenue Office circular DAA011.3, effective 18 September 2017, status current) | Stock-in-trade, materials held for use in manufacture, goods under manufacture, goods held or used in connection with primary production land, livestock, a registered motor vehicle, and a ship or vessel | Not addressed by the circular read, so `UNVERIFIED` |
   | Northern Territory | Plant and equipment is dutiable only if acquired with other dutiable property, and chattels acquired with land or a lease of land are dutiable except where that lease has nil or nominal value (NT Government overview linked from the Territory Revenue Office page; no update date shown). Under s 116 of the Stamp Duty Act 1978 (as in force at 1 July 2025), the provisions in force immediately before 9 May 2023 still apply to a conveyance first executed before that date, to one replacing an earlier conveyance, first executed before that date, of the same or substantially similar property, and to one whose property, or substantially similar property, was the subject of an agreement or option to purchase entered into or granted before that date, or of an option granted before that date to require another person to purchase it (s 116(1)(a) to (d)) | Stock-in-trade, materials held for manufacture, goods under manufacture, livestock, and motor vehicles to be registered or transferred | For a conveyance first executed on or after 9 May 2023 that s 116 does not cover, intangible property, such as goodwill, business names, trademarks, registered designs, copyright and patents, is not dutiable; certain statutory licences relating to mining are. For a conveyance s 116 covers, dutiable property as defined in s 4 immediately before 9 May 2023 also included, in paragraphs (b) to (ga), the goodwill of a business undertaking carried on or to be carried on in the Territory, a right to use there a business name, trading name or trade mark used in connection with it, a patent, a registered design or a copyright, and a statutory licence or permission used in connection with a business undertaking |

   The NT overview, at [nt.gov.au](https://nt.gov.au/employ/money-and-taxes/taxes-royalties-and-grants/stamp-duty/examples-of-duty-and-rates), is not indexed in `sources.json` because its host is outside the reviewed fetch list. Confirm each row against the current page and the jurisdiction's Act for the transaction date before relying on it. A row decides no liability, value, exemption or concession.

2. Reconcile consideration and valuation evidence and identify the dutiable-value rule from the jurisdiction's authority.

3. Test concessions, exemptions and foreign-purchaser surcharges separately with their documentary and timing conditions.

4. Prepare a conditional calculation using verified period-specific rules and reconcile it to any assessment and settlement provision. NSW references apply only to NSW; obtain local authority for other jurisdictions.

5. For a calculator comparison, match property type (including vacant land or off-the-plan), purchaser category, relevant transaction date and concession conditions. Check each applicable bracket, concession taper, cap and surcharge boundary, including treatment at the endpoint and rounding. Separate transfer duty from registration, title, mortgage and other settlement costs; a duty-only total is not the total cash needed to settle.

   If a calculator is used or compared, prefer a local one and keep its evidence. Before relying on it, confirm it supports the reporting month or income year in question rather than merely accepting the year. Send only figures, never client names or identifiers. Record what it consumed: the tool and its version, revision or URL, the period, the exact inputs, any scope statement it returned and the run date. A remote calculator is used only where the user has explicitly permitted that call for this question, and never as a fallback when a local tool refuses. Keep the calculator's own labels as its own: report its result beside its inputs, never as a compliance verdict or in place of the reviewer's decision.

## Hand-off and checks

A transaction/evidence matrix and assessment comparison. The conveyancer or lawyer decides liability and performs declarations, assessment submissions and payment.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Primary-source starting point

One revenue office per state and territory. Step 1 records what each page said when an agent read it.

- New South Wales: [Revenue NSW: What is transfer duty](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/taxes-duties-levies-royalties/transfer-duty/understanding-transfer-duty/what-is-transfer-duty) and [Transfer duty on business purchases](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/taxes-duties-levies-royalties/transfer-duty/understanding-transfer-duty/business-purchases)
- Victoria: [SRO Victoria: Transfer of land and business and/or goods](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/buying-property/land-transfer-stamp-duty/managing-duty-transactions/evidentiary-requirements-manual/transfer-land-and-business-andor-goods) , [Revenue Ruling DA-054](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/about-us/laws-legal-cases-and-rulings/public-rulings/goods-held-or-used-connection-with-land) and [Revenue Ruling DA-029](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/about-us/laws-legal-cases-and-rulings/public-rulings/transfers-land-and-business)
- Queensland: [Queensland Revenue Office: Dutiable property](https://qro.qld.gov.au/definition/dutiable-property/) and [Assessing if business asset transfers are dutiable](https://qro.qld.gov.au/duties/investors/business/assessing/)
- Western Australia: [RevenueWA: Duties Fact Sheet - Business Assets](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/duties-fact-sheet-business-assets)
- South Australia: [RevenueSA Information Circular 99](https://revenuesa.sa.gov.au/tools-and-resources/resources/publications/information-circulars/information-circulars/ic_099)
- Tasmania: [SRO Tasmania: Sales of business](https://www.sro.tas.gov.au/property-transfer-duties/dutiable-transactions/sales-of-business) and [Duty payable on the purchase of business assets guideline](https://www.sro.tas.gov.au/Documents/duty-payable-purchase-business-assets-guideline.pdf)
- Australian Capital Territory: [ACT Revenue Office Revenue Circular DAA011.3](https://www.revenue.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/2945597/DAA011.3-dutiable-goods.pdf)
- Northern Territory: [Territory Revenue Office: Stamp duty](https://treasury.nt.gov.au/dtf/territory-revenue-office/stamp-duty)

Open the relevant current or historical version for the work's actual period. This starting point is not a complete statement of the law.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A related-party transfer has nominal consideration and no valuation. Do not calculate duty solely on the nominal price.
