# AML/CTF customer due diligence and reliance file

## Inputs

The client code and customer type (individual, sole trader, body corporate, partnership, unincorporated association, trust or government body); the designated services the practice's assessment lists for this client, with the date each is to start; whether the practice's AML/CTF records treat the client as a pre-commencement customer, and the evidence; the KYC information collected so far and a verification evidence reference for each item, held in the practice's AML/CTF system; the client's ML/TF risk rating, who assigned it and the information it rests on; the politically exposed person and targeted financial sanctions screening dates for the client and each person the file lists; the practice's ML/TF risk assessment and AML/CTF policies, including those for delayed customer due diligence, reliance and any continued customer identification procedure; for reliance, who the other person is, the evidence that it is a reporting entity or a person regulated under foreign laws giving effect to the FATF Recommendations, the KYC information it passed on, any written agreement or arrangement with its senior manager approval and assessment records, and any record of the practice's reasons; for a client taken over with a practice, the sale or transfer record and the copies of the prior practice's records received; and the name of the AML/CTF compliance officer.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the file is needed and record the dates it rests on. Where the practice's assessment does not mark a service for this client as designated, stop and route the service to `au-aml-ctf-designated-services`; do not decide whether a service is designated. **Human review pending:** an agent read the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006* (AML/CTF Act) at compilation 63 (C2026C00447, in force from 1 October 2026), the AML/CTF Rules at compilation 1 (F2026C00274, in force from 31 March 2026), the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Transitional Rules 2026* at compilation 1 (F2026C00452) and the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Amendment Act 2024* on 19 November 2026, not a human review. Schedule 2 to that Amendment Act, which amended the customer due diligence provisions, commenced on 31 March 2026, and item 11 of its Schedule 3 delays Part 2 (customer due diligence) and Divisions 2 to 6 of Part 10 (record keeping) of the AML/CTF Act until 1 July 2026 for a practice in its capacity as a provider of a table 6 service. Section 28(1) of the AML/CTF Act says a reporting entity must not commence to provide a designated service to a customer until it has established on reasonable grounds each matter in s 28(2). Record the planned start date of each service and whether it has already started.

   Record what is settled and what is left to the Rules. As read on 19 November 2026, the register listed no later compilation of the Act or the AML/CTF Rules, and AUSTRAC's consultations page (last updated 24 March 2026) listed no open consultation; its last consultation on the AML/CTF Rules closed on 20 February 2026. This skill cites no draft instrument. The Act still leaves to the AML/CTF Rules any further matter to establish (s 28(2)(g) of the AML/CTF Act), the circumstances and period for delayed due diligence (s 29(a), s 29(c)(ii) and s 29(f) of the AML/CTF Act) and the requirements for reliance (ss 37A(1)(b), 37A(2)(e), 37B(1) and 38(e) of the AML/CTF Act), so open the Rules in force when the file is prepared.

   For a pre-commencement customer, s 36(1)(b) of the AML/CTF Act covers a business relationship that involved only designated services in item 2 of table 2, table 5 or table 6 as at the start of 1 July 2026. Section 36(3) of the AML/CTF Act disapplies s 28(1) of the AML/CTF Act for that customer, and s 36(4) of the AML/CTF Act applies it again if a suspicious matter reporting obligation arises or a significant change in the nature and purpose of the relationship makes the client's ML/TF risk medium or high. Record the status, its evidence and any change the records show, route the client to `au-aml-ctf-client-coverage` for its ongoing review, and prepare this file only where s 28(1) of the AML/CTF Act applies or the AML/CTF compliance officer asks for it. Whether the client is a pre-commencement customer is for that officer.

2. Build one row for each matter in s 28(2) of the AML/CTF Act: (a) the identity of the customer; (b) the identity of any person on whose behalf the customer is receiving the service; (c) the identity of any person acting on behalf of the customer and their authority to act; (d) for a customer that is not an individual, the identity of any beneficial owners; (e) whether the customer, any beneficial owner, any person on whose behalf it receives the service or any person acting for it is a politically exposed person or a person designated for targeted financial sanctions; (f) the nature and purpose of the business relationship or occasional transaction; and (g) any other matter the AML/CTF Rules specify. For each row record the KYC information collected, its verification evidence reference, the provision of the AML/CTF Rules relied on and a status: `on the record`, `missing` or `for the AML/CTF compliance officer`. Never mark a matter as established; whether it is established on reasonable grounds is for that officer.

   Check the minimum KYC information by customer type, from the AML/CTF Rules read on 19 November 2026. For an individual not in business, Division 1 of Part 6 of the AML/CTF Rules sets no minimum list: record the KYC information the practice's policies require and the reasonable steps under s 28(3)(a) of the AML/CTF Act to establish that the client is who they claim to be. For a sole trader, s 6-1 of the AML/CTF Rules requires the full name, any business name, any other names commonly used, a unique identifier for the business or, if none has been given, any unique identifier given to the customer, the principal place of business and information about the nature of the business. For a body corporate, partnership or unincorporated association, s 6-2 of the AML/CTF Rules requires the full name, any business and other names, any unique identifier and the principal place of business or operations, and adds any registered office, evidence of the customer's existence, information about the powers that bind and govern it, the full name and any director identification number of each individual with primary responsibility for its governance and executive decisions, information about its ownership and control structure and information about the nature of its business or operations. For a trust, s 6-3 of the AML/CTF Rules requires its full name, the kind of trust, any business and other names, any unique identifier, the principal place of business or operations, evidence of its existence, the powers that govern it, each individual with primary responsibility for its governance, each beneficiary's identity or, where the trust's nature does not allow that, a description of each class of beneficiary, the trustees' identity, its control structure, the identity of any settlor, appointor, guardian or protector and information about the nature of its business or operations. For a government body, s 6-4 of the AML/CTF Rules applies. Where a person acting on behalf of the customer, or on whose behalf it receives the service, is not an individual, s 6-5 of the AML/CTF Rules requires at least the identity information in s 6-2(2), s 6-3(2) or s 6-4(2) that would apply to it as a customer, not the rest of those sections. List each item missing for the customer's type as a missing element. Where a row records a business name, ABN, ACN, ARBN or registered office from a public register, record the dated search as the 'Register lookups' section of `tax-invoice-review` describes; whether a register result is enough evidence under the practice's policies is for the AML/CTF compliance officer.

   Test the beneficial owners owner by owner with step 3 of `au-aml-ctf-client-coverage`, which records any basis under s 6-7, s 6-8, s 6-17 or s 6-18 of the AML/CTF Rules and the trust roles by position; do not repeat that test here with a weaker one. Where the file relies on another provision that treats a matter as established, such as s 6-6 (persons on whose behalf the service is received), s 6-10 (an individual who cannot provide satisfactory evidence) or s 6-11 (previous compliance in a foreign country) of the AML/CTF Rules, record the provision, each of its conditions and the evidence for each, and route it to the AML/CTF compliance officer; do not decide whether it applies.

3. Record the ML/TF risk and the level of due diligence it calls for. Section 28(3)(b) of the AML/CTF Act requires the practice itself to identify the client's ML/TF risk from KYC information reasonably available before the service starts, and s 28(4) lists what it must take into account, including the practice's ML/TF risk assessment, the kind of customer, the services, the delivery channels and the countries involved. Record the rating, who assigned it, the date and the information it rests on; where no rating is recorded, list it as missing and do not assign or estimate one. Simplified measures are available only where the risk is low, s 32 of the AML/CTF Act does not apply and the AML/CTF Rules' requirements are met (s 31 of the AML/CTF Act); s 6-16 of the AML/CTF Rules requires the practice's AML/CTF policies to deal with applying them, so record where they do. List each circumstance in s 32 of the AML/CTF Act that the records raise, such as a high risk rating, a foreign politically exposed person, a person in a high risk jurisdiction for which the FATF has called for enhanced due diligence, or a request for services with no apparent economic or legal purpose or involving unusually complex or large transactions or an unusual pattern of transactions (s 6-20 of the AML/CTF Rules), as a question for the AML/CTF compliance officer on enhanced due diligence. Where enhanced due diligence would apply because of s 32(a), (b) or (d) of the AML/CTF Act, s 6-21 of the AML/CTF Rules adds the source of the client's wealth and funds as matters to establish where relevant to the nature of its ML/TF risk: record whether each is on the record. Where the records show that the client, a beneficial owner or a person on whose behalf it receives the service (not a person acting only on its behalf) is a foreign politically exposed person, or a domestic or international organisation politically exposed person with a high risk rating, s 6-23 of the AML/CTF Rules adds the source of that person's wealth and funds as matters to establish, with s 6-23(3) treating some foreign politically exposed persons as domestic, and s 5-5(1) of the AML/CTF Rules requires the practice's policies to obtain a senior manager's approval before the service starts: record whether each is on the record. Whether a person is a politically exposed person, and the rating, are for that officer.

4. Where a service started, or is to start, before every row is on the record, test delayed due diligence. Section 29 of the AML/CTF Act permits it only where the AML/CTF Rules specify the circumstances, the practice determines on reasonable grounds that starting first is essential to avoid interrupting the ordinary course of business and that any additional risk is low, it has policies to complete the due diligence as soon as reasonably practicable and within any period the Rules set, it implements policies to mitigate and manage the associated risks, and it meets any requirement the Rules specify. For a service provided at or through a permanent establishment in Australia, s 6-12 of the AML/CTF Rules requires, before the service starts, reasonable steps to establish that an individual customer is who they claim to be, the ML/TF risk identified, KYC information collected on every matter in s 28(2) of the AML/CTF Act appropriate to that risk (other than the source of wealth and funds in s 6-23(2) of the AML/CTF Rules) and the matters in s 28(2)(a) and (c) of the AML/CTF Act established; it bars transferring, or allowing or facilitating the transfer of, money, property or virtual assets for the customer, or otherwise making them available to it other than by holding them on deposit, before s 28(1) of the AML/CTF Act is complied with; and it sets the period as ending 20 business days after the service starts. Record each condition as `on the record`, `missing` or `for the AML/CTF compliance officer`, the service start date, and the 20 business day date only where the practice supplies the business days that apply; otherwise record that date as `UNVERIFIED`. AUSTRAC's [delayed initial customer due diligence](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/your-amlctf-program/customer-due-diligence/initial-customer-due-diligence/delayed-initial-customer-due-diligence) guidance (last updated 27 October 2026) says inconvenience to the practice or the customer is not a sufficient reason to delay; record that as the regulator's view, not the law. Where s 6-13 (opening an account and deposit), s 6-14 (certain financial market transactions) or s 6-32 (real estate transactions) of the AML/CTF Rules applies, that section has its own conditions and any period it specifies: record it for that officer and do not apply s 6-12 to it.

   Where the practice's policies use a customer identification procedure under the AML/CTF Rules as in force on 30 March 2026, s 7 of the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Transitional Rules 2026* treats those policies as meeting s 26F(3)(b) of the AML/CTF Act where, amongst other conditions, they identify each class of customer the procedure applies to and the date from which it will no longer be applied, and that section ceases to have effect on 31 March 2029. For a client in such a class, s 7(4) of the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Transitional Rules 2026* disapplies the AML/CTF Rules made for s 28(6) and s 29 of the AML/CTF Act, so s 6-12 of the AML/CTF Rules does not set the conditions or the 20 business day period. Section 8(2) of the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Transitional Rules 2026* permits starting before the procedure only where s 33 of the AML/CTF Act as in force on 30 March 2026 would have. Where the service started before the procedure, s 8(3) of the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Transitional Rules 2026* stops s 8(1) of the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Transitional Rules 2026* treating the matters as established unless the procedure was then carried out as s 34 of the AML/CTF Act as in force on 30 March 2026 required: record the date it was carried out and the evidence. Record the class the policies place this client in, the procedure used and the end date they give; whether the practice may use s 7 of the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Transitional Rules 2026* at all, and whether s 8 of the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Transitional Rules 2026* treats the matters as established is for the AML/CTF compliance officer.

5. Where the file relies on KYC information another person collected or verified, record which route the practice uses, because each has different conditions, and record each condition as `on the record`, `missing` or `for the AML/CTF compliance officer`. Reliance under s 37A or s 38 of the AML/CTF Act treats the practice as having complied with s 28(3)(c) or (d), the collection and verification of KYC information, and Part 10 is excluded from that effect. Record that the practice still identifies the client's ML/TF risk itself (s 28(3)(b) of the AML/CTF Act), applies enhanced due diligence where s 32 requires it and keeps its own records; AUSTRAC's [guidance on reliance case by case](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/your-amlctf-program/customer-due-diligence/reliance-customer-identification-third-party/reliance-case-case-basis) (last updated 12 October 2026) and its guidance on reliance arrangements (below) say the other person's ML/TF risk assessment and AML/CTF policies may not match the practice's own.
   - Agent or outsourced provider: s 37 of the AML/CTF Act lets an agent collect and verify KYC information for the practice, which remains liable. AUSTRAC's [overview of reliance](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/your-amlctf-program/customer-due-diligence/reliance-customer-identification-third-party/overview-reliance-customer-identification-third-party) (last updated 13 October 2026) says a KYC or outsourced service provider is not a third party for reliance, and its [outsourcing guidance](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/additional-guidance/using-outsourcing-help-meet-your-obligations) (last updated 13 July 2026) says the practice remains responsible for its obligations. Record a provider's report as collected KYC information under the practice's own due diligence, never as reliance.
   - Agreement or arrangement (s 37A of the AML/CTF Act): record the written agreement or arrangement and that it is in force (s 37A(2)(a) of the AML/CTF Act); the practice's record of its reasonable grounds, when it entered into the arrangement, to believe the requirements of the AML/CTF Rules were met (s 37A(1)(b) of the AML/CTF Act); that a senior manager approved it before the practice entered into it (s 5-5(1)(f) of the AML/CTF Rules); and, for s 6-29(1) of the AML/CTF Rules, that the other person is a reporting entity or a person regulated under foreign laws giving effect to the FATF Recommendations on customer due diligence and record keeping, that it has measures to comply with those obligations, that the arrangement is appropriate to the practice's ML/TF risks having regard to the matters in s 6-29(2) of the AML/CTF Rules, that it lets the practice obtain all the KYC information collected before the service starts or within the period under s 29 of the AML/CTF Act, that it lets the practice obtain copies of the verification data immediately or as soon as practicable on request, and that it documents each party's responsibilities, including record keeping. For this client, record that the practice obtained the client's identity information from the other person under the arrangement (s 37A(2)(d) of the AML/CTF Act). Record each assessment under s 37B of the AML/CTF Act: s 6-30 of the AML/CTF Rules sets intervals that take the practice's risks into account and are not more than 2 years, and a further assessment on a significant change in circumstances, and s 37B(1)(c) of the AML/CTF Act requires a written record within 10 business days after completing each one. Where the last assessment did not find reasonable grounds that the requirements are met, s 37A(3) of the AML/CTF Act removes the reliance for procedures carried out afterwards, until s 37A(4) restores it: record the date and list as missing the client's KYC information the other person collected or verified after it. AUSTRAC's [guidance on reliance arrangements](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/your-amlctf-program/customer-due-diligence/reliance-customer-identification-third-party/reliance-under-customer-due-diligence-arrangements) (last updated 18 November 2026) says it would not expect delays beyond one business day in providing information after a request; record that as the regulator's view.
   - Case by case (s 38 of the AML/CTF Act): record the evidence that the other person complied with s 28(3)(c) or (d) of the AML/CTF Act for the client (s 38(b) of the AML/CTF Act), not only that it collected or verified KYC information, that the practice obtained from it the client's identity information collected in doing so, and, for s 6-31 of the AML/CTF Rules, the other person's status and compliance measures, the reasons reliance is appropriate for this service (s 38(d) of the AML/CTF Act) and to the practice's ML/TF risks having regard to the other person's business, services, customers, delivery channels and countries, the grounds for believing the practice can obtain all the KYC information collected before the service starts or within the period under s 29 of the AML/CTF Act, the grounds for believing it can obtain copies of the verification data immediately or as soon as practicable on request, and the practice's documented reasons that these requirements are met (s 6-31(e) of the AML/CTF Rules). AUSTRAC enrolment of the other person shows its status only, not the other conditions. Where any reason is not documented, list it as missing.
   - Client taken over with a practice: s 6-27 of the AML/CTF Rules treats the matters in s 28(2) of the AML/CTF Act as established where the client became a client because another reporting entity transferred all or part of its business to this practice and this practice obtained copies of the records the prior entity kept under ss 107, 108, 111 and 114 of the AML/CTF Act. Record the transfer record and which of those copies were received; ongoing due diligence under s 30 of the AML/CTF Act still applies. Where the client was a pre-commencement customer of the prior entity, s 6-28 of the AML/CTF Rules sets conditions for ongoing due diligence on the same copies: record that status and its evidence. Where a copy was not received or the prior entity kept no record under one of those sections, list it as missing; whether s 6-27 of the AML/CTF Rules applies is for the AML/CTF compliance officer.

   For a foreign other person, record the country and any assessment the practice made of it; AUSTRAC's [guidance on foreign jurisdictions](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/your-amlctf-program/customer-due-diligence/reliance-customer-identification-third-party/managing-risk-and-assessing-foreign-jurisdictions) (last updated 20 October 2026) says reliance is generally not appropriate on a person in a country assessed as non-compliant with FATF Recommendations 10, 11 or 12, or publicly listed by the FATF as a high-risk jurisdiction or under increased monitoring for concerns about customer due diligence, record keeping or politically exposed persons; record that as the regulator's view. Whether reliance is appropriate, and whether to enter, continue, suspend or end an arrangement, are for the AML/CTF compliance officer and a senior manager.

6. List the records the file needs, by type, without copying their content. Section 111(3) of the AML/CTF Act requires records of the type and content of the data collected for s 28 or s 30 and of any risk analysis, assessment or decision. Where reliance applies, s 114 of the AML/CTF Act requires a copy of any record of the procedure the other person gave the practice to be kept until the end of the first 7 year period, beginning after the reliance took effect, throughout which the practice provided no designated service to the client, and s 114A requires each s 37B assessment record to be kept for 7 years after it was prepared. Route retention dates for the client's own records to step 1 of `au-aml-ctf-client-coverage`. This is a records list, never a deletion list.

7. Do not screen the client, verify identity documents, contact the client or the other person, form a suspicion or lodge a report. If a record suggests a matter that could involve a suspicious matter report, stop, route it to the AML/CTF compliance officer and disclose it to no one else; the tipping-off offence in s 123 of the AML/CTF Act can apply.

## Hand-off and checks

A customer due diligence file checklist for one client: a row for each matter in s 28(2) of the AML/CTF Act with its evidence reference, provision and status; the risk rating and the level of due diligence the records raise; any delayed due diligence conditions and dates; for reliance, the route and a row for each condition with its status; and the records list. The file's status is `ready for the AML/CTF compliance officer` when no row is `missing` and every other row has evidence or a named question, and `incomplete` otherwise; it is never `complete`.

This skill does not decide whether a matter is established on reasonable grounds, the client's ML/TF risk rating, whether simplified or enhanced due diligence applies, whether a person is a politically exposed person or designated for targeted financial sanctions, whether delayed due diligence is permitted, whether reliance is appropriate or available, or whether to start or continue the service or enter an arrangement. Those are for the AML/CTF compliance officer or another authorised person, and a senior manager where s 5-5 of the AML/CTF Rules requires approval.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A law firm refers a new company client for a restructure and sends the director's identity details with a note that the practice can rely on its checks. The file holds the law firm's AUSTRAC enrolment, no written arrangement, no documented reasons for relying on it and no ML/TF risk rating. Record the route as case-by-case reliance under s 38 of the AML/CTF Act, list each missing reason under s 6-31 of the AML/CTF Rules and the missing risk rating, and record that the practice must still identify the client's ML/TF risk itself, rather than marking the file ready because the referrer is enrolled.
