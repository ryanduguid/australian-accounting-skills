# Share buy-back workpaper

## Inputs

For the company: whether it is a listed public company, its constitution, board minutes, the buy-back proposal, offer documents and timetable, the first date it told shareholders or the market that a buy-back was in serious contemplation, every ASIC lodgement with its date, the share register before and after, the number of shares on issue, the share capital account history with every transfer into it, the latest balance sheet, any market valuation or listed price data, any class or private ruling, solvency evidence, payment records and any loan accounts the price was set off against, the franking account roll-forward and benchmark, and the distribution statements issued. For each seller: the parcels bought back with acquisition dates and cost base records, the seller's entity type and residency, whether the shares were held on capital account, as trading stock or otherwise on revenue account, and the statement the company gave them.

## Workflow

1. **Classify the buy-back twice.** For tax, s 159GZZZK of the [*Income Tax Assessment Act 1936*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1936A00027/latest/text) makes a buy-back an on-market purchase only if the share is listed on a stock exchange and the buy-back is made in the ordinary course of trading on it; a transaction the exchange's rules describe as special is not (s 159GZZZL); every other buy-back is off-market. Record also whether the company is a listed public company, which changes the rules in steps 3, 5 and 8. For the company's procedure, s 257B(1) of the [*Corporations Act 2001*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00818/latest/text) sorts buy-backs into minimum holding, employee share scheme, on-market (s 257B(6) and (7)), equal access scheme (s 257B(2) and (3)) and selective buy-backs, and tests the 10/12 limit in s 257B(4) and (5). Record each classification with its evidence; the two can differ. Before going further, record the answers to the questions in paragraph 40 of [PS LA 2007/9](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=PSR/PS20079/NAT/ATO/00001) (a dominant shareholder buying out others, another change in underlying ownership, a sale of part of the business, a transaction between shareholders), because the ATO says such an arrangement may not be a buy-back at all.

2. **Evidence the Corporations Act steps.** Section 257A allows a buy-back only if it does not materially prejudice the company's ability to pay its creditors and the company follows Division 2 of Part 2J.1; note any restriction in the constitution. From the s 257B(1) table for the buy-back's type, list each required step with its evidence and date: an ordinary resolution where the 10/12 limit is exceeded (s 257C); for a selective buy-back, a special resolution with no votes in favour cast by a seller or their associates, or a resolution agreed to by all ordinary shareholders (s 257D); the notice of meeting and its documents lodged with ASIC before the notice goes to shareholders (ss 257C(3) and 257D(3)); offer documents lodged before the agreement (s 257E); the 14 days' notice (s 257F); disclosure with the offer (s 257G); cancellation immediately after the transfer is registered (s 257H(3)); and the notice of cancellation to ASIC within 1 month (s 254Y(1)). ASIC's [Company share buy-backs](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/companies/company-share-and-shareholder-rules-and-changes/company-share-buy-backs) page names Forms 280 and 281 and says to report the change in share structure within 28 days after the cancellation; record both periods and diary the earlier. Record the directors' solvency evidence. Do not conclude that a step was validly taken or that the company is solvent; the directors decide and the company's lawyer advises.

3. **Split the purchase price.** The purchase price is the money and the market value of any other property the seller receives (s 159GZZZM). For an on-market purchase no part is a dividend (s 159GZZZR). For an off-market purchase by a company that is not a listed public company, the price less the part debited to the share capital account is a dividend paid to the seller out of profits on the day of the buy-back (s 159GZZZP(1)), and the remainder is not a dividend (s 159GZZZP(2)). For an off-market purchase by a listed public company, no part is a dividend (ss 159GZZZP(3) and 159GZZZPA); the ATO's [Share buy-backs](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/shares-and-similar-investments/share-buy-backs) page applies this to buy-backs announced after 7.30 pm AEDT on 25 October 2022, so record the announcement date and time. Where a dividend can arise, compute the average capital per share (share capital account balance over shares on issue at the buy-back) beside the company's proposed share capital debit. PS LA 2007/9 says the ATO prefers that method unless there are exceptional circumstances (paragraphs 12, 62 and 69), describes the share capital to retained earnings ratio as an alternative (paragraph 63) and says recent capital injections may attract the anti-avoidance provisions (paragraph 62). Show each difference between the proposed debit and the average as a reviewer item. PS LA 2007/9 issued in 2007, before s 159GZZZPA; do not apply its dividend split guidance to a listed public company's off-market buy-back that the new rule covers.

4. **Evidence market value.** The seller's consideration (s 159GZZZQ(2)) and the frankable part of a dividend (s 202-45(c) of the [*Income Tax Assessment Act 1997*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A05138/latest/text)) both turn on the market value of the share at the time of the buy-back as if it had not happened and were never proposed. For listed shares, [TD 2004/22](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=TXD/TD200422/NAT/ATO/00001) sets the ATO's method: the volume weighted average price over the last 5 trading days before the first announcement, adjusted for the S&P/ASX 200 movement from that day's opening to the close of the buy-back, which may need varying for other events; PS LA 2007/9 paragraph 14 says it does not apply to an arm's length capital-only buy-back. For unlisted shares, the ATO's [guidance to Community Bank companies](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/view.htm?docid=AFS%2FCommunityBankcompanies%2F00001&PiT=99991231235958) says an unlisted company proposing an off-market buy-back needs a market valuation, and lists what to consider, including the valuer's expertise, the method and its inputs, and the first announcement date, which minutes should evidence; record each of those points for any unlisted company. Apply the market valuation standard in `au-capital-gains`; keep the value `UNVERIFIED` until the reviewer accepts it, and treat an appraisal as a screen only.

5. **Prepare the company's franking and distribution records.** Under s 202-45 the dividend part of an off-market price is unfrankable to the extent the price exceeds that market value (s 202-45(c)), as is a distribution sourced from the share capital account (s 202-45(e)), a Division 7A deemed dividend (s 202-45(g)(i)) and an amount the Commissioner determines under s 45C to be an unfranked dividend (s 202-45(h)(ii)). For an on-market buy-back, item 9 of the s 205-30 table debits the franking account as if the purchase had been off-market and the company were not listed, at its benchmark franking percentage or, without one, the percentage item 9 sets; PS LA 2007/9 paragraph 163 says there is no item 9 debit where only the share capital account is debited. Item 9A sets the debit for a listed public company's off-market buy-back on the same basis. Schedule each debit for `au-franking-account` to post, test against the benchmark and check the distribution statements; PS LA 2007/9 paragraphs 168 to 171 cover the statement's timing in a buy-back and the Commissioner's discretion to defer it, which must be requested before the distribution is made. The buy-back and cancellation themselves give the company no assessable income, deduction, capital gain or capital loss (s 159GZZZN).

6. **Record the integrity and ruling facts.** PS LA 2007/9 says ss 45A, 45B and 177EA of the 1936 Act, s 204-30 of the 1997 Act and the value shifting rules in Divisions 725 and 727 can apply to a buy-back (paragraphs 18 to 24). Record the facts each turns on (recent capital raisings, which shareholders are offered or take part, any foreign resident shareholders, the discount to market value, value shifts before the buy-back) without concluding on them. For a listed buy-back, search the ATO legal database for a class ruling under the company's name, as the ATO's Share buy-backs page suggests; for a private company, ask for any private ruling. Record the ruling's number, period, the scheme it describes and what it states on the capital and dividend components, market value and the anti-avoidance provisions, and list each difference between that scheme and the facts. A ruling covers only the scheme it describes.

7. **Screen private company and Division 7A links.** For a company that is not listed, record each loan, payment or debt forgiveness connected with the buy-back: a price set off against a shareholder's or associate's loan, a shortfall released, money the company lent to fund the purchase, any part of the price left owing to the seller, and any price above market value. The ATO's [Division 7A page](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/private-company-benefits-division-7a-dividends) says a payment, loan or debt forgiveness to a shareholder or associate can be a dividend under Division 7A, and that Division 7A does not apply to amounts assessable under other parts of the law, such as normal dividends. Route each item to `div7a-compliance` and keep any Division 7A amount off the franking schedule.

8. **Prepare each seller's position.** The ATO's Share buy-backs page says disposing of shares held on capital account to the company is a CGT event, and that its time depends on the offer's terms, such as when the company accepts the application or, for a conditional offer, when the seller accepts; take the time from the offer documents. Then, for each parcel:
   - **On-market.** The consideration is the purchase price (s 159GZZZS).
   - **Off-market.** The consideration is the purchase price (s 159GZZZQ(1)), replaced by the market value from step 4 where the price is lower (s 159GZZZQ(2)), including for a listed public company. For a company that is not a listed public company, it is then reduced by the reduction amount, broadly the dividend part included in the seller's assessable income or that is an eligible non-capital amount (s 159GZZZQ(3) to (5)); s 159GZZZQ(3A) excludes a listed public company. For a corporate seller with a loss, flag the offsetable amount rule in s 159GZZZQ(8) and (9) for the reviewer. A dividend included in assessable income is not also counted under s 118-20 (s 159GZZZP(1A)).
   - **Dividend.** PS LA 2007/9 paragraph 158 says the dividend part and its franking credit are included in assessable income and a tax offset ordinarily follows; paragraphs 71 to 88 describe the qualified person rules (the holding period, applied last in first out, and related payments) that decide the offset. Record each parcel's holding dates and any related payment for the reviewer. Route a foreign resident seller to `au-nonresident-cgt`; PS LA 2007/9 paragraph 157 deals with dividend withholding.

   Shares held as trading stock or otherwise on revenue account use the same deemed consideration for the revenue calculation (s 159GZZZQ(1)(a)); record the basis for review. Pass each parcel's consideration, cost base records and acquisition date to `au-capital-gains`, and dividend reinvestment parcels to `au-dssp`. Where every input is supported, compute each parcel's consideration, dividend, franking credit and preliminary gain or loss and show the arithmetic; otherwise leave the result blank and mark it `UNVERIFIED`.

   **Human review pending:** an agent read Division 16K in compilation C2026C00454 (compilation No. 193, 1 October 2026) and s 202-45 in compilation C2026C00471 (compilation No. 268, 1 October 2026), both also on the ATO legal database, s 205-30 on the ATO legal database, Part 2J.1 in compilation C2026C00432 (compilation No. 149, 19 September 2026), PS LA 2007/9, TD 2004/22 and the ATO and ASIC pages linked here on 11 November 2026. This is not a human review.

## Hand-off and checks

A classification record, a Corporations Act step register, a price split with the average capital per share comparison, a market value record, a franking debit and distribution schedule for `au-franking-account`, an integrity and ruling facts record, a Division 7A screen and a seller schedule by parcel. The amounts debited to share capital and to other accounts must equal the total purchase price, and for an off-market buy-back by a company that is not a listed public company the dividend part must equal the price less the share capital debit; the price per share times the shares bought back must agree with the payment records; the shares cancelled must equal the shares bought back and the register after must equal the register before less those shares; each seller's dividend and franking credit must agree with their statement. The directors decide the buy-back and its terms; the company's lawyer advises on the procedure; a registered tax agent decides each tax position.

For each unresolved item record evidence needed, owner, status and next action. Keep dependent results conditional until the item is resolved.

## Fabricated acceptance example

A private company buys back one shareholder's shares off-market, debits less to share capital than the average capital per share, has no market valuation and sets part of the price off against the shareholder's loan account. Show the split beside the average, keep the market value and the frankable part `UNVERIFIED`, route the set-off to `div7a-compliance`, list the selective buy-back approvals still to evidence, and leave each tax position to the reviewer.
