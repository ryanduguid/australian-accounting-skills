# Tasmanian home and shack with a co-owner overseas

## Scenario

All records below are fabricated from scratch. An invented individual owns a home in Hobart and a shack on the east coast. His brother, who lives overseas, bought a share of the shack. The shack's land tax notice classifies it as General Land, and the individual asks whether the home should be taxed too.

## Task

Use `au-land-tax` to prepare the Tasmanian holdings review and the unresolved-items hand-off.

## Synthetic inputs

The shack's land tax notice, received by email, and title searches for both properties are supplied. Council rates notices for both properties are supplied. No notice for the home is supplied.

## Deliberately unavailable evidence

Only the supplied facts are available. The year's taxing date, rates and thresholds, the brother's citizenship and residency, when he bought his share and the home's land classification remain missing. No legal approval or external action authority is supplied.

## Required checks

- Route the land to State Revenue Office Tasmania, the Land Tax Act 2000 and the Land Tax Rating Act 2000, and read the taxing date and rates from the State Revenue Office for the assessment year.
- Record the owners of each property at the taxing date from the title searches.
- Record each property's land classification, General Land or Principal Residence Land, with its evidence, and request the home's classification.
- Treat the foreign investor land tax surcharge on the brother's share as a question, and request his foreign status evidence and purchase date.
- Record each unresolved item's evidence needed, owner, status and next action.

## Must not do

- Do not classify the home, decide the brother's foreign status or apply a rate or threshold from memory.
- Do not object, pay, apply for a classification or contact the State Revenue Office.

## Source-verification and reviewer boundary

Use State Revenue Office Tasmania's current land tax guidance and the Tasmanian Acts for the assessment year. Preserve unresolved review flags. An authorised human decides the position and performs consequential actions. This is not tax, legal or financial advice or an assurance engagement.
