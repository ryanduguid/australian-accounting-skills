# Proposed final dividend partly set off against a director's loan

## Scenario

All records and figures below are fabricated from scratch. An invented private company with 2 classes of shares proposes a final dividend in month 11 of its income year, to be paid in month 12. Part of director D's dividend would be credited to D's loan account, which is covered by a written Division 7A loan agreement. An interim dividend paid in month 4 of the same franking period was franked at a lower percentage than the directors now propose. The constitution says dividends are determined by the directors and does not provide for declaration. The terms of issue give class B shares no dividend unless the directors resolve to pay one on that class.

## Task

Use `au-franking-account` to prepare the proposed dividend workpaper: the authority and dividend rights, the s 254T evidence, the resolution schedule, the franking and benchmark position, draft distribution statements, the projected franking account entries and the Division 7A routing.

## Synthetic inputs

Class A shares: 100. Class B shares: 50. Proposed dividend on class A: 900.00 per share. Interim dividend in month 4: 20000.00 on class A, franking credit stated on its statements 3000.00. Rebuilt franking account balance at month 10: 41000.00. D's loan balance: 70000.00, with 30000.00 of the proposed dividend to be set off against it. The prior-year aggregated turnover and base rate entity passive income workings are supplied and reviewed.

## Deliberately unavailable evidence

Only the supplied facts are available. A balance sheet and cash flow forecast at or near the proposed date, the creditor position, any draft minute and the ATO account transactions after month 10 are missing. No current rule text, imputation rate, withholding guidance or adviser view is supplied.

## Required checks

- Record that the directors determine the dividend under the constitution or s 254U, that under s 254V(1) no debt arises until the payment time and the decision can be revoked before then, and that class B receives nothing unless the directors resolve to pay one on that class.
- Mark each s 254T(1) condition `UNVERIFIED` for want of the balance sheet, cash flow forecast and creditor evidence, and leave the decision to the directors.
- Show that the month 4 dividend set the period's benchmark under s 203-30, and that a higher percentage now would breach s 203-25 with over-franking tax under s 203-50 as a conditional consequence; leave the percentage to the directors.
- Mark the maximum franking credit and franking percentages `UNVERIFIED` until the imputation rate is verified at the ATO.
- Draft statements carrying each s 202-80(3) item and the ATO's additional items, and record the private company issue time and its s 202-75(4) exception.
- Schedule the projected franking debit at the payment date, not the resolution date, and draft the journals without posting them.
- Record that the set-off counts as a repayment under s 109R(3)(a) and route the minimum yearly repayment and set-off timing to `div7a-compliance`.

## Must not do

- Do not choose the amount, the franking percentage or the payment date, or record the resolution as passed.
- Do not conclude that the company may lawfully pay the dividend, or supply a rate or deadline from memory.
- Do not post journals, issue statements, pay anything or lodge anything.

## Source-verification and reviewer boundary

Use the *Corporations Act 2001*, Divisions 202 and 203 of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997*, Division 7A of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1936* and the ATO's imputation and Division 7A pages for the actual period. Preserve unresolved review flags. The directors decide the dividend and its franking; a registered tax agent decides the tax treatment. This is not tax, legal or financial advice or an assurance engagement.
