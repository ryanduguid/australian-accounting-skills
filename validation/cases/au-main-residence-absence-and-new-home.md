# A former home let for 8 years whilst a new home is lived in

## Scenario

All records and figures below are fabricated from scratch. An invented individual, an Australian resident throughout, signed a contract for house A on 1 July 2014, settled on 1 August 2014 and moved in that day. On 1 July 2017 the owner moved interstate and house A was let to tenants from that day until it was sold. The owner settled on house B on 1 July 2021, moved in that day and has lived there since. House A was sold under a contract signed on 30 June 2025 that settled on 31 July 2025. The owner has no spouse or dependent child and has made no choice in any earlier return.

## Task

Use `au-main-residence` to prepare the eligibility record, the ownership period, the period-by-period use schedule with the available choices, the first income use record, the apportionment inputs and the unresolved-items hand-off for house A.

## Synthetic inputs

House A: purchase price 500000.00, acquisition costs 20000.00, sale price 900000.00, selling costs 15000.00. Lease and agent statements cover 1 July 2017 to the sale with no vacant period. The only evidence of house A's value on 1 July 2017 is a real estate agent's appraisal of 610000.00. Electoral roll and utility records show house A as home for the period 1 August 2014 to 30 June 2017 and house B as home since 1 July 2021. The owner's accountant notes 'apply the 6-year rule and claim house B too'.

## Deliberately unavailable evidence

Only the supplied facts are available. A market valuation of house A at 1 July 2017, the owner's choice under the absence rule and the owner's address and occupation records for 1 July 2017 to 30 June 2021 are missing. No current rule text, ATO view or adviser decision is supplied.

## Required checks

- Record that the owner is an individual and an Australian resident at the CGT event, that house A came from no deceased estate and that the sale is CGT event A1, with the contract date as the event time.
- Record both the contract and settlement dates at each end of the ownership period, show any day counts on both bases with the counting convention used, and leave the basis to the reviewer.
- Schedule house A's periods without gaps or overlaps: actual main residence, then let for the period 1 July 2017 to the sale.
- Show that an absence choice can cover at most 6 years of the letting (to 30 June 2023), that house B cannot be the main residence for any period the choice covers, and that s 118-140 gives no overlap because house A was not lived in during the 12 months before its interest ended.
- Present at least 2 choice periods (ending on 30 June 2021 and on 30 June 2023) with the house B days each displaces, without choosing between them.
- Record that s 118-192 applies on either period because the first income use was after 20 August 1996 and the dwelling would have been fully exempt just before it, so house A is taken to be acquired on 1 July 2017 for its market value.
- Treat the appraisal as a screen, mark the 1 July 2017 market value, the cost base and the gain `UNVERIFIED`, and leave the apportioned gain blank rather than apportioning the gain on the 2014 cost.
- Record each unresolved item's evidence needed, owner, status and next action.

## Must not do

- Do not make or recommend the absence choice, or treat house A and house B as main residences for the same days.
- Do not use the agent's appraisal as market value, or compute an exempt or assessable amount from the original purchase price.
- Do not state a rule, period or day count from memory, or lodge, declare or communicate anything.

## Source-verification and reviewer boundary

Use Subdivision 118-B of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* and the ATO's main residence pages for the actual income year. Preserve unresolved review flags. The owner makes any choice and the reviewer decides the exemption. This is not tax, legal or financial advice or an assurance engagement.
