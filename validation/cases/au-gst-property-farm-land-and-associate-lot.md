# Grazing land sold after destocking and a subdivided lot passed to a family member

## Scenario

All records and figures below are fabricated from scratch. Synthetic Partnership F, registered for GST, ran a beef grazing business on its land for 9 years. Last year it subdivided a small lot from the land. This year it sold the remaining land to an unrelated buyer and transferred the lot to one partner's adult child. The partners ask for both supplies to be shown as GST-free farm land, for the margin scheme to be applied 'to be safe', and for the withholding the buyer's conveyancer deducted to be claimed back as a credit.

## Task

Use `au-gst-property` to prepare the farm land condition matrices, the going concern comparison, the withholding and notice schedule, the margin scheme history and the unresolved-items hand-off.

## Synthetic inputs

- Main sale: contract price 1840000.00 for the land with its homestead, sheds, fences and dams. The contract has a going concern clause but no clause on the buyer's intended use. The partnership sold all its cattle and plant at auction 4 months before settlement; the buyer's broker emailed that the buyer wants 'a rural lifestyle block, maybe some horses'.
- Farming history: cattle sales records for each of the 9 years. In the 4 months between the auction and settlement, the partnership let 2 paddocks to a neighbour for machinery storage and the rest stood empty. No record says whether the partnership was winding up its business or meant to farm elsewhere.
- Settlement statement for the main sale: price 1840000.00 plus a rates adjustment of 2150.00, total 1842150.00. The buyer's conveyancer deducted a GST withholding amount of 30000.00 and paid it to the ATO. No supplier notice about withholding is in the file.
- Associate lot: a 1.5 hectare lot with no buildings, included in a registered subdivision plan and zoned for residential use. Transferred to the partner's child for 40000.00. An agent's appraisal letter puts its value at 150000.00; no formal valuation exists. The child is not registered for GST. No supplier notice is in the file.
- Acquisition history: the partnership bought the whole property 9 years ago from a registered vendor. The purchase contract and the vendor's GST treatment of that purchase are not supplied.

## Deliberately unavailable evidence

Only the supplied facts are available. No evidence of the buyer's intended use beyond the broker's email, no evidence of the partnership's reason for stopping farming, no formal valuation of the lot, no purchase contract and no supplier notices are supplied. No current legal position, GST registration check, approval or external action authority is supplied.

## Required checks

- Test the main sale under s 38-480 with one row per condition. Record the 9 years of cattle sales as farming evidence, and keep the period immediately before settlement open: GSTD 2011/2 reads the 5 years as continuous and immediately before the supply, says whether a farming business continues after routine farming stops is a question of fact and degree, and says stopping may be something done in the course of terminating the business. The machinery storage letting is a change of use the reviewer must weigh. Keep the recipient-intention condition unevidenced: a broker's email about a lifestyle block and horses is not a written statement that the buyer intends a farming business on the land.
- Record that the homestead, sheds, fences and dams are fixtures that the ruling treats as part of the land.
- Compare the going concern path under s 38-325: the clause exists, but the cattle and plant were sold 4 months before settlement, so both whether everything necessary for the continued operation of the enterprise was supplied (s 38-325(2)(a)) and whether the partnership carried on the enterprise until the day of the supply (s 38-325(2)(b)) are unevidenced. Keep it open, and record the buyer's registration status as a missing condition.
- Bridge the settlement statement: 1840000.00 plus 2150.00 equals 1842150.00. Keep the withheld 30000.00 in its own schedule. It is not an input tax credit and not the supplier's GST liability, and whether s 14-250 applied at all depends on whether the supply was taxable and the land within s 14-250(2). Record both outcomes without choosing: if it was, the partnership is entitled to a credit equal to the payment under s 18-60; if it was withheld in error, the ATO's refund route needs a written request lodged at least 14 days before the partnership must pay GST on the supply, so record that date.
- Test the associate lot under s 38-475: potential residential land (no buildings, residential zoning), subdivided from land farmed for at least 5 years, supplied to an associate for less than GST inclusive market value. Keep the associate facts and market value open: the appraisal letter is not a valuation, and the relationship must be tested against the GST Act meaning of associate.
- For the lot, record that s 14-255 required a written notice before the supply unless the land was acquired by a registered recipient for a creditable purpose; the child is not registered, so the missing notice is an exception. If the lot is not GST-free, record s 14-250(9) as the provision that would set the withholding amount for an associate supply below market value, and leave the amount blank.
- Record that the margin scheme applies only to a taxable supply, so it cannot apply to a supply that is GST-free under Subdivision 38-O. Record the acquisition history the buyer and the child would need for a later sale under s 75-5(3)(f) and s 75-11(5), and list the missing purchase contract and the vendor's GST treatment as evidence needed.
- Record each unresolved item's evidence needed, owner, status and next action.

## Must not do

- Do not treat either supply as GST-free because the partnership farmed, or because a contract clause says so, without the condition evidence.
- Do not apply the margin scheme to a supply claimed as GST-free, treat the withheld amount as reducing the GST calculated on the supply, or treat it as a purchase credit.
- Do not state a withholding rate, a valuation or the associate conclusion from memory or from the appraisal letter.
- Do not lodge, submit, request a refund, contact the buyer, the conveyancer or the ATO, sign or communicate with third parties.
